## Chapter 830: Unwarranted Hostility

Continuing north from Silverwing Hold, they truly entered the heart of Ashenvale.

Once they were deep within this forest, Mount Hyjal and Nordrassil were no longer visible — they couldn't even see the sky.

Though the night elves had banned all arcane magic, the entire forest was suffused with a gentle, luminous arcane energy, soft as moonlight.

The source of this energy was the Well of Eternity atop Mount Hyjal, which nurtured the World Tree. Steeped in this energy, it seemed as though specks of light drifted between the trunks and beneath the canopy — though if you didn't look closely, you couldn't quite make them out.

This way, even with the sky completely blotted out, lifting your head in Ashenvale at night felt like gazing up at a true starry sky. The nightsabers the night elves bred had bones that inevitably swayed left and right with each stride. Riding on their backs felt like being tossed back and forth between two sturdy, muscular frames.

Jace held Vereesa on the back of a blue-spotted nightsaber. The elf's long ears trembled slightly in front of his eyes with the rhythm of the cat's gait, occasionally brushing against Jace's slightly overgrown stubble, which made her shake her head in discomfort.

Grede sat on a black nightsaber. Aside from sleeping and eating, he'd spent the whole journey scanning the surrounding forest — every nook and cranny.

Jace knew he was still holding out hope of finding Mosa Woodmane, imagining she might be watching from some corner, unwilling to show herself for whatever reason — dislike of the night elves, or something else.

The dwarf appeared to be sitting alone on the other side with the luggage, but in fact there was someone sitting behind him — Morophil.

That black nightsaber had long since been dominated by Morophil's charm magic. Now it was docile as a pet, completely ignoring the weight of the invisible succubus on its back.

The only problem was that occasionally, when the night elves called the nightsaber to do something, its reactions were much slower than usual. Once it even aroused the sentinels' suspicion, though in the end they chalked it up to the dwarf and his luggage and armor being simply too wearisome for the beast.

Following the main road and the star-lit lanterns toward Felwood, along the way they could see the Kaldorei watchtowers that had stood for thousands of years, and wooden pavilions and gazebos that had nearly merged with the forest.

After two days' travel, at a fork in the road Jace spotted a signpost pointing west, inscribed with elven runes he could now more or less piece together: "Astranaar."

At this time, the World Tree Teldrassil beyond Darkshore had not yet been planted, and the night elf capital of Darnassus from the game was out of the question. Astranaar should therefore be the Kaldorei capital — where Tyrande Whisperwind resided. It was probably a bit larger than that half-a-street village from the game.

If they weren't in such a hurry, Jace would actually have been quite looking forward to taking a look.

Passing this junction and continuing north, in less than two more days the terrain rose visibly.

Jace could feel it. Though the trees still blocked out the sky, they were in fact already climbing Mount Hyjal. Felwood sat at a very high elevation — it was, in effect, the foot of Mount Hyjal.

From here on, the large and small night elf temples along the roadside began appearing more frequently. From time to time, drifting melodies and the strains of unidentifiable instruments could be heard from the forest. Occasionally, groups of plainly dressed night elf civilians appeared, walking unhurriedly to the north — like pilgrims.

As the nightsabers climbed higher and pushed deeper into northern Ashenvale, Jace came to understand more and more why the night elves held Mount Hyjal sacred.

So different from the Felwood of the game, steeped in darkness and corruption from fel energy — the foot of Mount Hyjal here was practically a fairyland.

As the elevation rose, the deep shadows of Ashenvale slowly dispersed. Sunlight spilled between the layered canopies, casting dappled shadows that made one feel clear and comfortable from head to toe.

The creatures of the forest — from deer to birds — showed no fear of passing travelers. Some even leaped gracefully in front of the sentinels, unhurried, as if they didn't see them at all.

For the next week of travel, watching the roadside temples and shrines, ancient Highborne ruins, and sentinels' watchtowers, Jace felt none of the weariness of a long journey. Each day he felt more clear-headed.

And at night, far from being kept awake by the abundant magical energy, he slept deeply and soundly. Every morning he woke feeling reborn.

During this time, Vereesa had grown quieter and quieter along the way. Compared to the rather excited Jace and Grede, she seemed somewhat down beneath her curiosity.

Even if she didn't say it, Jace could guess why.

As a high elf, she must have keenly felt the power of the Well of Eternity and the World Tree.

Though the Well of Eternity here was two vials of water left over from the Well of Eternity destroyed in the War of the Ancients, it contained power even more profound than the Sunwell.

And though the Well of Eternity, like the Sunwell, nourished all of northern Kalimdor, it lacked the Sunwell's brilliance — or rather, its aggressiveness.

Looking up from here, Jace could not see a radiance blazing like the sun, nor any dazzling magical light that seemed bent on dominating everything. The power of the Well of Eternity had simply hidden itself in every blade of grass and leaf of every tree, in every inch of soil, flowing in the veins of every Kaldorei. The vitality of this place was testament enough to its greatness.

All of this filled outsiders like Jace and Grede with wonder and admiration. But for Vereesa, the emotions it stirred were far more complicated.

Passing the mountain road leading to the sacred peak of Mount Hyjal, the nightsaber company pressed on. But at the junction, Jace glanced up.

The canopy of Nordrassil, the World Tree, bloomed like a mass of green cloud above the sky, blotting out half the heavens. Gazing up at it even gave one an intensely oppressive feeling. Yet the sacred life-energy it radiated kept one from recoiling with anything like megalophobia. Beneath a clean sky, the ancient verdant trees stood like sentinels, guarding the mountain road from any evil's disturbance.

The unease Morophil felt had been building from the foot of Mount Hyjal to here, reaching its peak. Even the nightsaber in front seemed to sense something amiss, looking back from time to time.

She remained invisible, but Jace could feel her uncontainable fear. All Jace could do was carefully convey reassurance through the Soul Link.

After all, ever since the Burning Legion was driven back in the War of the Ancients, Mount Hyjal had been sacred ground that permitted no evil to defile it. This was precisely why Dasmora had written those words in the scroll, viewing this mountain as a thing of terror.

Leaving the road to the sacred peak, Morophil's unease settled. Her invisibility grew stronger, and the surrounding forest and nightsabers became less vigilant.

By now Jace had confirmed his guess: Darkwhisper Gorge. It had to be.

Even with the sentinels' nightsabers, skirting around the foot of Mount Hyjal had taken over a week. After leaving the road to the sacred peak, the temples and travelers to the north of Mount Hyjal grew fewer and fewer. The road became ever older and more weathered. The surrounding forest took on an increasingly primeval character.

Occasionally, fallen ancient trees lay across the road, their decrepit, elder-like faces and thick, limb-like branches jutting high.

That day, at dusk, the forest was bathed gold by the sunlight. Jace narrowed his eyes and looked up at the scattered, broken light — when he felt the blue nightsaber beneath him, which had been walking steadily, suddenly tense.

The two lead sentinels looked around sharply. Grede, who'd been dozing, snapped his head up. Vereesa eyed the right side of the road warily.

"What is it?" Jace asked.

"Someone's there," Vereesa replied in a low voice, glancing again to the right. "On both sides."

The sentinels drew their blades and shouted ancient elven passwords — as if to intimidate the enemy, or to issue orders. Grede asked, utterly bewildered, "How could there be enemies ambushing us here? Isn't this your sacred ground..."

A thick spear came flying from somewhere unknown and lodged itself in the stone seam of the road right before the lead sentinel rider, letting out a loud screech. Vereesa immediately unslung the bow from the nightsaber's side and nocked two arrows, aiming at the source of the spear in the trees. Then a black shadow suddenly burst from the left side of the road, roaring as it pounced on one of the sentinels, overturning both rider and cat to the ground. At the same time, two more shadows emerged from the front right. One let out a savage roar that spooked the nightsaber another sentinel was riding into a panicked retreat of several steps, completely throwing the formation into chaos.

By now, shadow after shadow was emerging from the bushes, leveling spears and bows at the people on the road.

They all had faces like wild bears, yet stood upright on bodies far leaner than a bear's. Their arms were long and powerful, as if built for hurling weapons.

These creatures all wore necklaces strung with bones, teeth, and animal skulls. On their heads they wore feathers of various colors, plucked from God-knew-what birds, tied to either side of their heads like rabbit ears — which looked rather comical.

These were furbolgs. Though they differed somewhat from their game counterparts — smaller heads, smaller eyes, slightly taller — given the location and their attire, Jace was certain.

But why were they so angry?

The Burning Legion hadn't arrived yet. Fel energy hadn't touched Felwood. They should be nothing more than peaceful inhabitants living around Mount Hyjal. And from Su'ura Swiftarrow's tone, communication between the night elves and the furbolgs was easy enough. Why were they drawing weapons without so much as a question?

Just then, the sentinel still on her nightsaber shouted loudly in Darnassian: "Hold, people of the Timbermaw! These are Silverwing sentinels. We have had no quarrel with the Timbermaw for many years. There must be some misunderstanding here."

The nightsaber that had been knocked down rose angrily, shielding its rider, but did not immediately attack the furbolg that had toppled it. Such restraint clearly showed an intent to avoid conflict.

The furbolg who'd pounced backed up a few steps, raised the woodcutter's axe in his hand, and said in Darnassian, "Outsiders stay. We will not harm the Kaldorei."

"These outsiders are honored guests!" the sentinel said angrily, speaking at length with the furbolg in Darnassian. But at Jace's level of elven comprehension, he couldn't follow anymore.

The furbolg responded angrily in even more impenetrable elven. The sentinel's words seemed to have no effect. "What are they saying?" Jace couldn't help asking Vereesa beside him.

"The sentinel is telling these Timbermaw about how we defeated Deathwing," Vereesa said. "But these furbolgs don't seem to believe it at all. They think we're all liars."

"Outsiders are all cunning criminals! Murderers!" the tall black furbolg on the road roared. "Must capture all outsiders! Kill all outsiders! Outsiders are full of lies! Outsiders have no respect!"

The surrounding furbolgs roared along with their chief's words. Jace realized at once that it was likely some other visitor from across the sea who had provoked these furbolgs.

He noticed the black furbolg's gaze on Vereesa was particularly hateful. Could it be those high elf mages who'd come to Winterspring seeking treasure, who'd slaughtered the furbolgs trying to stop them in order to enter Winterspring?

"To hell with it, we'll fight," Grede said, raising his stone axe. "I refuse to believe Grede Thunderfist will die here for no damn reason."

Jace's peripheral vision tracked Morophil. By now she'd already leaped off the nightsaber's back and, at an elusive speed, slipped among a group of bow-wielding furbolgs, waiting for her moment.

He gripped the sword hilt at his waist. "I guarantee you won't die, Grede. But there are too many of them."

Vereesa assessed the situation and said quietly, "Yes, if they all charge at once, we might not be able to handle it. I'm going to try the earring. Sentinel, call your companion back. Get closer to me. My ornament can create a zone of concealment — it'll help us slip out of this furbolg ambush."

The sentinel looked back, saw the earring Vereesa had drawn from her silver hair, and her eyes widened in surprise. Then she immediately moved her lips, signaling with a quiet code for the companion who'd been knocked down to hurry over.

Noticing the sentinel's movements, the furbolg chief seemed to sense something amiss. He took several steps forward, muttering "What are you planning" — but when he saw the silver light in Vereesa's palm, he froze.

"Ysera..." he shouted. "An emissary of the Goddess!"

The furbolg chief threw down his weapon and lowered his head. The furbolgs in the bushes all raised their spear points and crowded over curiously, wanting to see what Vereesa held in her hand.

Every furbolg who saw clearly immediately lowered their weapon and bowed their head, muttering in a strange tongue — but all of it laced with the name "Ysera."

Vereesa looked around in bewilderment at the furbolgs' reaction, forgetting to activate the earring. Jace let out a breath. "Well, not the effect we were expecting..."

"But it worked all the same," Grede murmured.
