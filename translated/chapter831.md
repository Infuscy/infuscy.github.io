## Chapter 831: The Timbermaw Homestead

Learning that Wrensa and her companions wanted to travel to Winterspring, the furbolgs gladly invited the forest goddess's emissary to their dwellings. Seeing that matters had been resolved peacefully, the sentinels took their leave of Jace's group one after another, mounting their nightsabers and heading back to Ashenvale.

The black-furred furbolg chief ambled along slowly up ahead, muttering in Thalassian with an obviously displeased tone. "Many years since so many outsiders invaded. Kaldorei didn't stop them. Our people live here, live with owls, live with deer and wolves — but not outsiders. Not pink-skinned Kaldorei, skinny Kaldorei, even shorter fat Kaldorei, green-skinned even-shorter skinny Kaldorei."

Though the furbolg chief's Thalassian came out like a madman's mumbling, to Jace it was perfectly plain and simple, with no complicated oddities.

"That's not what Kaldorei means," Wrensa tried to correct him.

"Huh?" The furbolg turned his head. "The goddess's emissary has a new view. Kruton is willing to listen!"

Hearing the furbolg say this, Wrensa thought it over and seemed to decide that launching into a lesson on racial distinctions would be too troublesome. In the end, she helplessly waved a hand. "Call it whatever you like."

After bidding farewell to the sentinels, the furbolgs led Jace's group deeper into the forest. As the ambush dispersed, more and more furbolgs emerged from all around, until there were twenty or thirty of them in all.

Given this fellow's size, if it had actually come to a fight, it would have been very difficult to walk away unscathed — and the supplies they'd gotten from the Silverwing stronghold might well have been wasted right here.

The furbolgs around them kept stealing glances at Jace and his companions — at Wrensa, of course, and at the earring she carried — while whispering to one another on the sidelines.

Jace could tell that what they were saying to one another was definitely not Thalassian, nor Common, nor any other language he'd learned. It was a unique, unfamiliar tongue that suited the furbolgs' own vocal habits — spoken naturally and rapidly, rather than with the labored enunciation of someone speaking Thalassian, every word forced out with effort.

It wasn't hard to guess: these furbolgs had learned Thalassian in order to communicate with the elves of the forest. That alone was enough to show they were a civilized race willing to engage in diplomacy, not a pack of savage monsters who killed on sight.

Just now he'd been considering using shadow magic to torture the chief into submission first and intimidate everyone else who might rush over. Looking at it this way, it was a good thing he hadn't acted right away — because if he'd offended these proud yet sensitive furbolgs, the only way through Timbermaw territory would have been to cut his way through.

This furbolg named Kruton led Jace's group through layers of dense forest, walking all the way until darkness fell, when they arrived at a small pond. Everywhere one looked, enormous sections of felled, hollowed-out logs lay about.

The logs were riddled with holes of varying sizes. Through some of those holes one could see flickers of light, shadows sweeping past. Some of the logs were draped with animal hides, others hung with an assortment of weapons — wooden ones, metal ones.

Was this the furbolgs' home? How interesting. Jace couldn't say why, but these great hollowed-out logs gave him a sudden, powerful urge to crawl inside and spend the night. He could see that Grede was fascinated too — the dwarf couldn't resist walking up to one of the logs, running his hand along it, and peering inside, until an irritated snort came from within warning him to back off. But the other furbolgs didn't seem bothered in the slightest.

The whole furbolg camp looked quite casual, with no apparent discipline or rules, yet every member of the tribe got along remarkably well. They greeted each other with a look when they made eye contact; familiar ones even embraced, with no aversion to physical contact, freely sharing the food in their hands — nuts, grape-like fruits, and so on.

The furbolgs seemed to adore all kinds of fruit. Jace could see specially made wooden barrels lying on their sides around the log dwellings. When he got close, the rich aroma of fermentation hit him — were these wine barrels?

Jace gazed at the scene, increasingly unable to reconcile these endearing furbolgs before him with the warriors and hunters who'd ambushed them, pointing a motley assortment of weapons at them, just a short while ago.

They'd truly been provoked by the growing number of intruders coming here with malice.

"I want to try their brew," Grede said, shaking the bizarre-shaped seeds off his cloak — picked up while pushing through the underbrush. "When I get back to Aerie Peak, I can tell the others I drank bear-brewed ale. You know, if I told them I killed a black dragon or something, those old gits who've spent their whole lives stuck in the Hinterlands wouldn't have the faintest idea what I was talking about — black dragon, white dragon, what's the difference to them? But if I told them I drank bear-brewed ale…"

The dwarf nodded, gave a thumbs-up, smacked his lips, and said, "Just thinking about it's impressive, you know?"

"Just thinking about how to brag when you get back," Wrensa muttered.

"What else am I supposed to do?" Grede said, looking ahead at the elf. "I haven't found Mosa, and I can't even tell how much money we're going to make. We can't go back empty-handed, can we? If I come back with one more thing to brag about, that still counts as the trip not being a waste."

So saying, he sidled up to one of the wooden barrels and gave it a careful sniff, then let out a breath and raised his eyebrows in an expression of pure enjoyment.

Jace looked at him and said, "If we get back from Winterspring and still haven't found any news about Mosa, then we'll go to Astranaar and ask. What do you say, Grede? I figure the leader of the Kaldorei — that high priestess — is bound to know things these sentinels don't. She might even be able to get in touch with the Keepers of the Grove and find out where Mosa actually is."

"Hard to imagine it going that smoothly," Grede said, scratching his ear as he walked back. "Don't dwell on it too much, Jace. If Mosa Woodmane can't take care of herself, then how's she supposed to be the tauren's savior?"

"That's a fair point," Jace said quietly.

Only after entering the furbolg settlement did Jace realize that not every furbolg wore feathers or strings of beads on their heads — some of the guards carrying weapons didn't either. So only a very few furbolgs wore such ornaments, which seemed to be a symbol of status as well.

And the furbolgs who'd ambushed them in the jungle earlier — most of them had been wearing such accessories. So had this furbolg chief brought a force of elite furbolgs to intercept them outsiders?

"I'd like to ask a question, Kruton," Jace said, trying out his Thalassian. "About outsiders."

The black furbolg Kruton glanced back at Jace. Though the expressions this furbolg could make were quite limited, Jace could still read the extreme displeasure on his face.

"You bear ill will toward the bear god's children. I, Kruton, only speak to the goddess's emissary who slew the great black dragon."

Wrensa was just about to explain when Jace immediately cut her off. "Don't tell them so much. It's already a miracle they're willing to talk to you."

"Fine. Then tell me what you want to know," Wrensa said.

Jace said, "Did you notice the way those furbolgs looked at you before you brought out Ysera's gift? They seem to have a particular hostility toward the Quel'dorei. So I want to know — who exactly came through here and provoked them?"

"That's what I'd like to know too," Wrensa replied. She looked ahead at the furbolg and asked in Thalassian, "Your tribe seems extremely wary of outsiders. We'd like to know what kind of outsiders made you so cautious."

"Many outsiders. Many outsiders drawing near these days. The Kaldorei sentinels can't stop them! They kill our people, take our beads, feathers, and tools, and sell them to your kind. I know this all too well. Kruton is clever. I know your trade, know your money and your ships!"

The furbolg pointed at Jace's forehead, getting more and more agitated, until finally he couldn't get any words out at all — just a low, rumbling snort, snort, breathing heavily.

Jace kept stealing glances at the axe the fellow was carrying, terrified that in a fit of rage the furbolg would take a swing and lop his head off.

This creature, while nowhere near as tall as an ogre, still stood close to two meters. But his thick arms, his muscles, his top-heavy body structure that seemed purpose-built for the savage swinging of weapons — all of it left Jace with little confidence he could go toe-to-toe with the brute.

"Sounds like a pack of poachers or mercenaries," Wrensa said, glancing at Jace. "But it's understandable. After all, they've lived alongside the Kaldorei here in peace for so long. Suddenly a bunch of intruders show up killing on sight — it must feel just like the orcs pouring out of the Dark Portal."

By this point, Grede had already sat down next to a pile of fruit and was gesticulating furiously in an incomprehensible conversation with several furbolgs. One of them had even brought him a bowl of some beverage — milk, soup, or some kind of ale, hard to say — and Grede had excitedly drunk it down.

The dwarf, of course, understood not a word of Thalassian, let alone the furbolg tongue. And those furbolgs weren't wearing beads or feathers, so they most likely didn't understand Thalassian either. And yet somehow they were chatting away with wild gestures, looking for all the world as though they understood each other perfectly, sitting together eating and drinking.

"We're not with those people," Jace told the furbolg. "We came here looking for a friend."

"We believe you," the furbolg said, pointing at Wrensa, then at Jace beside her. "But we don't believe him."

Jace looked at Wrensa with a trace of impatience. "Then ask him — among the people killing furbolgs, was there a Quel'dorei mage?"

"What, you suspect Dar'Khan has already made it here?" Wrensa asked.

"It's worth suspecting," Jace said.

Wrensa said, "He has fewer leads than we do, Jace. And when we left, he should still have been in Karazhan. Think about all the trouble we ran into along the way — how much better off could he possibly be?"

"He knows teleportation magic, Wen," Jace reminded her.

Wrensa said, "I don't think those conservative fellows in the Magisters would be willing to teleport to a completely unfamiliar location." Jace asked, "Has Dar'Khan ever taken part in the Reliquary's missions to northern Kalimdor before?"

Wrensa shook her head. "I don't know, but it's certainly possible. But if he could use arcane teleportation, he wouldn't need to fight with the furbolgs here, would he? He could just teleport straight into Winterspring."

Jace nodded. Wrensa had a point. A mage like Dar'Khan had no reason to pick a fight with the furbolgs by force.

Let alone a member of the Magisters — he'd have an easier time communicating with the furbolgs than Wrensa did. Even if he really needed to pass through here, he'd certainly have a more harmonious way of going about it.

The furbolgs' peculiarly hostile looks really were a matter of subjective judgment.

Perhaps it was because of what Amy had said before they left — "a mage from Quel'Thalas is more likely to teleport to Kalimdor" — that had stuck with him all this way. Combined with the Timbermaw furbolgs' hostility toward outsiders, something even the sentinels hadn't anticipated… all of it stacked together, making him hypersensitive.

"I don't understand your words, but you seem to be having a disagreement," the furbolg Kruton said, pointing at Jace in Thalassian peppered with a string of strange words. "If he… bullies the goddess's emissary, then I… Kruton will break his skull and smash his head! He's just… a fragile little Kaldorei."

Jace thought he'd misheard and instinctively took a step back. Wrensa immediately said, "We're just discussing something, Kruton."

"I'm sorry, but I can't help you there," the furbolg said. "I can take you to see the elder. The elder knows everything. He reveres Ysera, the forest goddess — because the forest goddess loves the great brothers, and loves their children, and loves us. We give thanks to the forest goddess."

"Wonderful," Wrensa said. "Please take us to see the elder."

Jace guessed that the "great brothers" they mentioned were Ursol and Ursoc — the bear gods that most furbolgs worshipped.

Ursol and Ursoc had attained immortal bodies and souls long before the Kaldorei ever walked Kalimdor, joining the ranks of the wild gods.

During the War of the Ancient, when the Burning Legion invaded, they stood forth and joined the ancient furbolgs on the front lines alongside the night elves and tauren against the demons. But in the end, countless mightiest furbolgs were slain by demons, and the great bear brothers themselves fell beneath the tidal wave of endless doomguards and felhounds.

Jace knew that these furbolgs must have heard this legend. If he unleashed demon magic here, or if Molophil's presence were discovered, then there would be nothing left but a fight to the death.

The furbolg beckoned to Wrensa, then headed down a path deep into the camp lined with torches. Wrensa followed. Jace glanced back at Grede, who was still chatting with the furbolgs, and said, "Are you coming to see their elder?"

"I'll pass. I can't understand a word they're saying anyway," Grede said, tossing a cashew-like nut into his mouth and chewing.

Jace tossed his demon bag to him. "Then hold onto this for me, Grede."

Grede caught the bag. He knew it contained Sazeno and a pile of scrolls and manuals inscribed with demonic incantations, so he glanced in the direction Wrensa had gone and nodded. "I know. Go on, don't worry."
