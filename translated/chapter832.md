## Chapter 832: A Suspicious Traveler

As they approached the furbolg elder's dwelling, the furbolgs who noticed their movements all turned to look over, seeming somewhat concerned that their elder was about to face outsiders.

By now, the reek of wild beasts and the smell of fermenting grain and fruit were fading. Jace began to catch a peculiar incense-like fragrance, seemingly rising from the torches planted on either side — either the fuel or the wood itself.

Jace didn't know much about this place, but he found that this incense set one's mind at ease.

Kruton murmured something in the furbolg tongue to a brown-furred furbolg standing at the entrance, clutching a wooden staff and draped head to toe in pendants, wearing a wooden branch crown. This brown furbolg looked Vereesa over, bowed in a somewhat comical fashion, and stepped aside to clear a path.

"The goddess's emissary may enter, but you may not," Kruton said, raising an arm to point at Jace.

Jace was just about to try talking his way in when a hoarse sound suddenly emanated from within the rotting-wood hut up ahead, surrounded by torches.

Hearing the sound, Kruton could only let out a low growl and wave a hand. "The elder says you may enter. The elder says he knew you were coming."

Jace glanced inside in surprise. What kind of furbolg elder was this — a prophet? How did he know he was coming?

Considering that after the Burning Legion invaded Mount Hyjal and fel energy corrupted all of Felwood, the Timbermaw furbolgs had still managed to maintain enough sanity to resist both their maddened kin and the demons who sought to harm them — it only made sense that a tribe capable of surviving like that, and becoming the largest of all furbolg tribes, had its reasons.

Passing through the torches arranged in what appeared to be a specific pattern and ducking into the cramped rotting-wood hut, Jace had braced himself for an unpleasant smell, but what greeted him instead was a not-unpleasant scent of bristles — like the smell of a blanket that had been washed clean and left in a cupboard for a very long time, without a trace of animal musk or sweat.

An aged, gray-white furbolg lay reclining in the deepest part of the hut, clutching a long-handled wooden mallet in his arms.

The mallet was hung with all manner of ornaments — metal, bone, and carved wood — making it look like an elaborate druidic staff. And yet it still had a hammer head that could easily crush a human skull.

Seeing Jace and Vereesa walk in, he spoke in Elvish with remarkably good pronunciation: "I knew of your coming. I could feel it. The scorching air, the flames — they all told me. They said the warrior who flows with scorching blood and has been blessed by the Life-Binder draws near. I expect that would be you, hairless one."

So he wasn't some kind of prophet, but a shaman — someone who could commune with the elements as well.

It probably wasn't strange for furbolgs to have shamans, but that the elements could forewarn him of Jace's arrival was something that genuinely surprised him.

Jace thought this over and rubbed his stubble, feeling somewhat odd at being called "hairless one." But he managed to suppress the urge to pick a pointless argument on the spot.

"I can feel the flames' warmth toward you," the furbolg elder said. "They are willing to trust you, and so I can trust you. This hut welcomes you — and of course, the emissary of the forest goddess."

Vereesa glanced at Jace with a smile, and Jace felt a wave of relief.

"The Kaldorei told us that to enter the snowy mountains, we must pass through Timbermaw territory, elder," she said. "So we'd like to ask for passage. We hope that's not an offense?"

The elder spoke slowly: "We have never intended to bar travelers who bear no malice. It's only that many people, out of fear born of differences in appearance and custom, shrink from contact with us and even try to force their way through with violence. As long as you don't destroy anything or harm any of our people while passing through our hold, it can't be called an offense."

"But the mountains we know have grown increasingly unsettled. Spirits refuse to depart. Dormant demons whisper in the dark. More and more of our people are lost among the corrupted forests and canyons. More and more invaders who would disrupt this harmony have begun to show themselves."

"The young ones have good reason to be worried, you two."

The furbolg elder's words came out of his mouth like recited poetry, making it hard for Jace to connect what he was expressing with his crude, bestial appearance.

At the same time, his words caught Jace's attention — spirits, demons, lost among corrupted forests and canyons — and he couldn't help but link these things to the report Dasmora had written to Kallrez: the labyrinth, the green giant wall, the moaning of spirits…

"Is it the same in the snowy mountains over there?" Jace asked.

"Yes," the furbolg elder said. "The foot of the sacred mountain has already been shadowed. I can feel it. The sky, the flames, and the wind can feel it too. They bring me their worry and their unease. I believe you can hear it as well."

He gazed at Jace. Jace nodded.

Although so far he hadn't been able to receive anything too detailed from the flames, after the elder's words he did come to understand some of the elements' feelings that he hadn't noticed before.

"Is there anything else I can help with?" the elder said in a low voice.

Vereesa let out a slow breath, glanced toward the entrance, and said, "We'd like to know something about travelers who came here before us — especially those unwelcome guests who sought to disturb the peace here. It might be useful for our own objectives going forward."

The furbolg elder said: "Those evil brigands and murderers rarely attempt to pass through here to reach the snowy mountains. They hunt us around Hyjal and take whatever they can find, without trying to communicate with us in any way… But not long ago, an outsider like you, accompanied by a beast companion, came to our hold wanting to reach the snowy mountains. She bore goodwill, so we let her pass."

"She? Accompanied by a beast companion? Like me?" Vereesa said, pointing to herself, then looked at Jace. "Could it be a Quel'dorei Farstrider who traveled here with a beast?"

"Are there any other Farstriders who came here besides you?" Jace asked.

"Now that you mention it… I'm not sure either," Vereesa said.

"What did she look like?" Jace asked.

"Golden hair, green cloak," the elder said.

"What about the beast?" Jace asked further.

"A leopard. A beautiful leopard," the elder said.

Jace blinked, then immediately asked: "Did that leopard have horns?"

"No," the elder shook his head.

"You think that might be Mosa in disguise?" Vereesa whispered in Common.

"It doesn't look like it, now," Jace said with a hint of disappointment in his sigh.
