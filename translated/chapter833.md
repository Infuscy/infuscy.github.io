## Chapter 833: The Anomaly in Winterspring

In the old tree-trunk hut, the warlock and the ranger exchanged a complicated look.

If Mosa hadn't gone to Winterspring, and hadn't been in Ashenvale, then where on earth had she gone? When he'd first heard about a leopard, Jace had really thought they were finally about to find Mosa.

"That green-cloaked Quel'dorei," Wrensa paused, then went on. "Did she have any other distinguishing features? For instance, what kind of bow she carried, or what other weapons — a sword, a blade, that sort of thing."

"Forgive me, emissary of Ysera. When that traveler came to Timbermaw Hold, I only caught a distant glimpse of her. My impression of her is very hazy — extremely hazy. I suspect it's because I've lived to an age my kind isn't meant to reach, and the day of my departure isn't far off. Perhaps if you ask the young ones who were guarding the hold at the time, you might get a more detailed answer."

Having spoken these words slowly, the aged furbolg elder took a breath and leaned against the wooden wall behind him.

Jace had been wanting to ask him something about alchemy or potion recipes, but seeing how exhausted the old fellow looked, the words died in his throat.

He glanced around the little wooden hut that could be taken in at a single glance. There was no alchemy equipment here, no herbs laid out — only a few wooden bowls with traces of medicinal broth still clinging to them.

A tribe this size, with an elder like this — he couldn't possibly be in charge of everything. There ought to be physicians in the hold who were skilled at gathering herbs and brewing medicines.

Walking back outside, two furbolgs holding long staffs were standing there watching them, probably ready to rush in and help at the first sign of trouble inside. Even as Ysera's emissary, Wrensa couldn't receive absolute trust here.

"Green cloak, golden hair — with just those two clues, who can you think of?" Jace asked.

"How should I know? I've got a greener cloak than this one too, Jace," Wrensa said with a slight sigh. "Golden hair — most Quel'dorei have golden hair, just in different shades. My own hair's only so light it doesn't look gold. The other colors are what's rare. If he'd seen a red-haired or blue-haired ranger, I could've given you a few names."

Wrensa tucked a stray lock of hair behind her ear, leaned against a nearby tree trunk, and watched the young furbolgs playing by the pond in the distance. "Honestly, I don't even care who she is. A Farstrider turning up here might have some other mission — that's not strange. What worries me is Mosa. I thought that old furbolg would have met her. She's not in Ashenvale, not on Mount Hyjal… and she didn't pass through here. Where on earth is she?"

"The furbolgs here guard a secret hermitage," Jace said quietly. "There's a place at the foot of Mount Hyjal where night elves lived even before the War of the Ancients — and it's also where the Lord of the Grove, Cenarius himself, and his children took refuge. It was from there that Zaetar left for Maraudon."

"Cenarius's hermitage," Wrensa said. "I always thought Cenarius lived in Ashenvale."

Jace said, "If you call all these great forests at the foot of Mount Hyjal 'Ashenvale,' including this place, then I suppose you could call that place Ashenvale too. The night elves themselves aren't all that strict with these place names. And besides, that tauren who called himself Baine told us that Mosa came here seeking allies against the centaurs. We considered earlier that the ones most likely to support Mosa would be the Keepers of the Grove, didn't we?"

Wrensa shot him a glance. "I hope it's as simple as you think."

"You're mocking me, aren't you," Jace said, shifting to lean against a tree root at an angle, watching the dwarf dozing in the distance beside a tree house. "Have you ever thought about one thing, Wen — why would that mysterious traveler want to go to Winterspring but not take the goblin airship?"

Wrensa rolled her eyes at him, her tone one of utter course. "Could it be because we destroyed the goblin airship?"

"Leaving aside that she might have reached Winterspring before we crashed… Don't forget, Gadgetzan runs two airship flights to Everlook every week," Jace said, holding up two fingers. "You forget? The whole journey takes about a week. If they're going to maintain that frequency, they'd need three or four airships operating at the same time — the *Sharkjaw* was just one of them. It took us nearly a month to walk here from the swamp crash. Even if Gadgetzan only had one airship left, it could've made the round trip twice by now."

Wrensa blinked. "All right, I really didn't give it much thought. You're right."

Jace nodded. "If she was on a Reliquary mission, there shouldn't be any issue of bad relations with the goblins. I get the feeling you and the Trade Cartel get along pretty well."

"We do," Wrensa said. "So what do you think? She's not a Farstrider, or… she's not actually Quel'dorei?"

"But I don't think the Kaldorei here could possibly grow golden hair," Jace said, scratching his head. "Could it be related to Dar'Khan, one of his mage companions or something? But then she had a beast companion too."

Wrensa thought for a moment. "A mage with a beast companion is nothing unusual. A lot of mages in Quel'Thalas can talk to animals just like I can. Maybe she was worried about getting lost on Mount Hyjal and found a big cat to help guide the way."

Jace rested his chin on his hand, drumming his fingers on his knee as he thought, when suddenly a completely different possibility occurred to him.

Quite a few green dragons enjoyed the company of night elves and took the form of night elves — such as Eranikus, one of Ysera's consorts. But a small number of green dragons preferred to take the form of high elves, like Itharius, who frequently served as a messenger between the green dragons and mortals or other dragonflights, or Valithria, who was accidentally captured and imprisoned within Icecrown Citadel during the later Alliance and Horde assault on the Lich King's fortress.

"A green dragon?"

"If it's a green dragon, why not just fly over?" Wrensa asked.

She touched her earring and said, "Besides — I'm carrying a token of Queen Ysera herself, and the furbolgs here practically worship me for it. Would a real green dragon who came here need to disguise herself as a Quel'dorei?"

Jace nodded, the drumming of his fingers on his knee growing faster.

In the middle of the night, the two of them sat beside a tree house sorting their luggage. Grede was snoring away beside them. Wrensa was tidying her pack while muttering under her breath: "How can this guy sleep so soundly? It's a real headache. I think he's just being lazy and pretending to avoid work…"

Jace glanced at her, about to say something, when he caught a faint medicinal smell.

He looked around and spotted, some distance away behind a cluster of trees, an enormous tree trunk with wisps of smoke rising from beside it — as if someone were cooking.

But by now the furbolgs were basically all asleep. Only a few furbolgs were out on patrol. Why would anyone still be cooking? They must be brewing medicine.

"Come over here with me, Wen," Jace said, standing up.

The elf looked up. "What are you doing?"

"My alchemist friend asked me to find out what alchemy recipes the natives of Kalimdor have," Jace said. "These furbolgs aren't willing to trust me. Without your help, they'd probably just ignore me."

"Hmph." Wrensa stood up. "Down south, I helped your alchemist friend risk her life going into an insect nest to gather samples. Now here, you want me to go into a furbolg settlement looking for mysterious alchemy recipes. I'd say you and that alchemist friend of yours have a very unusual relationship."

Jace said, a little embarrassed, "She's helped me out a lot — an enormous amount. If not for her, I probably wouldn't have lived to this day."

Wrensa patted the pouch of Ursula's potions hanging from Jace's waist. "The potions you brought before — were they all made with her help? That incredibly powerful fire resistance potion, the invisibility potion, the rare elixirs — all things that cost a fortune. And yet you drink them like they're free. So there's only one explanation: they actually are free."

"Not all of them are free," Jace said, adjusting his belt. "But she has given me a lot."

Wrensa straightened up and smoothed the wrinkles in her shirt. "Then let's go. We'll take a look over there and help you ask the furbolgs." Jace watched her walk ahead and got the feeling she had more she wanted to say.

"That's it? No more questions?"

"What?" Wrensa turned back, then caught Jace's meaning and smiled. "I am Wrensa Windrunner. I have absolute confidence in myself."

Jace threw on his cloak and followed her. "You can't find any confidence from me?"

Wrensa shot him a look. "A man who consorts with demons all day — the furbolgs don't even trust you."

"Hey… keep your voice down."

"They can't understand Common anyway. Besides, sneaking around like that makes you look even more like an evil warlock."

"Warlock's fine — can you stop adding 'evil' in front of it every time?"

"Hmph."

The two of them bickered their way forward. The closer they got to the place where the smoke was rising, the more distinct the smell of medicinal herbs became. Jace had spent enough time around Ursula that he could even detect the scent of Wild Steelbloom in the mix.

Arriving at the entrance, Wrensa asked in Thalassian, "May we come in?"

A low growl came from within. Though it was a bear's growl, it carried no hostility.

The two exchanged a glance and tried ducking into the tree hole. Inside, they found a small, brown-furred furbolg squatting and watching a large pot, clutching several bundles of unidentified herbs in his hands.

In the game, the most famous potion brewed by furbolgs was Winterspring Firewater — a drink that could enlarge a person and boost their attack power.

The furbolgs were obsessed with this green beverage. Many of the furbolgs living in Winterspring carried it on their persons and took a sip whenever they had a moment.

However, this Winterspring Firewater was actually brewed by satyr demons using raw materials corrupted by fel energy from Felwood. The goal was to corrupt all the furbolgs of Winterspring, turning them into demons' servants and sowing chaos.

At this point, the forest hadn't been corrupted yet, and Mount Hyjal hadn't been invaded. Even if there were satyrs lurking here, they wouldn't dare show their faces easily beneath the holy peak of Hyjal and the flourishing Nordrassil, in an environment where the night elves and furbolgs were united against external threats. The raw materials for brewing firewater were even harder to come by, so there was no chance of getting a taste either.

Jace peered curiously into the pot. The furbolg grabbed his cloak and pulled him back, displeased. "Step back. Your darkness will pollute my things! This is important broth — for the tribe in the snow mountains. Timbermaw's duty is to care for them… It is the elder's wish."

"What medicine is this?" Jace asked, stepping back. "What does it do?"

The furbolg stared at Jace for a moment. Wrensa seemed to sense something and quickly said, "Oh, I'd like to know too."

The herbalist tilted his clumsy head to look at Wrensa, then said, "Don't be deceived by him, beautiful forest emissary. Beneath his smooth exterior lies a dark heart that inspires fear."

"Well, it's not that scary, is it?" Jace said unconsciously, rubbing his chest. Wrensa smiled. "It's not that dark, occasionally."

The furbolg picked up a bowl nearby and drank down the broth made from boiled, chopped fruit. He ran his tongue over his wet fur, looked at Wrensa for a moment, then looked at Jace again. "This medicine can cure Ulbergarvalk."

Seeing the blank looks on the two before him, the furbolg took two heavy breaths, thought for a while, then said: "Ulbergarvalk is a disease — a curse that spreads from deep within the pine forests of the snow valley. Those poisoned will die within a few days, then rise from the snow — but their souls are completely gone. They become poisonous walking corpses, attacking every living thing they see."

Jace, having heard all this, asked: "I didn't mishear, did I — he's talking about something like zombies or ghouls?"

"You didn't mishear," Wrensa said, then asked further: "Do all the furbolgs in Winterspring have this problem?"

"No," the furbolg shook his head. "Only the Frosthammer tribe in the south. The curse grows stronger, but they refuse to leave their homeland. We must help them find a way to cure the curse. But this medicine can only slow the curse's spread — it can't resolve the source."

"Frosthammer," Jace murmured the name. He remembered that in the game, in the frozen mountains south of Winterspring, there was a group of elite mobs called frost giants — their name seemed to be something like Frosthammer giants.

Could the "Frosthammer" the furbolgs spoke of refer to those Frosthammers?

Considering the differences between Thalassian, Common, and the English of his past life, combined with the geographical similarities, it was indeed highly probable.

Most importantly, beyond where the Frosthammer giants dwelt, one would quickly reach the Darkwhisper Gulch of the game. Wrensa asked, "Is it related to that thing?"

"Very possibly," Jace nodded.

There was no question that in the game's Darkwhisper Gulch, there were nothing but demons who had escaped from the Battle of Mount Hyjal — not a single furbolg remained.

Perhaps these furbolgs had died in the demon invasion. Perhaps they had long since been tormented to extinction by this curse.

"There are many mercenaries in the little green-skins' town. They hunt our people on the roads and steal this medicine and other things," the furbolg herbalist said quietly. "Getting the medicine to them now is extremely difficult… But we must try. Otherwise they won't hold on."

"We can help deliver some," Jace said immediately.

Seeing the furbolg's startled expression, he added: "If you're willing to trust me."

The furbalist looked at the elf, then back at Jace. "It's hard to imagine an evil outsider would be willing to help us like this. You'd take our medicine and leave, then sell it in the little green-skins' town."

"I swear by Queen Ysera's pendant," Wrensa said, lifting the earring from her silver hair. "I'll watch him — he absolutely won't keep any of your things for himself."

The furbolg stared at the faint silver gleam of the earring, licked his lips, and nodded. "What do you want in return?"

"We're not doing this for—" Wrensa was just about to wave a hand and refuse when Jace grabbed her wrist and squeezed hard, then said, "When we return from your Winterspring kin, we'll bring proof that we delivered the medicine. If you really want to thank us for something, there's only one thing I'd like — to learn some of the Timbermaw tribe's special potion recipes."

"Agreed," the herbalist said excitedly, nodding.
