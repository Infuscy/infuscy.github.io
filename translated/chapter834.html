Chapter 834: Cold Bloodline

The next morning, the three of them set out heading further north, preparing to ascend the mountain.

Timbermaw Hold sat partway up the mountainside. According to the furbolgs, it was an extremely ancient complex of underground caves, excavated over hundreds — perhaps thousands — of years by countless furbolgs.

Long before the furbolgs had arrived here, the caverns of Timbermaw Hold had already existed for an age.

Neither the night elves nor the Timbermaw furbolgs knew whether these had originally been natural caves, or tunnels dug out by even older inhabitants.

Treading on the thick carpet of pine needles covering the slope, Grede — clutching the potions, first-aid supplies, and the rest of the luggage to his chest, hauling the great quiver and a large bundle containing armor, carrying his shield and axe — walked steadily at the front, looking like a self-propelled overloaded cart.

Jace followed behind with a quiver on his back, carrying the spare clothes and food for the three of them, along with the potions entrusted to him by the Timbermaw herbalist.

The furbolg potions were bundled up in bottles and jars bound with rope, all lashed together in a woven grass net. They didn't look all that easy to shatter — but earthenware pots were earthenware pots, and the sight of them was enough to set one's heart on edge.

Wrensa brought up the rear with Jace's books and paper and pens, carrying her bow and quiver, her gaze fixed on the pine branches high above at the moment.

Following the mountain path upward, the higher they climbed, the more the cold wind bit.

Though there was no snow around them and the emerald pines swayed gently in the breeze, Jace could feel that the warmth radiating from the holy peak of Hyjal didn't seem to be doing much good here anymore.

By midday, they could see ahead a stone bear's head with its jaws gaping wide, and a cluster of furbolgs squatting nearby. The narrow maw of the bear's head was a small tunnel opening — this was the site of Timbermaw Hold.

A black furbolg holding a long staff was waiting at the entrance. Seeing the three of them, he beckoned and said in Thalassian, "I'll take you to the snow mountains."

"Will it be far?" Grede asked, eyeing the tunnel entrance.

"Not far," the furbolg said.

The furbolg turned and walked into the tunnel. Jace looked at the various ornaments hanging from his body — black strings of beads, white strings of bone, a shoulder-piece-like accessory woven of branches — and could tell his status was quite high.

"Really or not — is it really this big in there?" Grede glanced at the two beside him.

Jace and Wrensa both shook their heads and followed the furbolg into the cave.

Entering through the tunnel mouth and passing through a long, narrow passage into the interior, one could feel the corridors and branching paths growing ever more spacious.

Only now did Jace understand how the Timbermaw furbolgs had been able to entrench themselves in these mountains for so long — even when the Burning Legion's demons had roamed here a decade and more later, they still hadn't been able to take the place by storm, only corrupting them in secret.

Jace's impression of Timbermaw Hold was still the simple cave passage with two or three branching paths from the game.

Walking through the middle of it, feeling out its complexity — the high and low, left and right, the cave tunnels visible in every crevice and gap — he was grateful they hadn't gotten into a fight with these furbolgs over some misunderstanding.

If they'd angered them, there would have been no way through to Winterspring at all. They'd either have to turn back and go around through Azshara, climbing over layer after layer of mountains along the coast that might not even be passable, or find a way back to Gadgetzan and take another airship.

Molophil had caught up to Jace by now, and feeling the soul link draw a little closer, Jace felt a measure more at ease.

But that peace of mind was quickly swept away by the furbolgs' reactions. He noticed that some furbolgs would steal wary glances back and forth as Molophil passed by.

At first he'd thought it was just coincidence. But as it happened more and more, even Molophil grew cautious, deliberately taking a longer route while moving through the passages to avoid being detected.

Then, as they passed not far from a furbolg with strings of beads hanging across his chest, that furbolg actually twitched his nose and fixed his gaze on the direction where the succubus was concealed.

Though the succubus was quick and swiftly pulled away, that furbolg still raised his axe and walked over slowly, looking back and forth for a long while.

Wrensa and Grede had no idea Molophil existed. They'd only seen a furbolg suddenly raise his axe and grow extremely alert toward some spot, and quickly realized something might be wrong with Molophil.

"What's going on?" Wrensa asked softly.

"These furbolgs…" Jace hesitated. "Their perception of dark power is far higher than I imagined."

"Nothing wrong, I hope? I mean…" Wrensa said quietly.

Even though the two of them were speaking in Common, since there were furbolgs here who spoke Thalassian so well, who knew whether some furbolg scholar or guard squatting in some corner hadn't also picked up a little Common from the goblins?

Jace shook his head. "Don't worry. In a way, it might actually be a good thing for us."

"How so?" Wrensa asked.

Jace held up the bottles and jars in his hands. "I was planning to deliver these partly to get in good with the furbolgs in southern Winterspring ahead of time, hoping they might give us some leads. Think about it — if the furbolgs there are just as sensitive to shadow as the Timbermaw furbolgs, maybe they can help us even more."

"Let's hope so," Wrensa pressed her lips together. "But you're really not doing this for that alchemist?"

"Killing several birds with one stone — what's wrong with that?" Jace said.

Wrensa smiled, her gaze sliding to the silhouette of the furbolg up ahead as he swayed from side to side. "The hired hunters in Everlook, the evil in Winterspring — I hope they can hold on a little longer."

Led by the black furbolg, they went deeper into the hold. The terrain gradually climbed, and the temperature steadily dropped.

After spending a night in the caves, the next morning they hadn't been walking long before a cold wind began to howl through the passages. Every gust that swept past made Jace feel as though his face were being flayed.

If not for the blood of the red dragon flowing through his veins, he'd probably have been shivering by now.

After a while, a faint light began to appear up ahead. Several furbolgs standing guard here were all dusted with snow. The lead black furbolg pointed upward and said in Thalassian: "Winterspring."

Jace nodded his thanks. Wrensa put on another outer garment against the cold. Grede glanced back at the bundle of clothes in Jace's hands, looking as if he wanted to say something but held back.

"When you return to your world," the furbolg said, looking at Jace and Grede, "I hope you can tell your kind that the Timbermaw people are friendly, that we wish to live in peace. We don't want to kill."

"No problem, big fella," Grede said.

Though Jace knew that the hunters and mercenaries who came near Everlook to hunt furbolgs and plunder their valuables wouldn't care in the slightest whether they were friendly, he still nodded.

Even if it didn't make them recognize the furbolgs' friendliness, at least it would make the goblins — or any other travelers coming to Kalimdor — understand the alliance between the furbolgs and the night elves, giving them pause about how Ashenvale might react before they struck. That alone would be something.

Looking out from this exit, snow and mist obscured everything in sight, making it impossible to tell whether these were snowflakes drifting down from the mountains or whether it snowed here year-round.

Deep valleys flickered in and out of view. Frozen lakes. Where the mist thinned, a wall of towering black pines came into sight in the distance.

Those pine trees were tall enough to make Jace's heart race just looking at them from afar.

After bidding the furbolgs farewell and entering Winterspring, they followed the path the furbolgs had trodden. By midday, the snow had nearly reached Grede's waist, making every step an agonizing effort.

Putting on a padded jacket and throwing a cloak over it, shouldering the luggage once more and wading back into the snow — it was already impossible to tell whether the luggage was carrying the dwarf or the dwarf was carrying the luggage.

"If you can't find us a place to shelter from the cold by tonight, we're all going to freeze to death here, little elf," Grede grumbled as he walked.

"At least not me," Molophil called from behind.

"Me too!"

Sazeno poked his head out of the bundle on the dwarf's back. A blast of cold wind blew over and actually snuffed out the flames in his eyes in one go.

"Ahh… my eyes, I can't see anything! Ahh! Ahh!"

The imp screamed, clutching his eyes. Grede cursed: "Get back in there! Stop squirming on my back, I'm already exhausted enough, you damned thing… Jace Sesso, hurry up and take this accursed bag of yours — and this demon! Take them!"

"All right, all right…"

Jace strode up ahead and hoisted his demon bag onto his belt. By now Molophil was walking past him.

The dwarf looked at the demon, whose legs and midriff were exposed, wearing nothing but a simple breastplate and waist guard. She was treading steadily through the snow, gradually overtaking him and moving to the front.

"This… damned demon — if you get out alive, don't forget to spread our story. I don't want to die and have no one know about it for years and years."

"Spread it where, the Twisting Nether?" Molophil asked as she walked. The dwarf swore under his breath. Molophil smiled.

Wrensa glanced at the demon ahead, pulled her hood tight with a helpless look, and squinted at the surroundings. Snow and more snow — a blinding white expanse that hurt to look at.

Grede braced his knees. "The heroes of Grim Batol, who froze to death in Winterspring delivering medicine to the kindly furbolgs — what a lamentable ending to the story."

Jace raised his hand and a burst of flame sprang up. "Now the story can continue."

"Damn it, I almost forgot," Grede coughed, straightening up with a grin. "We've got a mage in our party too — really an easy detail to overlook."

"But he's not using the arcane to summon flames. The mage in our party is getting further and further from being a mage," Wrensa said, looking at the fire at Jace's fingertips. "Even in a place like this, the fire elementals are still willing to answer your call?"

"I had my doubts too, but it looks like they really did answer."

Jace pinched out the flame and went on: "Good thing we stopped in Ironforge before coming to Winterspring. Otherwise, in a place like this, there'd be no way to start a fire relying on Immolate or Fireball. One night down and we'd probably have drained every last one of our mana potions."

"So should I be thanking those Dark Iron dwarves?" Grede asked.

"Me first, before that," Jace said.

Walking across the snowfield, the path underfoot grew harder and harder to make out.

Seeing the sky grow darker, Grede climbed a low rise and looked into the distance, then shouted back, pointing at several fallen pine trees in a lower area: "You two look over there — beneath those toppled trees… we can gather some pine branches to throw together a temporary snow shelter."

Jace and Wrensa ran after the dwarf. Reaching the tree trunks, Grede found a spot to set the luggage down under one of the trunks, patted the snow off himself, and said: "I'm going to chop some wood first for the frame. See, this trunk here can serve as the main support, but it's not enough to build a full shelter."

"Wrensa, can you go find some pine branches?"

"Of course." Wrensa set down her luggage and surveyed the woods not far off. "I'll head over there."

"You're on fire duty," Grede said, looking at Jace.

Jace nodded. The dwarf pointed at Molophil. "Don't you stand around either — find something to do."

"Come with me to find branches," Wrensa said.

"Go with her," Jace said.

The succubus nodded and followed the ranger. Jace picked up a stone from the ground and looked at it. "Get out that map of Everlook. I'll take a look at where we might be in a bit."

"Let's eat something first," Grede said, chopping wood as he spoke. "That map's been squeezed to the very bottom — digging it out's going to be a big project. I won't have the strength to look if I don't eat."

"Fine, let's look before we sleep, then."

After night fell, the snow came down a little lighter than before. By now only a few drifting flakes were falling from the sky.

Wrensa and Molophil were just two faintly swaying little dark shadows over by the woods. Jace piled up some stones to make a fire spot, gathered some dry grass and leaves, and guided the fire elementals onto them, lighting it easily.

Grede was building the shelter. Feeling the firelight, he glanced over and said: "This is a real cozy spot. Reminds me of spending winters out in the wilds of the Hinterlands, Jace. And there are no trolls here — actually makes me feel a little more at ease."

Jace smiled and said: "Maybe Wrensa can find some wild game and we can have some fresh meat."

"Maybe!" Grede licked his lips happily, raised his stone axe, and chopped through a thick branch lying at his feet, laying it across the fallen trunk. Jace kicked the wet leaves and wood scraps away from the stones, trying to keep them from getting singed by the flames.

The firelight drove back the darkness, and the snow slowly melted a little — but his gaze fixed on a snow-covered boulder beside them. Something about it looked wrong, the more he looked the stranger it seemed.

He brushed the snow off the stone. Beneath it, matted, frozen fur was revealed.

Jace's eyes widened. He took a few steps back, looked at Grede, and said: "Get over here and look at this."

Grede raised his head, looked over, and walked over hesitantly with his axe. After studying it for a while, the two of them immediately set about clearing all the snow around this "boulder." Beneath the snow, a massive white-furred body gradually came into view — thick arms and legs, broad hands and feet.

"Oh, for fuck's sake!" Grede shouted. Jace immediately walked over to where he was and saw Grede staring at a hideous face half-open in a snarl, exposed from beneath the snow — like the face of some fiend.

It was the head of an unknown creature even larger than the dwarf. Its face was somewhat like a gorilla's, and somewhat like the troggs of Uldaman. Its yellowed teeth were shaped more like a man's.

Combined with this enormous body, judging by the thick limbs, this was a creature that would stand at least three or four meters tall on its hind legs.

Two horns like a bull's — one buried in the snow, the other broken off more than halfway — but it looked as if it had been broken off a long time ago. This was a dead yeti.

"Can you tell how it died?" Jace asked.

Grede swallowed. "I didn't find any external wounds. It looks like… like it froze to death. And not too long ago, either."

"A yeti that froze to death?" Jace asked in surprise.

"I can't tell you, I'm not a Bronzebeard!" Grede pointed at the yeti. "I've barely even seen a few of these creatures myself. Don't ask me — call Wrensa back and let her take a look."

Jace nodded and shouted toward the distance: "Wrensa!"

For some reason, the wind picked up again, carrying his voice away without a trace.

The swirling snow swallowed the line between here and the distant woods. Under the sudden, brutal cold, Jace couldn't even feel the soul link between himself and Molophil clearly anymore.

"Careful, Jace," the dwarf said, raising his axe.

"What?" Jace asked.

"This wind's not right. I can feel it," Grede said, staring into the sky. "Just treat me as a Wildhammer sage for a moment — I'm telling you, this wind… it didn't rise on its own, damn it!"
