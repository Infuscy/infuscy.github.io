Chapter 835: Ruler of the Snowy Mountains

The leaping flames were extinguished in a violent shudder. The scattered embers were blown away into the darkness, and the snowflakes that had glittered in the firelight were swallowed by shadow in an instant.

The cold wind howled. The frame they'd just built was sent flying, and the swirling dance of snow devoured everything in sight.

Jace held Azurewrath at the ready and backed up to Grede's side. The dwarf unslung his war hammer, picked up the shield he'd set down under the tree trunk, and hefted it, reacquainting himself with its weight.

"Come on, come on — whoever you are, come fight Grede Thunderfist face to face! Stop skulking in the shadows!" the dwarf bellowed into the wind and snow. There was no response.

Jace scanned every direction in turn, wondering who had conjured this storm.

This was definitely no blue dragon's doing. He held Azurewrath, reforged by Malygos himself. Even if most blue dragons bore little love for mortals and would attack without hesitation any creature that approached their lairs or dwellings, they wouldn't strike without reason at a friend of the Blue Dragon Aspect.

The wind howled overhead. The violent currents made it hard for Jace even to catch his breath. The distant forest — never mind any sign of Wrensa and Molophil, he couldn't even make out the black silhouettes of the trees now.

"Don't be scared, little Jace. Don't panic," Grede said quietly, leaning against Jace's leg.

"Shut up. Who's scared?" Jace cursed under his breath.

The dwarf smiled. "How long has it been since the two of us fought side by side like this, huh?" Jace answered, "I don't remember."

He glanced at the dwarf beside him — his hair was already caked with white snow, making his whole head look a size larger, and snow had piled up on Jace's own body too. The snow kept accumulating. After standing guard for a while, the snow had already risen past Jace's knees.

Grede struggled for a while, hauling his leg out of the snow. "Now you know how that yeti died, right? Damn it — whatever's behind this, it never planned to show itself!"

Jace nodded. By now he could feel his boiling blood being suppressed by the brutal cold, on the verge of congealing and ceasing to flow. His heart pounded frantically, but it felt as though something had locked it in place — every beat more labored than the last.

The runic markings beneath the collar of his void robe glimmered faintly. No ordinary natural phenomenon could possibly cause this enchanted robe to awaken its own magic. Grede was right — this was no ordinary storm.

But its resistance to cold and frost was far weaker than its resistance to shadow and flame. At a moment like this, all it had going for it was its thickness.

The dwarf struck the ice climbing up his boots with his hammer and muttered, "Damn it — trying to turn the two of us into ice sculptures, is it?"

His body suddenly swelled, shattering the ice that had accumulated on him — but was followed by several sounds of fabric being torn, indicating he'd also ruined the thick clothes he was wearing.

Yet even though he'd awakened his Earthen form and no longer feared the cold's damage, the dwarf couldn't do much more either. After all, against an enemy that refused to show itself, he had nowhere to put his strength even if he had it.

"I'll block the wind for you, Jace. You be careful too — don't keep standing in one spot, don't let yourself get frozen in place!" Sheltered by the Earthen form, they could hold out a little longer, but it was no lasting solution.

Grede's broad shoulders and body rapidly accumulated more snow and ice, soon turning him into a white, frosty mass all over again. At this rate, when he no longer had the strength to maintain his form, the situation would be incomparably worse than it was now.

So they had to take the initiative. "Flames…"

Jace intoned in the old Kalimdor tongue. His hand ignited at once. Flames crawled up his fingers to his fingertips, coalescing into a burning, luminous blaze that shuddered violently in the blizzard.

The fire elementals were shrieking. Jace could feel it — but this shrieking wasn't excitement, wasn't fervor, wasn't satisfaction. It was panic and fear.

This ball of flame in his hand couldn't withstand the snowstorm. They were crying out to him for help, complaining.

Jace intoned the elemental tongue once more, calling for more blazing fire. The surrounding tree trunks, branches, and any wood that hadn't yet been blown away all burst into flame at once. Some had just caught before being snuffed out by the wind; others, answering Jace's call, surrounded him and formed a blaze storm firm enough to stand against the gale, tearing into the wind and snow.

Now the moment of truth for his fire power had arrived. If he couldn't hold back this blizzard, that yeti would be his and Grede's fate.

He gripped Azurewrath and drew it. The sword, originally ice-blue, erupted into flames at once. Empowered by the blade, the fire elementals around him grew fiercer and fiercer, finally forming a force powerful enough to stand against the surrounding storm!

Grede stared in a daze at the blazing storm roaring as it spun counter to the blizzard. Jace's cloak fluttered in the chaotic currents, glinting with scale-like reflections in the firelight.

Jace knew his energy couldn't sustain a storm like this all night long — but he also knew the other side couldn't keep this storm going forever either.

So it came down to who could hold out longer.

But right then, Jace saw a shadow sweep across the sky.

It was neither a dragon nor a gryphon. It was like a spreading black cloud — and then it vanished just as quickly.

"Grede… did you see that?"

"I saw it." The dwarf brushed away the snow-covered beard and hair from his face and pointed at the sky. "That's the bloody thing. What kind of dragon is this?"

"If I'm not mistaken… a chimera," Jace said with difficulty.

"A chimera?!" Grede turned his head in surprise. "Didn't the little elf say that was a kind of beast? Why's it bigger than a drake?"

The dwarf had a point. When that incomparably enormous shadow swept across the sky, it felt as though even the furious blizzard had been caught up in its wake and lost its rhythm. But what followed was an even more violent vortex of currents — as though the chimera's appearance had further roused the storm elementals.

"Because this is Mount Hyjal, Grede!"

Jace roared, raising Azurewrath high with both hands — but under the ever-stronger storm, he could barely keep his feet.

Ice shards scraped across his cheeks and eyes. Even though he couldn't see a thing, he could feel that as the frostwind chimera whipped up more and more of the storm, he could barely hold out any longer.

In Winterspring, it was fighting on its home ground. Even though the fire elementals here were willing to help Jace withstand the storm, they were fundamentally outclassed.

It was just like the giant poison spiders of Ashenvale that Sura Swiftarrow had mentioned — terrible monsters nourished for ten thousand years by the World Tree Nordrassil, incomparably more powerful than their kin elsewhere, whether in size, strength, or magic.

"Watch yourself, Grede. I can't hold on. I can only…" Jace muttered through gritted teeth.

"Only what?" Grede looked at him, startled. "What do you mean you can't hold on? Don't tell me you can't hold on."

Jace didn't answer. He recalled the feeling of casting Immolate laced with shadow — when he no longer deliberately held back the fusion of shadow power with the fire elementals, when he completely unleashed his heart, then…

Suddenly, a tinge of purple appeared in the blazing flames along Azurewrath's blade. The purple rapidly intertwined with the fire. The fire elementals' roars shifted from anger and resentment to madness, and their color quickly turned into an unhealthy, murky crimson.

"What are you doing, Jace?" Grede turned to look Jace in the eye, his face full of shock.

Jace knew why. He'd also seen his own reflection in Malygos's scales — his eyes had already turned the same color as the shadowflame.

The blaze storm resisting the blizzard was rapidly consumed by shadow, transforming into a wilder, more ferocious shadow fire. Black, red, and purple flames wailed as they intertwined, screaming against the blizzard's suppression. The surrounding tree trunks and broken branches continued to wither, to rot, to blacken beneath the embrace of this dark force.

"Hey, hey, hey…"

Grede patted out the flames that had caught on his beard, wincing in pain. Though the dark fire was under Jace's control and struggling outward, some of it still grazed past the two of them.

But under the resistance of this dark force, the wind and snow rapidly weakened. That shadow finally revealed itself — soaring overhead, beating its enormous wings. Illuminated by the shadow fire, Jace finally saw its true form.

It was a creature covered in white and blue fur, resembling an enormous bird. Its body was far larger than any drake Jace had ever seen. Each of its two heads resembled a withered dragon skull, or a sharp-beaked vulture.

Its two heads let out piercing shrieks, spewing great sheets of ice at Jace and Grede. The ice instantly disrupted the rhythm of the dark fire storm Jace had summoned, sheathing the entire ground in a layer of frost. After the flames were extinguished, thick smoke with a heavy, fishy stench rose on all sides.

Seizing the chaos, the frostwind chimera shrieked and climbed higher. The storm seemed on the verge of whipping up again — but once its form was revealed, things were no longer up to it.

The incantation for Curse of Agony was still on Jace's lips when Grede's lightning hammer was already hurled.

A streak of white lightning smashed into one of the chimera's heads in the darkness. The head, which had been shrieking and summoning the ice storm, took the hit — several broken horns flew off, and it hung at a grotesque angle, drooping, unable to lift itself no matter what.

With that head smashed unconscious, the storm that had been gathering instantly dissipated.

That hammer blow wasn't the end of the assault. Jace's Curse of Agony followed close behind. The chimera, which had been glaring this way in fury, was invaded by shadow into its heart and couldn't help but let out a string of muffled roars. It no longer tried to press the attack, but turned and flew away with difficulty toward the pine forest to the north.

Though a chimera with two heads and a body covered in thick fur was far less agile than a drake, it was simply enormous. A single beat of its wings carried it a great distance away.

Jace was already channeling a Shadow Bolt with Azurewrath. Grede caught his war hammer, cursed, and winded his arm for a long throw — when a flurry of panicky cries caught the attention of both of them.

The yeti's corpse had already been burned grievously by the shadow fire. Black and purple bones were exposed, giving off a bloody stench laced with the foul miasma of shadow.

Beside the yeti's corpse, two young chimera the size of bulls were struggling to crawl away.

Their fur and wings had been partly burned away by the shadow fire. One had even had its leg burned. Injured this badly, they probably couldn't take flight any time soon.

Seeing that Grede and Jace had spotted them, they cried out even more pitifully. One of them even tumbled down the snowy slope, scrambling to its feet in a sorry state — like a rooster with its head chopped off, flopping about on the ground.

The yeti's corpse was no longer in its original place — it had been dragged a long distance away. That must have been the work of these two young chimera.

Grede sighed and lowered his war hammer, its glow fading bit by bit until it dimmed completely. The surroundings had already been thrown into chaos by the shadow fire and the blizzard. The camp they'd built earlier was nowhere to be found.

"No need to show them no mercy," he said, returning to his normal size as he spoke. "These beasts were only looking after their young."

Jace nodded and released the shadow he'd already gathered. "And if we finished off these two little ones, that chimera might keep thinking about ambushing us. We've got more important things to do — having a monster like that fixated on us all the time would be more than we could handle."

The dwarf asked: "So the yeti was their prey? It probably thought we were going to steal their kill, so it attacked — using the blizzard to split us up and slow us down, then letting the young ones sneak the prey away. After all, we don't have much meat on us — not enough to fill a monster like that's teeth."

"That's probably exactly what happened," Jace said.

The cries of the young chimera quickly disappeared behind the slope. By now, two shadows were appearing on the snow in the distance — one of them still holding a sword blade burning with an ominous flame.

Jace had been planning to conserve his mana and have Molophil use her fire to start a blaze or something. But seeing that the flames she conjured were practically indistinguishable from the dark fire he'd just unleashed — and thinking of what yeti flesh roasted by that fire would look like — he gave up on the idea.

"Jace! Grede!"

Wrensa's voice came from over there.

"We're here!" Jace struck a flame from his fingertips as a signal. But the moment he produced that flicker of fire, he realized a problem.

The fire elementals hadn't grown angry and refused his call just because he'd used shadow to empower the flames.

He stared at the fire elementals on his fingers. He could feel them striving to maintain their existence in this cold wind. An indistinguishable voice murmured within the flickering light — he couldn't tell what it was trying to tell him.

Wrensa asked hesitantly: "Are you two all right? That wind and snow just now…"

"Master…" Molophil said expressionlessly. "Just now we were blocked over there. The wind and snow were so fierce we could barely leave the pine woods. At that point I could hardly feel your existence at all."

"She can't feel you — that's definitely something a normal blizzard couldn't do, right?" Wrensa asked. She looked at the chaos all around them — especially the spot that had once been a small camp, and the yeti in the distance that had been burned quite miserably.

She twitched her nose. "Something's wrong. What on earth is going on? Another black dragon attack?"

"Don't worry. It was my magic," Jace said.

Wrensa looked at him in surprise. Grede smiled and said: "You have no idea what you missed. The two of us just successfully drove off an attack by a legendary chimera. That chimera is very likely the ruler of all of Winterspring! When we find the horn it dropped and get back to Everlook, we might just become legendary hunters!"

The elf frowned and looked at Jace. "Really?"

"Legendary chimera," Jace repeated Grede's description, coughing self-consciously. "Very likely real. Otherwise, how could it possibly summon a blizzard this terrifying?"

Wrensa sighed. "No wonder the Magisters' archives mention the frostwind chimera so many times, and repeatedly warn mages who want to travel here never to set foot anywhere they might dwell. I was wondering what kind of terrifying creature would make those magisters so wary. But I didn't expect to run into one less than a day after arriving. I'm sorry, Jace — I was completely useless just now."

"As long as you're fine, Wen." Jace glanced at the yeti's corpse. "We probably wandered into the chimera's hunting grounds."

"Get the map out and take a look, Grede. Don't get us lost."

"Damn it — it's probably buried who knows how deep!" Grede looked toward where the camp had been — now nothing but a vast expanse of snow.

Jace had no energy left to care where the luggage was right now. He tried to take a deep breath, easing the violent pounding in his heart from wielding the shadowflame so recklessly, letting that intense urge to destroy fade.

He looked at Molophil. The succubus seemed to see right through what he was feeling.
