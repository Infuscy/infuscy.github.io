Chapter 837: No Ordinary Mercenaries

The group relived their battle against the chimera as they crammed into the goblin tavern with no sign. The narrow little room was already packed. They squeezed into a two-person table, and the goblin boss spotted Morophil and immediately shouted, "Hey, get your succubus out of here! That stink is unbearable! You still want to eat or what?"

"My companion doesn't give off the scent you're familiar with, sir," Jace said.

"The last guy said the same thing," the goblin said, slapping the counter. "Hurry up and get her out!"

"Sorry, but I can promise… it won't affect everyone's appetite," Morophil said with a smile.

The goblin boss was taken aback for a moment, then nodded. "Well, since you put it that way, then it's fine. Indeed it is. Please, have a seat, my lady."

The tavern's other patrons, who had looked up curiously at the notice the argument, also lowered their heads one by one and went back to their own conversations after catching Morophil's gaze.

Jace sat down and said quietly to the succubus who'd taken the seat next to him, "Try to use your charm magic as little as possible. It might attract the attention of the goblin guards here."

"And what would you have done if he hadn't let me in, Master?" Morophil asked.

"I said as little as possible," Jace emphasized.

"Evil warlock," Vereesa and Grede said in unison, then both couldn't help but laugh.

"What can I get you, guests?" a goblin waitress approached and asked. "Care to try our Everlook Super Omelet?"

Grede slapped the table. "One for each of us, then! What kind of drinks do you have? Something to warm us up?"

"Oh, sure we have drinks. Eggnog, frothy brew, elven mead…"

"What's elven mead?" Vereesa asked curiously.

The waitress answered, "Mead brewed by the elves of Mount Hyjal. A recipe ten thousand years in the making — definitely worth a try."

"I'll have that," Vereesa said.

"Same for us," Grede said, looking at Jace. Jace nodded.

"About the omelets," the goblin waitress said. "We have giant owl eggs and owlbeast giant eggs, and the prices differ quite a bit. A giant owl egg omelet is one silver and twenty copper for four portions; owlbeast eggs would be twenty silver."

"What… owlbeast eggs?" Grede asked, stunned. "There are owlbeasts here? And you people eat their eggs?"

"What's so strange about that?" the goblin asked.

"No, no, no," Jace said. "We'll have the giant owl ones."

"No problem." The waitress smiled and turned to leave.

Grede stared wide-eyed. "People actually steal owlbeasts' eggs to eat — that's insane! Even the Amani trolls of Zinsara wouldn't dare do something like that!"

"Well, that's why they're so expensive," Vereesa said, resting her chin on her hand.

"I'd like to see what kind of roasted meats they have…" Grede craned his neck, then suddenly his expression changed and he fixed his gaze on the door. Vereesa noticed the shift in his eyes. "What is it?"

Grede made a "pssh" sound with his lips and flicked his eyes toward the door. Vereesa and Jace both looked over.

An elf in a green cloak had just walked through the door and pulled back her hood, letting loose meticulously braided golden hair. She swept her gaze across the entire tavern, then fixed her eyes on their table.

The moment their eyes met, Jace immediately looked away. This was the mysterious traveler the furbolg elder had mentioned? The moment he saw her, Jace had the feeling she wasn't a Farstrider.

Though she wore casual emerald-green clothes underneath, her outer cloak was wide and billowing, like a cloak of dense young leaves draped over her body — nothing like Vereesa's cloak, which was designed for concealment without hindering movement. And she had no weapons on her at all, neither blade nor bow.

The bigger issue was that she didn't have the big cat the elder had described by her side.

Back at the trading post entrance, Jace had already seen plenty of people bringing their animal companions into Everlook. At a table not far from here inside the tavern, someone had a hound crouched at their feet.

So it wasn't because they weren't allowed to bring them in. Was this another coincidence too?

Grede had clearly looked down as well, noticed there was no animal coming in, and lowered his head in disappointment, turning back toward the counter, where the goblin waitress had just received four omelets from the kitchen.

Jace figured he shouldn't get too nervous either. This place had all kinds of people — there was no need to get wound up every time someone in green or someone with a feline beast walked by.

And even if they did find the person the furbolg elder had mentioned, it didn't necessarily have anything to do with Mosa. Maybe she was just some random passerby…

"Hello."

A low female voice sounded beside them. At some point, the elf had already made her way to their table.

She had the look of a classic high elf — a face far thinner than Vereesa's, delicate but slightly aggressive features. Yet for some reason, her overall expression and bearing were remarkably like the sentinels they'd encountered at Silverwing Hold. And the arcane light radiating from her eyes wasn't like Vereesa's, like a flickering flame — it was more like moonlight pouring out.

What was even stranger were the dark green tattoos on her pale face and a silver crescent-shaped pendant hanging from her forehead. All of this made her look like a high elf trying to disguise herself as a night elf who didn't much care that her appearance was distinctly off.

"Hello," Jace said, looking up.

"You're mercenaries? I saw you at the fur trading post earlier. To be able to handle a chimera that powerful — you're no ordinary lot," the strange elf said. "I have a rather troublesome task, and I'm afraid you're the only ones who can help me."

"We have plenty to deal with already, so we're not taking on other jobs," Vereesa said.

"Mercenaries are in it for the money, aren't they?" the strange elf said. "I can offer a reward that's absolutely generous — one that matches your outstanding abilities."

"This has nothing to do with how much the reward is," Jace said.

He glanced at Vereesa and Grede — both of them looked a little suspicious — so he said, "Why don't you tell us what you want us to do?"

"Rescue someone," the strange elf said.

Jace's heart sank. He could see Grede opening his mouth to speak and stopping himself, and Vereesa seemed to be holding back words too, so he said directly, "Rescue someone? Can you be a little more specific?"

The strange elf hesitated, then said, "A tauren."

"Mosa Woodmane?!" Grede exclaimed.

"How could you possibly know her name?" the strange elf's expression changed in an instant. "We'll take the job," Jace said decisively.
