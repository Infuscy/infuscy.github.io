Chapter 841: Source of Savagery

Under the green dragon's guidance, the group walked into the pine forest between the gorges. By now it was the middle of the night; the snow seemed to have let up a bit, and the wind was no longer as vicious as before. The sound of their footsteps crunching through the snow grew clearer and clearer.

"How much farther?" Grede couldn't help asking.

"Just ahead," Sola said, pointing at several massive boulders ahead that looked as though they'd collapsed from the cliffside.

Near the boulders stood a number of toppled pines, their trunks wrapped in withered, unidentifiable vines — a scene of utter disarray. As they reached the center of it, Sola's expression grew heavier and heavier. Jace tried to focus his mind and sense the magic around them, but he felt nothing at all.

"You mentioned that this trap was set by some evil force. If you're talking about Shadow magic, I imagine it's mostly dissipated by now, Miss Sola."

Sola nodded. "The last time I came back here, the power the trap had unleashed had already mostly faded. I shouldn't have gotten my hopes up."

"We should search more carefully," Vereesa said. "Whatever kind of trap it is, once set, it's bound to have left other traces behind. Even an arcane trap leaves residual embers — a magical resonance."

"Then I'll go have a look among those rocks," Grede said.

The group split up and moved outward in different directions. Jace gazed toward the deeper part of the pine forest between the gorges. He could vaguely sense that something dark lurked within, but he couldn't be entirely sure.

"Morophil, can you sense anything?"

"I don't know," Morophil said. "You could ask that little thing — it's more sensitive to this stuff than I am, Master." Jace nodded and said softly, "Sazeno."

"What is it, Master?" the imp popped out of the bag, looked around, and said, "Why is it still all snow? When are we getting out of this damned place?"

"The sooner you detect any unusual Shadow power here, the sooner we can leave," Jace said.

"I'm telling you, Master… no unusual Shadow power, but unusual arcane power — I can feel a little of that." It craned its neck to look up at Jace, then focused its gaze on the sky and murmured, "Why does the moon feel so bright today?"

"The moon?"

Jace raised his head and looked up, then realized that the moon seemed to have gained a halo around it.

No — it wasn't that the moon had grown brighter. It was as though the entire forest had been dusted with a silver lunar glow. The silvery light flickered more and more, as if a hazy filter had been draped over the whole pine forest and the surrounding boulders. In the dead of night, it was rather dreamlike.

Then came a series of ethereal sounds echoing through the valley…

"Jace Sesso, move!" Sola's shout suddenly rang out from a distance. Jace was startled to hear the green dragon, who always spoke in a calm, even tone, suddenly yell like that — but he immediately sprang aside.

But it was already too late. The ethereal sound turned piercing in a span of time far too short for Jace to react, and the silver glow around him suddenly coalesced into a blinding white beam that came crashing down from the sky!

The bombardment came without warning. Jace had only shifted slightly before he was struck hard. A ring in his ears, then darkness, and he felt as though something had severed a string in his mind — he lost nearly all sensation, able to do nothing but cling to that last shred of remaining awareness to tell himself he was still alive…

Still alive…

When his senses returned, the black dragon cloak was already burning, the arcane magical flames giving off a brilliant purple-tinged white glow.

Jace beat out the flames on the cloak a few times, then looked back at where he'd been standing. Clusters of white-hot arcane magical embers were scattering into a drift of glowing motes. The snow and earth had been blasted into a shallow crater seven or eight meters across by the terrible arcane force — it looked exactly as though a cannon shell had hit it.

What kind of terrifying magic was this? He felt as though he'd been hit by some kind of precision-targeted artillery strike. Was this Starfire?

Without question, the black dragon-hide cloak had blocked most of the damage for him, but the collar and sleeves of his robe had been blown apart, his shirt was more than half burned away, and the trousers under his robe had been blasted into nothing but burning rags. One boot had been blown far, far away.

But this was already far better than he'd imagined — at least the robe had more or less survived.

Jace tried to climb to his feet, but his legs didn't seem to obey him. He lifted his robe and looked down, seeing that the leg missing its boot had been blasted pitch-black — he just couldn't feel any pain…

At that moment, a warm breeze enveloped him. Sola, eyes half-lidded, already had pale yellow natural energy swirling around her hands. She murmured an incantation, guiding the warm breeze to heal his body.

Soon, along with the warm feeling, a violent pain seized his mind. Jace couldn't help but cough a few low coughs — at least this meant consciousness and sensation were both returning. The healing was working.

He tried to get up again, and this time he finally stood — but the moment he took a step, he nearly fell. "Jace! Hey! What the hell was that?!"

Grede came running from a distance. On the other side, Vereesa suddenly went pale, raised her bow, and shouted, "Behind you! Look out!"

An arrow whistled past Jace's face and thudded into something — he wasn't sure what. In a daze, he turned his head and saw a massive figure, white as snow with a patch of deep-blue feathers atop its head, that had crept up close at some point without anyone noticing, completely filling his field of vision.

It was an owlbeast of suffocating size — far larger than the one he and Grede had encountered in the Hinterlands. Its broad, elk-like antlers were practically crystal-clear in the moonlight, still radiating arcane power.

Had that spell just now been cast by it?

The owlbeast took the arrow just above its chest, staggered back a step with a choked sound, raised a hand bearing enormous sharp claws, and pulled the arrow out of the thick, blood-stained feathers of its neck. It tossed the arrow aside, then fixed its furious gaze on Jace.

It howled again and raised its hand to slam down hard. Seizing the moment, Jace shook his mind clear and tried to dissolve into shadow to get away — but having just been ambushed by that Starfire, both his incantation and casting speed were a beat too slow. He was about to eat that blow.

But the owlbeast before him swayed and froze. Seizing the opportunity, Jace shot several meters in a shroud of shadow — but the owlbeast seemed to have lost its desire to attack. The raised claws slowly lowered, showing no intention of pressing the assault further.

"Master, I'd advise the rest of you not to disrupt my magic with anything else. It may be heavy, but its movements aren't slow at all — not its spellcasting, not anything." Morophil's voice rang out from the side.

Jace looked at the succubus. The magical sigils across her body were all blazing with fiery light, and a steady stream of purple-red blood was flowing from the corner of her mouth. Where it dripped onto the snow it instantly melted a furrow.

It must have been the Soul Link sharing a significant portion of the arcane damage that left her wounded like this. "You seduced this monster."

"It won't last long," Morophil wiped the blood from her chest, licked her hand, and said. "Its will is too strong. We need to leave quickly — there must be more around. I can feel it from this beast's emotions."

Jace nodded, retrieved his shoe, and made his way over to Sola and the others. Vereesa was aiming at the owlbeast, but in the end she lowered her arrow. She had caught sight of several massive dark shapes already moving through the snowy valley below — they seemed to have noticed the sudden arcane explosion and were curiously making their way over. This was no time to get bogged down in a fight.

The group quickly withdrew toward the higher forest. Though Jace still had ringing in his ears and ached all over, under the sustained nourishment of the green dragon's magic he was already far better than before.

"How are you, Jace?" Vereesa asked. "I'm fine," Jace said.

"Don't worry about his injuries, Quel'dorei," Sola said. "Red dragon essence flows in his blood — he won't be destroyed so easily. And I will tend to his wounds."

Vereesa's brow relaxed a little. She clenched her fists and said, "I couldn't sense these beasts approaching at all, nor the magic guiding them. Damn it!"

Sola explained, "They're no ordinary beasts. They've almost merged with this valley entirely. And their magic isn't like a mage's incantations — it's a raw force. That's what makes them so powerful and dangerous."

The group found a dark spot and, confirming no owlbeasts were approaching, finally breathed a sigh of relief.

But given that enormous owlbeast had approached as tracelessly as a ghost just moments ago, none of them could ever truly let their guard down. "Here, Jace — I know you'll be fine." Grede tossed over a healing potion.

"Thanks." Jace nodded.

Recalling the silver glow that had shimmered around him when that spell came down, Jace guessed it might have approached under cover of the moonlight.

Morophil's movements were far slower than normal. The moment she stopped, she leaned against a tree to steady herself, the usual nonchalant look gone from her face.

That Starfire just now had been genuinely terrifying.

Sola observed the wounds on Jace's leg for a moment. Jace asked, "Why did this owlbeast attack me first without the slightest hesitation?"

"I don't know," Sola shook her head. "It may have sensed your demonic power…"

"Then shouldn't they attack the demon first?" Grede asked.

"Everything here is chaos. It's difficult for me to give a precise motive for these mad owlbeasts' behavior, dwarf." Sola stood and looked toward the succubus. "You saved that poor beast that didn't know what it was doing, Morophil. I'm grateful. I never imagined a demon could possess… compassion. But dark magic will only provoke them further. We need to be more careful from here on."

"I only wanted to protect my Master, green dragon," Morophil said. "And that owlbeast had been tormented by some other demon's curse for a long time. Perhaps I could help it remember, just a little, the days when it was still sane."

"How do you know that?" Sola asked. "What demon curse?" Jace asked.

Vereesa and Grede also looked over.

"I'm certain someone tried to enslave these owlbeasts before me. That owlbeast's will resisting my power told me a great deal." Morophil explained, "Whoever placed this curse upon them, their magic is more aggressive than mine — not mere seduction."

"So," she looked at Sola and said, "you really don't need to worry about my power making anything worse."
