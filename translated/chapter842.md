## Chapter 842: Traces of Corruption

Morning. The snow-blanketed woods stood still as if frozen. Sunlight spilled off the snow in glittering sparks, setting off the scene like an exquisite oil painting.

A white snow hare broke the peace. The little creature left a trail of hops across the smooth snow, darting back and forth among the trees, pausing now and then to look around, then plunging back into the snow, continuing its little leaps until it vanished from sight.

The woods fell quiet again. A breeze swept by, the dappled shadows swayed, and Vereesa raised herself from a thicket and said, "No owlbeasts in this stretch of woods. We can try moving forward a bit."

"You're sure that rabbit could see well enough?"

In such a quiet atmosphere, Grede's rough dwarven voice stood out especially.

He brushed the snow off his hood and climbed out of the snow. Vereesa glanced at him and said, "A hare's wariness is a match for my own, Grede."

"Using yourself as the benchmark isn't exactly convincing," Grede said.

Sola, holding her hood, came to Grede's side and looked toward the forest ahead. "I trust Lady Vereesa Windrunner. She's an excellent forest walker. Even many Kaldorei druids would have a hard time getting such a stubborn little creature to obey like that."

"Hear that, dwarf?" Vereesa said softly.

Grede leaned on his axe against the tree trunk and said in a low voice, "Praise her a little less, Miss Sola. I'm not joking."

Sola glanced at the warlock on the other side, as if confirming the situation here, and perhaps a little curious as to why Jace had been silent all this time.

Truth be told, Jace didn't have the energy to join in their bickering. At this moment the wound on his leg was killing him, and his ribs and half his body throbbed with waves of pain.

As the healing potion took effect and the green dragon's healing worked on him, the pain from the damage dealt by that Starfire or that god-awful Moonfire last night grew ever more distinct.

He knew this was of course a good thing — being able to feel pain was infinitely better than going limp and foggy-minded and feeling nothing at all. But pain was still pain, no matter how you sliced it.

He lightly touched his calf and felt a sharp, tearing pain. Right now all he wanted was for Vereesa to send that rabbit a little farther, to explore more of this valley woodland, so he could find another chance to rest a bit more.

"Jace, I think we can move forward a bit. How are you?" Vereesa asked softly.

"No problem." Jace nodded and pulled himself up using the tree trunk beside him.

They hadn't rested all night — not just because this was owlbeast territory, but because after sensing that demonic power, with Mosa still nowhere to be found, none of them could sleep easy.

"Are there any demons in Winterspring?" Vereesa asked, looking toward Sola.

"I don't know," Sola said. "But not many demons would dare approach Mount Hyjal anymore."

"We found a demon who'd been to Mount Hyjal in Dire Maul, Miss Sola," Vereesa said. "Her name was Dathremor. She was an Ered'ruin. Have you heard of her?"

"Ered'ruin?" Sola said, somewhat surprised. "How dare she appear near Mount Hyjal? What was she doing there?"

"Searching for demonic treasures," Vereesa said. "Either left over from the War of the Ancients, or brought here by other demons. We're not certain yet."

"I also appeared near Mount Hyjal," Morophil said. "I even passed right under it. Though I'll admit, if Master hadn't insisted on taking me through there, I'd rather have had my fel blood drained and been thrown back into the Twisting Nether than pass by there again. Mount Hyjal isn't a forbidden zone that demons cannot set foot in. As long as the temptation outweighs the risk, a demon could very well venture deep here."

"What kind of demon do you think could have provoked these owlbeasts?" Vereesa asked. "Could it have anything to do with Dathremor?"

Morophil said, "I can't tell. Perhaps a powerful succubus, or Nathrezim, though that's unlikely. As for Ered'ruin, they probably wouldn't have the patience to corrupt a whole species by such an intricate method. They'd prefer to annihilate this place outright with Shadow Bolts or fel fire."

"Satyr?" Jace asked.

Hearing the name "satyr," Sola visibly frowned.

Grede laughed and said, "If it's really a satyr, then there's nothing to fear. I could take five or six of those demons by myself! Have you forgotten, Jace, in Maraudon?"

Vereesa and Morophil exchanged a glance. They surely still remembered that terrifying satyr lord deep in Maraudon, Vilethorn, who could practically ignore Shadow magic.

Even though he was dead and the power of his sigils had already been transferred to Morophil, the difficulty they'd had fighting Vilethorn back then was still fresh in Jace's memory.

Seeing that everyone still had a lingering fear of that satyr, Grede put away his smile and continued forward.

The whole morning, the sky was crystal clear. Morophil walked at the front, searching everywhere for traces of demons. Grede kept his eyes locked on her, looking as if he was about to go up and ask her something whenever she made a move.

Jace was just about to go reassure him when Morophil suddenly turned back and pointed at a dense stretch of pine forest to the north. "Over there, Master."

The succubus charged swiftly through the forest on her hooves, as nimble as a blood-red deer. Behind her, everyone except Vereesa lumbered after her like a pack of starving, weak black bears and clumsy wolves.

Catching up to Morophil, Jace caught his breath, clutched his still-unrecovered waist, and gritted his teeth — then saw a massive grey-white shape lying before him.

It was the corpse of an owlbeast. It seemed even larger than the one that had attacked Jace last night — or perhaps it just looked bloated from lying there dead, blanketed in snow. Its head, compared to its body, was like a tiny parrot's head stuck onto a bear. But its absolute size was still terrifying — at least far larger than an ogre's head. Not to mention the elk-like antlers atop its head, which, though shattered, could still be called "magnificent."

"Did a demon kill it?" Grede asked.

"No." Sola walked around to the other side and said, "Its chest was blown open by arcane energy. Another owlbeast killed it."

Morophil stepped onto the owlbeast's corpse, raised her dagger, and drove it hard into its skull. The sharp Blade of Eternal Darkness cleanly split open a hole in the solid bone.

In such freezing weather, even a long-dead corpse would be hard to decompose — but the owlbeast's body still gave off a faint, unsettling stench that was disturbing on both a physical and psychological level.

Morophil reached her hand into the hole, scooped out a lump of something — unclear whether it was clotted blood or some other kind of mucus — and stuffed it into her mouth. She chewed a couple of times while gazing into the distance, like a lioness savoring her prey.

"Why would an owlbeast kill an owlbeast?" Grede asked.

"Perhaps because it was too far gone with demonic corruption — beyond saving," Vereesa said. "Possible," Sola nodded.

"Or maybe not severe enough. Not enough to fit in," Jace said.

Everyone looked at Jace with rather complicated expressions. Then Morophil suddenly said, "I can sense the trail of the demon that infected it. Follow me."
