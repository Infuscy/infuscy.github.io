## Chapter 843: Den of the Fallen

With that, Morophil leaped off the owlbeast's corpse and headed north at a quick pace, her hooves leaving only a string of dull clopping sounds on the snow and branches before she vanished from sight.

Though the thought of having to run again made Jace's calf ache faintly, having finally found a lead, he was still quite excited.

As she followed, Sola murmured to herself, "If Ysondre knew I trusted a demon this much, she'd surely lock me up and never let me out."

"Who's Ysondre? Your mother?" Grede asked.

"No, but she did raise me, so she might as well be my mother."

Sola's tone was light, but her expression was anything but. The name "Ysondre" seemed to have slipped out unintentionally, but now she appeared to somewhat regret bringing it up.

Jace had never heard the name Sola Shade — or rather, her real name, Solamiel — but he was more than familiar with the name Ysondre.

Ysondre had once appeared in the game as a world boss. She was one of Ysera's most powerful green dragon guardians, and one of the first green dragons to fall to the Emerald Nightmare. Perhaps that more or less explained why Solamiel had never appeared in the game.

Either she had fallen alongside Ysondre and become a mad green dragon, casually slain like the whelps under Eranikus in the Sunken Temple. Or she had died fighting the Nightmare — killed by Ysondre's own hand.

Judging from Sola's expression, Ysondre may have already begun showing signs of trouble by this point — meaning the Nightmare's corruption was already starting to have a real effect.

What was the state of the Emerald Dream at this time, and what were Ysera's intentions?

Jace didn't much want to think about things he couldn't do anything about. As long as the green dragon before him didn't run into trouble, that was enough.

Based on what Jace knew from his past life, he could roughly judge that since Ysondre's condition worried Sola, she was most likely standing on the opposite side of the Nightmare — at least she hadn't been corrupted yet.

Morophil came to a stop beside a chaotic tangle of snow-laden shrubs and toppled pines. Jace ran up and found her staring at a cave entrance half-concealed by dead branches.

The cave entrance looked like an ordinary place for a giant bear to hibernate, but the moment the succubus arrived here, it seemed deeply sinister.

"I'm guessing we're going in no matter what," Grede said in a low voice. "Musa might even be in there," Jace said.

"I can't say," Sola said. "If Musa were in there, I should at least have some sense of it."

Morophil said, "I don't know if that tauren is in there, but if you want to find out who's been trying to influence these creatures, you'd best go in and see."

"Could that staff be in here?" Grede turned his head and said. "The Moon Goddess's staff — isn't there a rumor that the owlbeasts hid it away?"

"I'd rather that were just a rumor," Vereesa said. "Otherwise we might have to face a demon wielding the Moon Goddess's staff."

"Your goddess Elune would surely choose us over the demons," Grede said, looking at Sola. "Of course," Sola said, but her expression wasn't a good one.

Grede of course also sensed her complicated unease and swallowed with difficulty.

Actually, Jace wasn't too worried. He didn't think they'd find the Scythe of Elune here, and if someone really had claimed that staff and hidden themselves inside, the area around this cave would probably be crawling with worgen by now.

He pointed inside and said, "We'll know everything once we're in. Morophil, take the lead."

"Yes, Master," Morophil replied.

"This one's got no heart at all," Grede muttered. "You've got the nerve to say that?" Vereesa said.

Grede fired back a couple more lines. Sola looked at the two of them, then also holding her cloak, walked into the cave.

The interior of the cave, piled with feathers, broken branches, and rotting leaves, was still fairly spacious — at least enough for the group to walk upright without difficulty. The owlbeasts had probably dug it out as a dwelling.

The tunnel was filled with the smell of scalding water on chicken feathers — unpleasant to breathe in. But within that odor also lingered a faint, bloody scent, like the smell of Shadow, yet not as sharply explosive as the flame belched by a black dragon.

After entering the cave, the wind outside and the sound of branches scraping were suddenly muffled without. Footsteps became crystal clear, and though everyone moved forward with great care, it was inevitable that they'd step on something.

Jace braced against the wall, trying to keep his boots from making too much noise, but at this point even the sound of his hand rubbing against the earth wall seemed deafeningly clear.

In this kind of silence, it was easy to let oneself think there might be nothing in this cave at all.

Morophil walked at the front, hidden in shadow. Jace could sense that even while fully invisible, she was being extremely careful. She definitely knew something was lurking in here.

When it came to demons, no second person here could judge more accurately than she could. "Master, we can't go any farther."

Morophil's voice arrived before her shadow did. Jace was stopped by her outstretched hand, and only then did the succubus's full form emerge from the shadows.

In an extremely low voice, she whispered, "I can sense demon magic lying in wait ahead, Master. But I'm sorry — I can't accurately determine their positions. These might be the traps that Sola Shade mentioned."

"Does that mean there really is a demon hiding in here?" Grede raised his shield, stared inside, and said in a low voice. "The one who took Musa — damn it."

The dwarf was just about to move forward when Vereesa reached out and yanked him back, saying softly, "What are you doing? Didn't you hear Morophil say there are traps ahead?"

Grede said, "I'm not you, with your skinny arms and legs. Let me trigger all these traps. We have to hurry — if that demon gets away…"

"Careful," Sola warned. "Even if I revealed my true form, I couldn't break free from those dark traps. Whoever set them, they were prepared to trap targets of great power."

"Then what do we do?" the dwarf asked, looking at her. "The answer is right in there! I don't think I have many options."

"There's no need to take unknown risks ourselves, Grede." Jace pulled the spellbook from his bag, glanced at it, silently recited the incantation once to make sure he hadn't misremembered it, then took out a soul shard and tossed it to the ground.

"Let me do it — or to be precise, let my minion. Muoanan, Suojia, Tuluoxiduosi, Luokexini!"

He swept his hand in a circle. The dark cave was instantly illuminated by the energy of a void portal, radiating a purple-blue arcane glow. A blood-red felhound crawled out of the torn rift, flipped over, and landed steadily on the ground.

Jace could tell that this felhound wasn't as large as he'd expected.

Without Teron Gorefiend's staff, plus the two fairly powerful felhound watchers he'd summoned earlier in Ilgathar Tower taking up a portion of his energy, he could no longer summon felhounds as powerful as he once had.

But this was only meant to trigger traps — he wasn't expecting it to perform any feats of glory. This would be enough.

"Go on, Dazarim the Fifth. Time for you to shine."

As Jace said this, the felhound shook its horns and bristles, its tentacles wriggling excitedly as it burrowed into the depths of the tunnel.

Morophil's eyes widened slightly as it charged recklessly forward, barreling straight toward traps that might spring at any moment. She seemed a little surprised at the degree of its loyalty to Jace.

"I almost forgot," Grede said excitedly. "Get in there, demon!"

"But why the Fifth, Jace?" Vereesa blinked.

"Because—" Before Jace could finish, Grede said through a barely suppressed laugh, "I feel sorry for the previous four Mr. Dazarims."

"Don't feel sorry yet, Grede — the previous four aren't all dead yet." Jace pointed ahead and said, "Go, follow it!"

Sola stared at the three people ahead, somewhat shocked, unable to say a word — as if she was starting to lose track of whether she was fighting evil or working alongside it.

But in any case, the immediate problem had been solved. So she also touched the crescent pendant on her forehead, dimming the faint light it emitted once more, and caught up with the group ahead.

Dazarim the Fifth darted through the tunnel, rounding several bends. Jace and Morophil barely kept up with it, staying vigilant at all times. Not long into the run, Grede behind them couldn't help but ask, "How come we haven't hit a trap yet…"

The words had barely left his mouth when a shriek of Shadow detonation suddenly erupted ahead. Rounding a small bend, they saw the felhound bound to the ground by chains of dark green, vicious and violent energy constantly eroding it, as if trying to strangle it to death. Was this a fel fire bondage?

No wonder Sola had said she couldn't sense much Shadow energy from the place where Musa was trapped. Although this trap involved Shadow power, most of its strength was still fel fire.

The felhound struggled constantly — it was hard to tell whether the Shadow chains or the felhound had the upper hand. Soon, Dazarim the Fifth suddenly broke free of the fel fire chains and charged into the darkness, leaving behind only a scatter of emerald flames and scorched demon blood.

"Well done, Dazarim the Fifth!" Grede said excitedly. "I knew these traps were nothing to fear!"

Morophil said, "Felhounds have never been particularly afraid of these magics, dwarf. They're Annihilan, bred specifically to hunt mages. You could say they were born for this."

"Perhaps," Grede said. "But when I become a true Mountain King, Miss Morophil — when I can truly awaken the legendary avatar… that Earthen avatar capable of withstanding countless terrible sorceries and magics — you'll know a mere felhound is nothing compared to me."

"By Quel'Thalas's standards, you already count as a true Mountain King, Grede," Vereesa said. "Not enough. Your elven praise means far too little to us!" Grede said.

Vereesa snorted coldly and walked to the front.

Hearing Grede's words, Jace recalled the Avatar ability from Warcraft III, which was spell-immune… but the Avatar that players could use in World of Warcraft, of course, couldn't be spell-immune — it could only break some movement-impairing effects.

But could the descendants of the Earthen truly awaken a spell-immune avatar?

Jace knew better than to take every legend at face value, but legends usually had some basis in fact. Perhaps only the rulers of Ironforge — the Bronzebeard clan — knew the truth of this legend.

The group chased the felhound deeper into the cave. This cave was far deeper and larger than its outward appearance suggested. Jace watched the demon blood the felhound left behind along the way and felt that if there was one more trap, it might not hold out.

And then, as if summoned by his thoughts, came the sudden explosion of a fel fire detonation. The felhound let out a shriek. This time it was bound tightly to the ground by fel fire, utterly unable to break free. And it looked as though its strength was not only insufficient to break the chains — it couldn't even withstand the fel fire's burning.

Vereesa softly murmured an incantation and, following behind, tried to dispel the fel fire chains with arcane energy. But the fel fire suddenly expanded, nearly swallowing her up as well. Fortunately, she dodged in time, retreated several steps, and was caught by Jace.

"Don't try to save it!" Sola said. "That's how Musa got trapped…"

"Does this mean it's time for Dazarim the Sixth to make his entrance?" Grede asked.

"Summoning a felhound wastes too much mana," Vereesa said. "If there are many more traps inside…"

"Miyouni, Zhaibaluoni, Moluokairuio." Jace looked at the felhound and recited the Demon Empowerment incantation from the spellbook. In an instant, the felhound's entire body erupted in blood-red flames — even Morophil couldn't help but lick her lips watching.

Empowered by these flames, the felhound's form seemed to grow much larger. It instantly roared, broke free of the fel chains' control, and charged deeper into the cave. The burst of demonic aura it gave off made the people behind it freeze in shock.

"Don't hesitate, keep up," Jace reminded them. "This empowerment incantation won't last long."

At that moment, roars in demonic tongue echoed from the depths of the cave.

"They've broken in! Stay here and hold them off — serve me to the last!"

Following that shout, the once peaceful depths of the cave suddenly erupted with one bizarre shriek after another. Hearing these sounds, Jace felt the hackles on the back of his neck stand on end.

"The demons inside are trying to escape, Master!" Morophil said.

"After them, Dazarim — full speed ahead!" Jace commanded.

The felhound let out a mad shriek and charged into the depths like a burning cannonball, its cries blending completely with the demons' strange howls into one.
