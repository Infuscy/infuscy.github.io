Chapter 846: Fleeing from the Nightmare

Sormarel flew through the night sky, several people lying on her back fast asleep, when Jace suddenly threw off his cloak and sat bolt upright, staring at the starry sky beyond the snow-capped mountains.

Wrensa, Grede, and even Molophil were all dead asleep nearby. Only now did Jace recall what had just happened — Sormarel had been flying them toward the elven ruins, using green dragon magic to help them rest properly along the way.

He had to admit, even waking this abruptly, he still felt thoroughly rested. The sleep quality from a green dragon's magic was something else.

As if sensing the movement on her back, Sormarel turned her head. "Why are you up, Jace Sesso? How is your wound? In that cave, I don't think the owlbeast's injuries have fully healed. If you need further treatment, just tell me."

"Pretty much fine. I didn't wake up because of the wound," Jace said, rubbing his head.

Still, he realized the Chain of Will around his neck seemed a little hot. Had the Chain of Will resisted the green dragon's sleep spell for him?

The thing really couldn't tell friend from foe. Recalling how comfortable that sleep had been, he almost wished he could get hit with a lullaby spell or something — the past few days had exhausted him, body and soul.

"My sleep spell does no harm to mortals. You can sleep soundly," the green dragon said. "Rest a while longer. We're still some distance from the Kaithrill ruins."

"I'm not worried about your magic." Jace, freshly awake, smacked his dry lips uncomfortably. "It might be that cave. Those demons…"

"I've never fought demons before, Jace Sesso. Facing those felhounds, I didn't dare try any spells, so I really was at a loss. You're the most magic-dependent of them — you should understand how I feel."

"Of course I do. Channeling magic in front of a felhound is suicide. But that's not what I meant. I meant that doomguard."

"I've only heard of that kind of demon in War of the Ancient stories. Seeing one in person is truly unsettling. What a terrifying warrior," the green dragon said.

As she spoke, she beat her wings once, maintaining her balance. Jace could feel the gentle natural energy radiating from her, blocking the bitter Winterspring wind from him and the others still asleep.

Jace gazed at her golden horns. "No matter how powerful he was, he's dead now. What concerns me more is that a demon's strength usually reflects his master's. That means the demon who fled may have extraordinary power. So the battles ahead could be very difficult. I want you to tell me everything you know. Who exactly are we facing? Or rather, what?"

"I know no more than you do," the green dragon said, tilting her head. "Or do you think I'm deceiving you?"

"At least you're hiding something from us," Jace said.

Sormarel slowly breathed out, white vapor escaping her jaws. "Very well. The Dream is no longer as peaceful as it once was, Jace Sesso. I don't know when it started, but in my dreams I can no longer see Ysondre. Shadows have covered her lair, and not long after, those shadows even tried to seep into my mind and body. I felt frightened, and Ysondre wouldn't answer me, so I fled from there."

"You fled the Emerald Dream?" Jace asked.

"Yes," Sormarel said. "I woke up, just like you did moments ago — but far from this easy. I barely escaped by force. Those shadows… even now, waking up, I've nearly forgotten their shapes. Perhaps that's a good thing. I don't know how I finally broke free. It must have been Queen Ysera who gave me that courage. But what frightens me is that now I can't even hear the Queen's voice anymore."

"But I know I must find a way to locate Ysondre again and figure out what's wrong with the Dream. But no one was willing to help me. My brothers and sisters tried to stop me from waking — they wanted to capture me, and the druids' unconditional loyalty to them meant that once they found me, they'd surely report my whereabouts. Only one tauren druid was willing to help — Mosa Woodmane."

"She's not like the Kaldorei. The tauren have no pact with Queen Ysera to guard the Dream. On the grasslands where they live, you can't even see the shadow of Nordrassil. So she wouldn't willingly submit to my siblings' control — that was good news for me. She only wanted to save her own people, just like me, Jace Sesso. But we both understood that relying only on our own strength against the threat we faced would lead only to death."

"So we must find the Scythe of Elune, enter the Dream, and call upon the forest guardians to cleanse the seeds of corruption that seek to taint it. Once everything settles, she can use that powerful artifact to save her people — that is her due."

As she spoke, Sormarel's tone was resolute. The young dragon's voice was hoarse yet calm, accompanied by natural magic brushing past like a breeze, as if she could convince anyone who doubted her abilities.

But she couldn't convince Jace. Though he hated to think it, deep down he couldn't imagine her accomplishing all this — whether finding the Scythe of Elune or saving the Emerald Dream.

And right now, he could detect a trace of fear and unease beneath the green dragon's resolute tone. If he weren't a warlock, if he hadn't known Molophil, after all this time of learning to read emotions, even now he might not have sensed that faint tremor.

But as the green dragon began to reveal some of what she'd concealed, her emotions slowly surfaced as well.

Jace could roughly guess the source of her anxiety. Witnessing the horrific corruption of the Nightmare — those blood-red plants, tumor-like growths of eyeballs, tentacles, deformed vines, trees like skeletons — feeling that darkness gradually spread across the land where she was born and raised, being forced to flee to avoid losing control… and now, wanting to save her loved ones, being forced to find a way back.

No fear, no terror — that would be impossible.

Sormarel's moonlit eyes trembled like a stream rippling in the wind. She stared hard at the frozen lake beneath the snowy wind, beating her wings once more as if to release that invisible pressure.

She continued, "Before I left the Emerald Dream, I thought the Kaldorei had long since cleansed the demons around Mount Hyjal — after all, even the war between the satyrs and the Kaldorei was thousands of years ago. I never expected to encounter so many, so powerful, extraterrestrial invaders in the sacred land of Winterspring. You may be right, Jace Sesso — perhaps the satyrs have returned. But it doesn't matter. The Scythe of Elune defeated them once; it can defeat them a second time…"

"Since you lived in the Emerald Dream, you must know who those forest guardians are," Jace said.

"Werewolves," Sormarel said without hesitation. "I know how humans view such creatures, Jace Sesso, but the Great Tree of Dalanir in the Emerald Dream soothed their savagery, making them willing to forever aid the druids and Queen Ysera in guarding that place."

"I just feel that humanity's terrifying legends about werewolves aren't baseless," Jace said.

Sormarel lowered her voice. "I think I understand the Emerald Dream better than humans do, dark mage."

Jace was silent for a moment. "Sorry, I know. I'm just worried things might go wrong. But why are you willing to tell me all this now?"

Sormarel said, "Because of everything these past few days, I now believe in your strength and your goodness. Just as I believe in Mosa Woodmane."

"After seeing my dark magic?" Jace asked in a half-joking tone.

Sormarel said, "If the Kaldorei knew I wanted to find the Scythe of Elune, they'd think I was no different from a demon. But they don't realize the danger in the Dream… There are enemies far more dangerous than out-of-control werewolves in this world. I know you understand that too. I can feel it."

Jace nodded. "But I'm curious — how do you know where that artifact is? Who told you it was hidden in Winterspring?"

"Ysondre told me long ago. She said Elune's beloved beast guards Elune's treasure in Winterspring… and the Winterspring tribe's furbolgs confirmed it as well."

"But we found nothing in those snow mountains," Jace said.

Sormarel said, "That proves nothing. Winterspring doesn't only have owlbeasts there — there are high cliffs to the north, snow valleys to the south. Once I rescue Mosa, we'll head to the next location to search. This time we'll be more careful."

"We can help, Sola," Jace said.

"Mortals can't set foot in the Emerald Dream like druids. And you even less so, Jace Sesso." The green dragon turned her head, those moonlit eyes fixed on him. "It's not that I want to refuse your help. I'd love nothing more than for you and your friends to adventure with me as we did yesterday, cleansing the evil from Winterspring. But the Dream… it's just like that. It's not Winterspring."

Jace was at a loss for words.

The green dragon didn't know everything he knew, so it was only natural that, facing the Dream's corruption, she placed her hopes in the legendary Blade of Elune said to be connected to the Dream.

After all, when facing the Burning Legion's invasion, even the night elf druids who had sealed away the Scythe of Elune had no choice but to bring it out again to fight the demons.

And the druids had thought the Emerald Dream had purified the werewolves' savagery — it was only after summoning them that they realized the werewolves were out of control again, forcing them to find a way to seal them back. But by then, it was too late.

But by the furthest stretch, the strength of this green dragon didn't seem remotely sufficient to stand against the Nightmare's corruption alongside an unfinished tauren druid.

But then, thinking back not long ago, when he'd gone to Grim Batol alone, how much better off had he'd been than this green dragon?

Wait — for something as critical as the Nightmare, shouldn't he consult the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza?

When leaving Grim Batol, Jace remembered Alexstrasza saying that if he ever encountered a problem he couldn't handle, he could go to her.

Without question, the Dream's corruption was absolutely beyond Jace's ability to handle, and it had already gotten serious enough to frighten a green dragon.

Jace said, "Don't rush, Sola. Once we find Mosa, we'll return to the Eastern Kingdoms, and I'll seek out Queen Alexstrasza for her counsel. Queen Ysera is her sister — she would never stand by and watch the Emerald Dream be eroded."

The green dragon turned her head. Though it was hard for a dragon's head to make proper expressions, Jace could still sense her undisguised surprise.

"You can really go to Queen Alexstrasza for help?" Jace said. "She told me so herself."

The green dragon fell silent, pondering for a moment. "That would be an unimaginably great help, Jace Sesso. I hope that queen will remember that you saved her and be willing to hear my story. Honestly, you're nothing like what I expected. I can't see the kind of… a great hero might have…"

"Confidence?" Jace asked.

"No, I was going to say certain negative traits." Sormarel beat her wings; the howling wind drowned out her words. She waited a moment, then continued. "You must know the Guardian Aegwynn. She was human too. If you know her, you know what I mean."

"I can't compare to her, Sola," Jace shook his head. "She's far too powerful. I'd say I've just been lucky."

In the distance by the frozen lake, the ruins of the Highborne city jutted out from snow slopes and glaciers — crooked towers and enormous temples still standing, their half-buried bodies enduring thousands of years of wind and snow.

Jace carefully studied those ruins under the moonlight, still able to sense their grandeur before destruction. This place reminded him of Eldre'Thalas, minus that daunting outer wall.

Kaithrill had arrived.
