Chapter 847: The Ice Lake of Kaithrill

The green dragon landed in the forest far from the frozen lake. When she touched down, the several people on her back were still asleep.

Jace woke them one by one, sorted through the luggage, and found a place to rest for the time being. Wrensa went out first to scout the surroundings and the situation between here and the Kaldorei ruins, returning before long to the others' side.

"There's nothing alive here," she said.

"So we came for nothing?" Grede said. "I was half expecting a whole demon army waiting for us."

Wrensa rolled the fragment of the Simarlo Crystal between her fingers. "I said there are no living people, Grede. I didn't say there are no dead. You know why so many Quel'dorei mages perished here? Because the ancient Kaldorei spirits still wander these ruins. I could feel them just now — and this is no ghost story."

She glanced over. "This place reminds me of the Thoradin ruins, Jace. The unnatural cold isn't just from the altitude and the weather."

"Looks like this batch of potions is going to come in handy ahead of schedule." Jace pulled out the Amani witch doctor's potion sack and handed it to the dwarf beside him.

Grede frowned. "You really expect me to drink this?"

"Think about what the owner of this hide would do if he saw you outside, Grede. Would he greet you with a big laugh like your Thunderfist brethren?" Jace asked.

"Probably open fire without a word," Grede said, taking the sack. "Then strip me bare, sell everything for coin, and toss my corpse into the wilderness for the dogs… Come to think of it, that does make me feel better."

He lifted the sack and took a swig. His face immediately turned steel-blue, and he couldn't help but mutter, "Tastes like drinking corpse blood. Might actually be dwarf blood, damn it — after all, it's stored in a dwarf's hide."

"You've drunk that stuff before?" Wrensa asked.

"Guessed," Grede said, passing the sack to the elf.

Wrensa tilted the sack and took a small mouthful. She swallowed, paused, then said, "Not bad. This isn't the worst-tasting potion I've ever had."

She passed the sack to Jace. He'd already drunk it once, so he had a fair idea of its peculiar taste. After drinking, he handed it to Sola. "You need to drink this too."

"What does it do?" Sola asked.

"Lets you see ghosts," Grede said.

"Ghosts…" Sola held the sack. "If it's like the spirits of beasts after death, I wouldn't need to drink this to see them."

"That's in the Emerald Dream," Jace said. "In the real world it's hard to say. To be safe, drink up, Sola. No harm in it."

Grede asked, "How long does this stuff take to kick in, Jace? I forget."

"Half an hour." Jace glanced at his pocket watch. "Let's head out now. By the time we reach the ruins, it should be about right."

Grede hefted his shield. "Hope that witch-doctor friend of yours sold you undiluted merchandise." Walking through the winter forest at night, the dense pine branches high up overlapped so thickly one couldn't make out the color of the sky.

Deep in the pine forest it was pitch black. Jace stared hard at every tree shadow, every dark corner, trying to see whether the troll potion had taken effect.

Approaching the forest's edge and returning to the frozen lake, half an hour had long passed. Just as he was starting to suspect the potion had expired during the journey, Wrensa silently pointed toward the distant frozen lake.

Several faint spirits were slowly making their way across the ice, a few hundred meters away — extremely blurry, heading in the direction of Kaithrill.

Jace and the others stopped and crouched low, waiting off to the side. Once everything around them had gone quiet, he could actually hear a faint, haunting song drifting over.

"When you flew over, did you see any ghosts down there, Sola?" Jace asked.

"Of course not," Sola said. "Does that mean the potion worked?"

Jace nodded and turned to Wrensa. "What are they singing? Can you understand it?"

"Only a little," Wrensa said. "The song seems to be anticipating something good. In any case, what they're singing isn't exactly sorrowful."

"They're anticipating entering another world — a dreamlike, beautiful world," Sola said.

"You understand these ancient spirits' language?" Wrensa asked, surprised.

Sola nodded. "I imagine they've been trapped here for thousands of years and have long since suffered enough."

"I don't give a damn about that," Grede said. "All I want to know is what these ghosts have to do with the demons we ran into earlier. Did the demons snatch that fragment — what's-it-called crystal — from them?"

"Very likely," Wrensa said.

Grede asked, "So what's the play — go ask those ghosts where the demons went? They don't look like they're in much of a mood to chat. Should we get the Consecrated Oil ready first?"

"You sure you want to break out Consecrated Oil here?" Wrensa asked. "If they're on friendly enough terms as it is, and they notice our weapons are all enchanted with bane-of-the-undead coatings, won't they either bolt or attack on the spot?"

"Ghost-seeing potion, ghost-killing enchantments…" Sola asked curiously. "Did you know you might run into ghosts before coming here?"

"We'd already heard there were spirits in Winterspring," Jace said. "So we came prepared."

"Let's try asking first," Jace said. "If they're hostile, I'll try hitting them with fire magic first. You use that window to oil your weapons — have that bottle ready."

"All right," Wrensa said.

"So who does the talking — you, Jace?" Grede asked.

"Do they even speak Common?" Jace said. "My Thalassian is still shaky. Forget having a conversation with these ancient spirits."

Wrensa said, "Could Sola go? I don't know what attitude they'd take toward a Quel'dorei like me."

"Then I'll go ask," Sola said.

"Good," Grede said. "If only we'd brought that goblin-invented Necrotic Essence Collector. God knows how much money a ruin this size could've made."

"That machine's a pain to haul around. If we'd actually brought it, you carrying it?" Jace asked.

"We've got a dragon now, Jace," Grede said, pointing at Sola.

"I'm not your pack animal," Sola said, displeased.

Grede said quickly, "Who said you're our pack animal? This is a partnership. If we actually make money, we can cut you in."

"What would I do with money?" Sola asked.

"Uh… like, hire more people to do things for you?" Grede said.

"If it really comes to a fight, remember to siphon these souls, Molophil. My soul shards are almost gone," Jace whispered to the succubus beside him. "The souls of Highborne who've existed for thousands of years definitely have more energy than those centaurs."

"Understood, master," Molophil said.

Wrensa and Grede glanced back at the warlock and his demon. Sola swallowed uncomfortably.

Jace looked at them. "What? Without soul shards, how am I supposed to summon a felhound to trip traps for you?"
