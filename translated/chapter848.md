## Chapter 848: Ancient Spirits

Under the starry sky, the night of the frozen lake shimmered with a ghostly blue hue. Four cloaked figures — three behind, one ahead — walked slowly toward the Kaithrill ruins.

The spirits' hollow singing drifted in and out. Occasionally there came an indistinct warble, impossible to tell whether it was laughter or weeping. Jace lifted his head and gazed at those few swaying pale shapes ahead, blinking hard.

Then he looked at Sola, who walked at the front. He could hardly imagine what kind of reaction these Highborne spirits, who had lingered here for thousands of years, would have on seeing the likes of them — living people.

The closer they walked, the colder the air over the lake became. By now even the sound of wind scraping across the ice had vanished. The silence was absolute. The forms of those ghosts grew ever more distinct. They wore luxurious, faintly glowing translucent thin garments; some seemed to be wearing beautiful metallic crowns upon their heads.

Jace could make out the faint shadows of bones swaying beneath the garments — blurred like an illusion, not something the eye ought to see.

These Highborne had been bound to this world by hatred, terror, and other violent emotions that had gathered too much shadow magic, tethering them to this place, refusing to go where they ought.

Beside him, Grede couldn't help but sniff. The shapes ahead suddenly turned their heads.

The singing, the laughing, the weeping — whatever it was — all congealed at once, leaving only a faint echo carried by some indiscernible medium.

Sola stepped forward and greeted them in the ancient elven tongue. Only then did those spirits seem to relax, as even the frozen air began to flow once more.

The spirits' conversation with Sola came naturally, and they bore no hostility.

Sola had at least told the truth about one thing: she must have encountered the spirits of many different living beings in the Emerald Dream and conversed with them in all sorts of ways. If he had been the one walking up to have a conversation with those ancient ghosts, Jace doubted he could have been this at ease.

But after a while, Sola lowered her head as if a little downcast, and the spirits' expressions betrayed a hint of helplessness. Wrensa frowned. Jace asked curiously, "What are they saying?"

Wrensa hesitated. "Roughly — it seems these ghosts haven't seen any demons."

"Lying ghosts," Grede said almost at once. "They're definitely covering for those demons."

"The demons stole their treasures, Grede." Wrensa shook her head. "These ancient Kaldorei valued their treasures above all. They probably hate the demons more than we do — even as ghosts."

Grede whispered, "Then you read it wrong. That fragment might have nothing to do with any crystal, or else…" At that moment, Sola turned her head. "They seem to know nothing about demons, everyone. Should we show them the fragment?"

The group had originally not intended to show the Simarlo Crystal fragment to these spirits — it might cause misunderstandings — but now it seemed they had no choice if they wanted to make themselves clear.

Jace nodded, and Wrensa took the fragment out of her pack. Grede stood beside her and drew a breath, as if already prepared to fall out with the spirits.

When those Highborne saw the fragment, their expressions indeed changed at once. They looked at one another and spoke in the incomprehensible ancient elven language. Jace frowned and listened carefully, but couldn't make out much.

The Highborne spirits appeared quite agitated and angry. Sola tried to explain something to them. After a while, she turned back. "This is indeed part of the Simarlo Crystal. They want to take it."

"So I didn't read it wrong," Wrensa said.

"No," Sola said.

At that point, one of the Highborne spirits added something. Wrensa asked, "Are they planning to take us to see their leader?"

Sola nodded. "Yes. High arcanists, it sounds like — the rulers of this city in the past. They seem to want to find these fragments again."

"They're dead. How are they supposed to take physical objects?" Grede said.

"My guess is they don't actually know they're dead," Sola said.

Wrensa looked at the spirits. The ghosts looked back at her and whispered among themselves, making her expression sour — but in the end she tossed the fragment to Sola.

With some barely perceptible magic — a telekinesis spell — the spirit snatched the fragment from Sola's hand, letting it float freely in the air, and said something angrily toward Wrensa.

Sola shook her head helplessly. Wrensa merely glared back.

Jace and Grede were both at a loss. Wrensa glanced at Jace and simply said, "Don't take it too hard. I didn't have high expectations for these ancient 'nobles' either. They just thought I was desecrating a sacred object."

Several Highborne spirits slowly turned, conversing among themselves as they walked toward the distant ruins. One of them nodded in their direction. Soma said, "Let's follow."

"I'm curious — it's fine that they don't care about you and Wrensa's race, but they don't care about us two either?" Grede asked.

"You're overestimating what's left of their awareness, dwarf." Sola tapped her own head. "They don't even know whether they're dead or alive. They have no energy left to identify what you are. They're just residual shadows moving on what magic lingers in this place."

"Then counting on them to help us find the demons is a stretch," Jace said.

Sola said, "It is a stretch… but on the other hand, if demons really did try to take the treasures they've been guarding, given their intense craving for magic and artifacts, it should have left a deeper impression."

She sighed. "This is starting to feel like I'm consoling myself. But we don't have many other clues, do we? Perhaps we can find some other traces somewhere in this city."

Grede nodded. "As long as there's a road, let's walk it."

The ancient Kaldorei ruins around Lake Kaithrill were far larger than the game's version — blue and white stone pillars, temples, walls, streets, like the crumbled remains of an ancient Greek city.

Though many of the structures were half-buried in snow, with only portions exposed, those spirits still strolled through them with ease — talking or frolicking among the ruins, as if the magical catastrophe that had destroyed this place had never happened.

Walking along the broken streets, one noticed that the spirits here paid these outsiders who had come here utterly no mind. Only the nearest ghosts occasionally glanced in their direction.

That gaze felt like curiosity, like suspicion — some even carried hostility; some hid behind buildings in fright.

Wrensa and Grede remained carefully on guard against these ghosts. Jace tried to use Detect Demons to survey the entire city, but right now he clearly felt that concentrating to observe demon-shadows had become much more difficult.

For instance, he could sense that Molophil was somewhere in the distance, but he couldn't make out her form clearly. Those drifting spirits occupied too much of his attention.

Could it be that Sil'ya's potion had interfered with his Detect Demons?

In the game, a character who had mastered the ability to detect a certain type of creature could see markers for its tracked targets on the minimap — but activating another type of tracking would overwrite the first detection.

Could there be a similar problem here? If you could see undead, you couldn't see demons? Jace felt a headache coming on. If demons really were hiding here, that would be bad.

The succubus sensed her master's unease. Jace could feel the shift in her emotions through the soul bond, but even she didn't dare approach. Because even though she was invisible, some of the spirits — like sentinels or patrollers — seemed able to detect her presence.

Some ghosts whispered in the direction where Molophil was, discussing whether something strange had appeared. Still, this actually put Jace slightly more at ease.

Since these spirits wouldn't allow any demons near and could sense the threat of an invisible demon, if the one who had stolen this fragment really was a demons, then the high arcanist leaders they were going to see might genuinely know something.
