Chapter 849: The Blurred Boundary

Several spirit nobles led Jace's group to the front of a temple's stairway. It reminded Jace of the grand stone ramp before the Temple of the Moon in Darnassus from the game — and like that ramp, the end of this one was an arched, towering structure.

Many spirits were walking up the steps, singing a melodious, optimistic tune — like some of the ones Jace had heard on the lake earlier. The ghosts had completely ignored the group of them, slowly ascending the ramp toward the heights like pilgrims, as though the temple were the destination of their dream journey.

But Jace didn't feel that the beautiful world these spirits yearned for was anything good at all. So even though the ghosts seemed quite happy, he couldn't shake the feeling that something was off.

"I want to smear on some Consecrated Oil, Jace. Something about these ghosts feels wrong," Grede muttered, hand on the bottle at his waist.

"It's too late for that now, Grede," Jace said. "If they notice our hostility, we'll instantly be surrounded by every spirit here. No saying whether we'd make it out."

"Then I regret coming in," Grede said.

"You don't want to find Mosa anymore?" Wrensa said, looking all around.

Grede glared. "Of course I do…"

"I can sense Mosa…" Sola knitted her brows. She brushed aside a strand of golden hair and looked up, speaking softly. "I don't know why, but I keep feeling Mosa might be close by."

"You mean that for real? You're sure?!" Grede still kept his voice low, but he was so excited he nearly broke voice, drawing disgusted glances from several spirits who were heading up the heights.

"I can't say with absolute certainty," Sola said. "But it really does feel like there's a druid hiding here."

"These spirits are extremely wary of demons," Jace said, looking toward the shadows below the ramp. "Could Mosa be using these ghosts to evade the demons' pursuit? Right now she might be afraid of being caught by the spirits, so she doesn't dare come out to see us."

"Very likely," Sola nodded. "Then let's go in first and explain to their leader that we bear no hostility. We've recovered a fragment of their treasure — perhaps they'll let us search for Mosa here."

"Would they be that well-meaning?" Wrensa asked.

"If they won't let us…" Grede patted the hammer hanging at his waist. "Then I'll teach them a thing or two — show them what a living person of the new age looks like."

"Let's hope it doesn't come to that," Wrensa said. "Thousands of years ago they were arrogant and powerful arcanists. Now they may have weakened considerably, but they've probably gone many times madder."

"I've killed mages before," Grede muttered.

Sola and Wrensa exchanged a glance and each sighed faintly — but they, like Jace, knew Grede was right.

If they really couldn't understand, if they tried to stop these few from finding Mosa, then aside from oiling up and fighting, there was nothing else to be said.

Arriving at the temple entrance, the guarding spirits didn't step forward to question them. They simply stood there staring into the distance, occasionally glancing over, as if they couldn't care less about this group of living people.

The guiding spirits led them into the hall. At once, two of them struck up a conversation with a luxuriously dressed spirit nearby — was that the so-called high arcanist?

After a while, the mage who had been pulling the crystal magic fragment left them and continued deeper inside. The spirits who had stayed behind to chat remained there and never left again.

Jace felt that everything here didn't conform to the logic of the living world. What the spirits thought, what they did — utterly unpredictable. Only the guide holding the crystal fragment seemed to remember what he intended to do.

But looking at that hollow gaze, those swaying footsteps, that lost expression tinged even with a hint of joy, Jace felt he couldn't count on anything either.

The temple hall was tilted somewhat overall and quite plain inside. Perhaps ten thousand years of wind and sand had worn away most of the interior decoration, leaving it as rough as a magnificent bare shell.

Only the shattered statues and elegant stone pillars seemed to be trying to prove that this had once once been an incomparably glorious place.

Walking deeper in, Jace saw the spirits here drawing toward a central altar, which emitted a faint, emerald glow. As the spirits approached, its light brightened slightly.

Several tall, imposing figures stood behind the light, watching the spirits approach. Beside them, a lavishly dressed spirit was speaking to them excitedly — but these figures seemed completely uninterested in his impassionate speech, unwilling to give even the slightest response.

When that spirit saw the guide holding the fragment appear not far away, he stopped his prattling all at once and stared over blankly.

"Simarlo…"

Jace heard the spirit's murmur.

Hearing that name, the tall figures also lifted their heads and looked over.

When the altar's emerald radiance illuminated their faces, Jace noticed those figures weren't spirits — they were a group of elves. Living Kaldorei.

Living Highborne? They did look somewhat different from the Kaldorei in Ashenvale — perhaps ancient nobles who had survived the War of the Ancient or the fall of Kaithrill.

But they wore green tree-patterned robes and carried wooden staves. They were druids? The Highborne nobles had no druids — they even somewhat looked down on these "forest savages."

So why, in a Highborne magic temple, was there a group of druids?

And it appeared that the leader-like spirit was treating them with the utmost deference, completely unbothered by their rude disregard.

Jace looked at Sola beside him, wanting to ask what was going on — but Sola had already gone white-faced, standing there in stunned shock. In the days since he'd known Sola, Jace had never seen this green dragon show such intense emotion — terror!

"Sola…" Wrensa's voice made Sola flinch.

"We're leaving… I need to leave." She murmured.

"Why?" Grede pointed at the Kaldorei figures among the spirits. "Aren't we going over to ask?"

"They're a group of fallen druids. Here… here… no, not…"

She looked around in terror, clutching a nearby stone pillar and stumbling backward, muttering something in an incomprehensible language — draconic? No, Jace didn't think so.

"You cannot leave here anymore, nightmare dragon. You have already stepped onto the road of no return." One of the Kaldorei said this in elven, then waved a hand.

Thick layers of branches drilled out of the stone crevices and wove themselves into a heavy wall, blocking the exit from the inner hall.

Sola's retreat was cut off. She grabbed the branches in terror and pulled at them, shouting, "I don't want to go back. I won't go back into the Dream. Let me out…"

"Nobody dares drag you back, Sola!" Wrensa said, staring at the Kaldorei.

Sola seemed completely unable to hear Wrensa's words. She just trembled and violently tore at the vines — but no matter how she pulled, the vines only grew thicker.

The Highborne spirits in the hall had already been completely drawn to that emerald altar. Here they sang, praising "another world" — that dreamlike world.

Jace recalled Sola's earlier words and suddenly realized it — could this dreamlike world literally mean a dream? Were these druids guiding the Highborne into the Emerald Dream?!

This was a Dream entrance?!

How was that possible! These Highborne were wrathful spirits — shadow, necromancy, maddened arcane energy. Would druids throw such chaotic things into the Emerald Dream?

Everything, everything about this was unbelievably bizarre and wrong.

Jace looked at the several figures in the middle and asked in elven, "You mean Sola has already been corrupted by darkness? We've seen her original form — that was nothing like a nightmare dragon."

"You know what a nightmare dragon looks like, mortal?" A tall night elf druid in the center asked.

He appeared to be a steady, elderly druid — deep-green beard and braids, his green robe billowing like a cloak.

Jace felt uncomfortable from his tone. Though the night elves were all immortal, would they be as arrogant as the high elves, calling outsiders "mortal"?

Another short-haired night elf druid, who seemed like his student or servant, spoke up: "This green dragon has already used the Scythe of Elune's legend here to deceive many innocent druids and other spellcasters. She devours these druids' power, twists their natural energy into her own power, and then grows in a diseased manner. To protect the Dream from being tainted by her fall, Queen Ysera severed her connection to the Dream — who could have expected her to grow only more insane and evil…"

"No." Sola roared upon hearing Ysera's name. "It's that they don't understand what's really happening in the Dream!" Jace said, "The Dream has already been shrouded in darkness. She's not the only one being harmed."

"Don't you understand — you've been deceived by her words," the apprentice-like night elf said with a smile. "You just don't want to admit it. You don't want to wake up."

"What the hell are you all talking about…" Grede asked, bewildered. "Someone translate for me?"

Wrensa looked at Sola, then at the druids before them, her hand slowly reaching for her quiver. "I don't believe you."

"I'll help you peel away her disguise and see the truth," the night elf said, looking to the other side. He spoke to a druid who had been silent all along, who looked much younger. "Shadevelos — reveal her true form."

Shadevelos?

Jace felt this name was somehow awfully familiar.

Hearing the night elves' words, Sola was so frightened she fell to the ground. Jace moved to intervene, but the night elf named Shadevelos easily stepped aside to dodge. From his bosom he took out an emerald gem suffused with a faint moonlight.

"No, no — don't let me see the Dream, don't…" Sola lunged over and grabbed Wrensa nearby. "Please, Lady Wrensa Windrunner — save me!"

Wrensa, completely unprepared, was grabbed by Sola. Her earring swung out, the silver light it emitted startling Sola. She immediately backed away several more steps to the other side of the hall, screaming, "Stop him — stop that fallen one. Don't let him show me the Dream. Please, I beg you!"

Shadevelos smiled slightly. The gem in his hand radiated silver light. Wrensa's earring glowed silver in response. Sola shouted in terror, "Demon! Demon!"

Demon.

Jace suddenly remembered what Shadevelos was.

He was the chief culprit behind the corruption of the Winterspring furbolgs — a quest boss, a satyr lord. He was the maker of Winterspring Firewine, the source of the furbolgs' fall!

Killing him would give furbolg reputation… In his Classic days, he'd killed this boss many times farming the Winterspring saber achievement! "FUEKTE!"

Jace rapidly spoke the Fear incantation. The silver light in the night elf's eyes faded, and the gem in his hand dropped to the ground.

"Are you out of your mind?" he snarled. "So obstinate?!"

But the moment he asked this, an arrow punched through his head and felled him to the ground.

"What in the—!" Grede shouted in surprise. "Little elf, how did you…"

"Arrogant bastard…" Wrensa said through gritted teeth. "Sola, don't be afraid! We'll protect you!"

The Kaldorei groaned and was instantly enveloped in a cloud of red mist. Within that red mist, red mane, goat horns, and black hooves appeared one after another!

Wrensa fired again without hesitation, piercing his back. The red mist gradually dispersed. The Kaldorei on the ground had transformed into a tall satyr, growling and curling up, never to move again.

"Shadevelos can't accomplish much of consequence, my prince," another Kaldorei said, looking toward the tall druid standing at the very center.

"It doesn't matter. They'll soon realize they've chosen the wrong side, Tristan. Such is the sight of mortals," the tall druid said.

Jace chanted a Mage Armor spell, bracing for battle. He watched the glowing patterns envelop his robes and cloak and said to the two beside him: "Kill them all. They're all satyrs!"

"Satyr demons?" Grede raised his battle axe angrily, shook it, and then realized something was wrong. He muttered in surprise, "Why won't the storms and thunder answer my call? Damn it!"

He simply hurled the hammer at the central druid. The moment it struck, the figure melted into a shadow and dispersed. When the dwarf tried to recall the hammer, it didn't respond at all.

"What the hell?!"

Another Kaldorei was pierced by a sharp arrow and likewise vanished like a shadow. Wrensa clutched an arrow and looked at Grede. "I can't commune with the arcane either, dammit… I can't enchant my arrows, Jace!"

Not only the two of them — Jace also watched the Mage Armor he'd just gathered shatter and dissipate before his eyes. Looking around, the hall's surrounding walls had somehow vanished. Gray fog enclosed everything.

They had stepped onto a dark land. Within that fog, tree shadows could faintly be seen.

The spirits, the stone pillars, the altar, the Kaldorei — all had disappeared. Only the three of them stood back to back, alone in the middle of the fog.

"We've been dragged into the Nightmare."

Jace spoke as calmly as he could. For him, this wasn't hard to judge.

"How do you know? How is this possible…" Wrensa asked.

"I just know," Jace said.

"Listen to him," Grede said warily, staring at the shadows in the fog. "His guesses are usually right."

Wrensa observed the surroundings. "Those demons — where are the spirits?"

"What about Molophil? She didn't desert us in the middle of the fight, did she? Damn it!" Grede cursed.

"I can't sense her," Jace said. "She might be blocked outside the Dream. She's not like the satyrs — she's a demon of the Twisting Nether. I'm guessing that's why."

Jace felt his pack. Sazeno was gone too.

Recalling the moment they'd first entered the Kaithrill ruins, his bond with Molophil had grown gradually faint. Could it be that ever since then, the Dream had slowly begun blurring the boundaries of reality, which was why he couldn't identify demons?!

Now he had no ability to judge at all. Everything he'd seen and heard in Kaithrill just now had already become like a dream one struggles to recall after waking — extremely hazy.

"So what do we do now?" Grede asked.

"Dodge…"

A familiar voice drifted from the distance.

"Sola?" Jace turned blankly.

Hearing Sola's voice, the three of them simultaneously turned to look toward where Sola had been standing — but saw nothing.

"You hear that? Quick, dodge!"

A piercing shriek tore through the fog. A colossal, horrifying figure — its entire body rotting — charged out of the mist!

The great dragon dragged tattered skin and muscle behind it. Its spread wings were like two tattered ghost-ship sails. The scales and skin that constantly sloughed off its body were like falling volcanic ash. Its gaping maw was filled with pustules and yellow, rotting fangs…

A nightmare dragon!

"I… Sormarel… please… dodge, quickly!"

The dragon howled in a weeping tone, already brewing a massive mouthful of mist-like blood breath. Jace shouted at the sight: "RUN!"
