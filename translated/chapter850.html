Chapter 850: The Scarlet of the Nightmare

Blood rain spread across the sky. A dark shadow swept through the deep-colored canopy, carrying with it a hysterical, intermittent shriek — or perhaps a howl. Jace grabbed his cloak to shield his face and threw himself to the side with all his strength. He didn't know what being enveloped by this blood fog would do to him. This was the Dream… anything could happen.

In the deep white fog, the dead trees the blood rain swept past transformed from thin, withered shadows into sickly, swollen tumors — then exploded. A terrible, despairing stench filled the nostrils, gradually making every drawn breath an agony.

Jace covered his nose and collapsed into a patch of cold, black muck. Looking back up at the sky, the nightmare dragon thundered past, carrying with it vast clouds of blood drifting further into the distance…

He simply couldn't believe that was Sormarel. The young dragon who had flown so gracefully was completely gone. The corrupted abomination that replaced her was like a colossal skeleton ship sailing through the night.

"Is that really Sola?!"

Grede lifted his head, watching that dark shadow pass overhead once more. Jace raised his arm and pointed toward the indistinct colossal shape.

"Anunigi, Durzha…"

He recited the incantation for Curse of Weakness — but before he could finish, a sharp pain struck the top of his head and his consciousness swam. So it wasn't just the elements or the arcane — even shadow power couldn't be used?!

A wave of overexertion's agony hit him. Jace felt he might pass out on the spot. He opened his potion pack and found all the alchemical potions had lost their energy — like bottles of colorless well water.

A powerful sense of unreality surged into his heart. But how could it be real? This was the Emerald Nightmare!

"Believe me, Wrensa. Believe me, Grede. Believe me… Jace! I never wanted to deceive anyone, much less you. I only wanted to save. I only wanted everything to return to how it was. Why is it so difficult…"

The shadow in the fog rapidly became distinct once more. Jace shouted, "She's coming again — get clear of here! To the left. We can't get separated in the fog!"

The words had barely left his mouth when the dragon's head burst through the white fog. All three threw themselves to the far side and barely dodged the impact…

Sormarel, her nearly dislocated, bone-exposed lower jaw dragging, slammed into the ground and kicked up a great swath of pus-blood-colored muck. Her entire body was like a highly decayed whale carcass. Her raised wings were like strips of flesh lifted from the corpse. She turned her head toward them, eyes weeping blood mixed with foul, dark-yellow pus.

"It was never simple, Sola," Jace murmured, staring at her — as if talking to himself.

He drew Azuresong and found that even Malygos's scale no longer radiated arcane light. The dimmed blue blade, though still beautiful, looked haggard.

"She's about to attack! Quick, dodge!"

Grede roared. The nightmare dragon had steadied herself and suddenly raised her head. A blood-colored dragon claw lunged from behind — the distance and angle of that claw's strike were completely inconsistent with what that posture should have allowed, as if the claw were connected by a chain, raking out a blood-shadow that covered the entire field of vision.

The dwarf suddenly transformed into a colossal Earthen golem. He raised his golden shield and met the blood claw head-on — but failed to halt the dragon's assault as he'd expected. The blood claw simply flashed past. Jace had to scramble backward several steps to avoid being caught.

Wrensa nimbly leaped backward to avoid the scarlet sweep, firing an arrow at the same time. The cold, sharp arrow cut through the mist and struck the flying corrupted dragon — only to pass right through, as if through a shadow, a wisp of smoke, a layer of cloud, flying unimpeded into the unseen distance.

Seeing this, Wrensa, already nocking a second arrow, said in near-desperation: "I can't hurt her! My arrows can't even touch her. She's like a…"

"Shadow," Jace said.

For some reason, Sormarel had already vanished, leaving behind only rotting flesh and slowly settling blood fog.

"This is the real me." A voice came from the fog, now near, now far, causing all three of them to look around simultaneously. "A rotting ghoul. A wicked fallen one. A source of corruption. I'm the one who ought to be destroyed… am I not?"

They all stared at the pitch-black sky but had no idea which direction the voice came from. Sormarel wept and laughed by turns, the chaotic sounds echoing from every direction — but aside from the pervasive white fog, there was nothing to be seen.

The dwarf had already reverted to his original form, standing in front. Jace felt something was off and rushed over immediately. "What is it, Grede? What just happened?"

Grede swallowed hard, clenched his hand uncomfortably, and lowered his head to look at his arm. A purplish-red hue had begun to spread.

It wasn't just there. On his neck, on every place not blocked by armor and cloak, there were traces of decay-like bloodstains.

"Damn it. Bad luck. How the hell was I supposed to know that dragon couldn't even be hit?" Grede said, his voice trembling. "Is this corruption? Am I going to turn into one of those trees?"

Grede clenched his fist. His arm suddenly became like metal — but that layer of nightmare corruption wasn't dispelled. Instead it turned into a mass like black charcoal, still slowly spreading.

When the metal returned to flesh, the purplish-red corruption remained. "Damn it. What do I do?" the dwarf asked.

Wrensa noticed the necklace around Grede's neck had stopped glowing. "Your necklace…"

"It doesn't work here," Grede said, lifting his chain. "Better this way. At least it won't attract a few more black dragons."

"Stop dwelling on that," Jace said.

"I thought you were still complaining about me wrecking the airship," Grede said with a laugh.

"Nobody cares," Jace said.

"Damn. Mine too," Wrensa said.

Jace noticed the earring she clutched had completely lost its green luster, turned into a dull, grayish-white stone. And the Chain of Will?

Jace thought of the chain he wore. When he reached to touch it, he suddenly felt as if he'd been burned.

Here in the Dream, the Chain of Will had completely shed its outer appearance of an ordinary black-iron trinket. It had grown red and searing hot, like a heated branding iron. His neck had even been blistered by the heat — but the skin there felt no distinct pain.

This was the Dream… the Nightmare. Nothing here could be connected to the logic of the real world.

"What about Grede — what do we do, Jace?" Wrensa asked.

"First we need to find a way out of this dream," Jace said, looking at Grede's arm.

"How do we get out?" Grede finished asking and slapped himself across the face. "That doesn't wake me up either."

"At least we can't stay here," Wrensa said. "We can't fight Sola at all. She's completely intangible."

"Where?" Grede looked at the tree shadows in the fog. "Charge into that mist?"

Watching the purplish-red corruption on his arm grow larger, he finished with a self-deprecating, bitter laugh. Jace took off the Chain of Will and hung it around Grede's neck. "Try this."

"Why's it so hot—"

Grede winced and grimaced for a moment, but after putting it on, he immediately noticed the traces on his body had stopped spreading — even receding slightly.

"It works, Jace!" Wrensa said excitedly.

Grede couldn't help but laugh. Jace breathed a sigh of relief. "Then let's keep it that way."

The dwarf, as if suddenly realizing something, looked up. "Then what about you?!"

"You're not as dumb as you look," Jace said, standing — only to hear a murmuring from afar. In the pitch-black forest, ghostly figures had appeared, one after another, from who knew when. "It was you who destroyed our dream…"

Those figures shouted in unintelligible elven, step by step drawing nearer. When they walked out of the fog, Jace finally saw their appearance. These things were all pale-red deformed monsters, hobbling like some kind of withered, fallen swamp beast — but the "vines" covering their bodies were woven from scarlet, blood-vessel- or nerve-like tissue.

And yet they shouted in elven, wailing like ghosts. "This isn't the place we wanted to go!"

"You lied to us!"

"We're going to be trapped here forever."

"Nourishment for the dream…" The several of them followed the sounds around them and looked back. In the forest on the other side, these creatures had also begun to crawl out.

It wasn't hard to guess — they were all the spirits who had wandered Kaithrill and walked into that so-called altar, gaining some kind of new body in the Nightmare…

"How the hell do we fight these things…" Grede raised the axe in his hand. "Are they intangible too — can't be hit?"

Wrensa resolutely fired an arrow at the nearest hobbling blood-creature. The arrow struck the terrifying blood-filled eyeball on its neck. The blood-creature immediately shrieked and collapsed to the ground.

"No," Wrensa said.

"Hah!" Grede laughed. "So finally we've got some living targets." Jace raised Azuresong. "Then we fight our way out."

"Don't know what we're fighting toward," the ranger said, drawing another arrow.

"Wherever we get to," the dwarf raised his axe.

As Wrensa's second arrow flew, a tall scarlet abomination toppled with a cry. Jace raised Azuresong and drove it into a creature of indescribable appearance that had crawled up on several tentacles, twitching — cleanly severing what seemed to be its spinal tissue and cutting short its mad attack.

Grede awakened his Avatar and charged into the horde like a spinning windmill, hacking wildly. Every swing felled a great swath of monsters — but Jace could still notice that his Avatar was no longer as glorious and powerful as it had been in Dustwallow Marsh. It must have been the Nightmare preventing Neltharion's necklace from taking effect.

When the horde of hobbling creatures pressed to within arm's reach, Wrensa also had to draw her two fighting blades and join the slaughter. That golden Quel'Thalas dagger of hers carried its own blazing flame. When it struck those monsters, it quickly set them alight, burning out a nauseating stench that drew sharp shrieks from the creatures.

Jace pierced the eyeball of a hobbling monster that had collapsed on top of him, withdrew his sword, and dodged the dark-yellow pus.

He instinctively wanted to call on the fire elementals for help — but in the Emerald Dream… there seemed to be no fire, or wind elements at all. He couldn't hear any voice of the elements. Not even those unintelligible whispers.

In the dead air, only the shadow howled.

Seeming to sense Grede's terrifying killing power, more and more hobbling creatures emerged from the dense fog, surrounding him. Grede's roars were gradually drowned out by howls of every kind. Jace could even hear some creatures shouting "For the Light of Lights."

They had become nourishment for the Nightmare, transformed into these wretched, twisted monsters — and still remembered the empress they had seen ten thousand years ago before they died?

Some of the tall hobblers took gaunt black steps, raised their arms full of red tentacles, and pointed toward Grede. Their maws full of white, fanged teeth chanted incantations he couldn't understand. Sinister magical energy pulled at Grede's movements — seemingly some kind of Life Drain-like magic, but more abstract, more insidious…

Grede struggled and hurled his battle hammer at the creature casting the spell. The hammer flew out, smashed its mouth and neck, and toppled to the ground, howling and rolling — a struggling mass of shadow and blood-flesh.

Jace realized that whether it was Grede calling on the elements or Wrensa enchanting her arrows with arcane magic, both required mana.

Once their mana was exhausted, they would still find it taxing to wield these powers. But just now, when the two had tried to cast and failed, there had been no sign of overexertion — only anger and shock.

Why was it only when he used shadow magic that he felt taxed? Trying to cast Curse of Weakness, he'd felt woozy and nearly collapsed on the spot.

Was it possible that shadow magic could still be cast — he just didn't have enough mana… but the mana potions were all used up. Ruuhalavektar, Ru'alakiptar…

He silently recited this all-too-familiar, dangerous incantation he hadn't used in a long time. Life Tap.

For an instant, it felt as if something in his mind had been yanked taut. A gloomy, cold power poured from his brain into his heart. Everything around him connected to the Nightmare's corruption — whether severed limbs, monsters, or that fog — all at once grew powerless.

The transaction with the shadow was complete. Mana surged once more. Jace looked at his pale hands and said, almost to himself: "About right."

Another tall, gaunt hobbler charged at Grede. Wrensa fired an arrow that grazed its eye and struck into its neck. Jace reached out a hand — and a thin red line immediately seized its movements!

Blood Grasp!

Jace felt a mad urge to kill suddenly surge into his heart. Countless tiny, low, twisted monsters around him were instantly shredded by the Blood Grasp. Severed limbs and blood fog flew everywhere.

Wrensa's gaze turned this way, nearly frightened by the scene. Grede, sensing Jace's magic, shouted: "This guy's basically come home!"

He was right. This was the Emerald Nightmare… shadow was everywhere!

Several tall hobbling monsters that hadn't been shredded were dragged by Jace to his side. Before they could steady themselves, Wrensa cut them down like a sudden storm. The remaining tall hobblers were either blown apart by Jace's Shadow Bolts or cleanly shot through the eye by Wrensa, madly charging into the other monsters. Under the three of them fighting side by side, the encircling horde could clearly no longer hold…

Grede, axe in hand, had already carved out a path. He roared and pointed in a direction in the distance. "Let's get out of here — you two, follow!"

Jace looked at the dwarf, wanting to respond — but amid the blood-red horde, he saw Grede's expression toward him shift from excitement to shock in an instant, then to rage.

"Behind you, Jace!"

Jace unhesitatingly released a Dark Shield — then felt all sound yanked into silence by the sharp sensation of something piercing the magical barrier…

He turned his head and saw a scarlet satyr appear before him. Red fur all over, dark-gray pectorals, gray-white fangs, curved demon horns… blood-red eyeballs staring straight at him!

He smiled coldly, gripping a branch dagger that he drove straight into the black film. The Dark Shield shattered with a sound — but the shockwave of shadow that dispersed sent countless blood-limbs and corpses flying, yet failed to stop the satyr's thrust!

The demon laughed triumphantly. His scarlet fur was blown open by the shockwave, yet he still forced the dagger through every obstruction of power and plunged it into Jace's chest.

Jace only felt a tightness in his chest, followed by a torrent of scalding heat overflowing from the center.

The satyr's originally triumphant expression vanished entirely. He threw Jace to the ground and backed away step by step. Only after a while did he mutter in demonic: "What is this?"

The branch dagger in the satyr's hand had burst into brilliant flame. That flame, amid the white fog, the darkness, and the blood-red, was so fervent — like sunlight bursting out of the night. It frightened the satyr into throwing the dagger away directly. But even his hand had caught fire.

The fervor of life and the corruption of the Nightmare collided fiercely. Alexstrasza…

The demon muttered this name, clenched his fist, and extinguished the flame. "Jace Sesso! You son of a—!"

Grede's roar yanked Jace out of his weak haze. The dwarf, furious, swung his axe and lopped the head off a hobbling monster blocking his way, stepped on its corpse, and closed in rapidly. Noticing the berserk dwarf, the satyr immediately melted into the shadows — only to be struck out of concealment almost in the same instant by an arrow flying in from afar.

The satyr pulled out the arrow and let out a furious roar. He turned and pointed at the pursuing Grede, chanting some incantation — but to his surprise, it had no effect whatsoever. Instead, an axe hacked off one of his arms.

The Chain of Will around the dwarf's neck flickered. The satyr looked at it in disbelief — then took another axe blow hard across the belly.

The satyr clutched his abdomen and stumbled back several steps. Just as Grede was about to pursue and chop again, an even taller shadow appeared behind him and seized him. This was a satyr covered in blood-red demonic runes, its long black horns towering high, clad in black leather armor, a blood-colored nightmare hook in hand…

"Prince, save me."

The hacked satyr roared in panic — but the tall satyr gave no response.

He wielded two blood-red rune curved blades and slashed toward Grede. Grede, relying on his silver body, forcibly withstood two blows — cracks appearing on his form — then hacked into the satyr's shoulder with his axe. He hadn't expected the other party to be utterly unperturbed either, fiercely butting Grede to the ground with his horns.

"Get down here and help, you stubborn beast!"

The satyr demon called a prince roared in demonic: "Appear and protect your master!"

A howl came from the sky — that was Sola's voice. Jace closed his eyes in agony. A pain enough to make one cry out uncontrollably struck his chest.

He bit his lip, tried to push himself up — but found his limbs strengthless.

Shadows coiled around the demon prince. Grede relied on his shield to block his attacks again and again. At that moment, a hobbling creature suddenly wrapped its tentacles around Jace's neck — only to be split in half by a sharp blade from the middle.

Wrensa appeared. She bent down and caught Jace by the neck, her voice trembling. "How are you… Jace…"

"It's just a dream, Wen," Jace said with all his strength. "Help Grede… help Grede! I'm fine!"

Wrensa nodded, looked into the distance — her gaze resolute, filled with hatred. Jace noticed the corrosion had already crawled up her pale neck and was spreading at a speed visible to the naked eye.

Jace roared and sat up. He watched the blood in his chest, burning and even giving off open flame and thick smoke, gush out another stream — but this time he gripped Azuresong and slowly raised it.

Behind him came a saliva-like slithering sound — the approach of more filthy blood-creatures. He turned and swung his sword, cleanly slicing a crawling brainless monster in half.

Straining to watch the distant dwarf, Wrensa had already joined the fight. One tall and one short, two satyrs were locked in combat with the elf and the earthen warrior. Even as he watched, Wrensa hurled that burning dagger and cleanly pierced the heart of the shorter satyr. A shriek nearly piercing Jace's eardrums poured into his brain. He closed his eyes in pain and smiled.

This entire Nightmare was probably propped up by these two satyrs. They were corrupting the Emerald Dream here. As long as they could kill them… they could break free of this place.

Perhaps.

Just then, a hollow weeping echoed from the sky once more.

"Forgive me, Jace… forgive me, my friends."

A dragon's shadow that blotted out the sky swept over the dead forest, pushing aside the white fog — and with it came a vast deluge of blood fog… Jace had no strength left to drench. He bathed in the blood fog, watching the red engulf the three locked in combat before his eyes.

The demon prince's form grew more and more bloated — now appearing nearly three meters tall, his body enormously swollen and enlarged. The long blades in his hands now looked like two small daggers. Grede and Wrensa immediately appeared small and frail before him.

Even so, the two of them fought more and more madly. Their bodies were already soaked through with blood-red — but this didn't slow their combat power at all. Instead it made the demon increasingly hard-pressed.

"Sormarel! Quick — help your master!"

The demon cried out in fear. Grede couldn't help but let out a laugh. As the satyr parried Wrensa's thrust, he leaped up and chopped off one of his horns with his axe, felling him to the ground entirely. Then he hacked another blow at the demon's head. The demon struggled to get up — only to have Wrensa drive a blade precisely into his heart.

Even so, this raging demon prince still struggled to his feet, turning into a shadow that flashed a great distance away — but soon reappeared, collapsing to the ground once more.

His heart gushed demon blood. The blood-light in his eyes rapidly dimmed. In the end he could only cry out in pain, "Father… Lord Xavius…" and slump to the ground with a heavy thud.

The demon was dead. Wrensa and Grede nearly collapsed to the ground at the same time — but the dispersal of the Nightmare they'd been hoping for didn't happen.

Those hobbling creatures were still slowly approaching. Jace stared at them blankly, listening to the sound of great wings flapping drawing closer in the sky.

The Nightmare's foul stench filled his nostrils. He had never imagined a place could simultaneously hold maddening, sweltering heat and terrifying, bone-chilling cold — and here, he felt it. Truly.

"Never thought I'd die in a dream," Wrensa's voice came faint and distant.

"This counts as dying on the battlefield, little elf," Grede said. "We…"

A rumbling great sound drowned out the dwarf's voice. Jace turned his head and saw the nightmare dragon's shadow emerge from the dense fog. She again held a mouthful of breath and charged head-on.

Jace wiped a handful of burning blood and smeared it on Azuresong. The blue blade suddenly erupted in wild life-flame, streaming down full, bright-red, scalding blood.

He raised his head and charged to meet it. When that searing life-flame met the shadow of the Nightmare, there suddenly burst forth a sound of flesh tearing. In an instant he was submerged in corrupted blood.

Pushing aside the rotting flesh and sticky blood before him, he barely stood amid the stench and filth and looked back. The dragon had crashed to the ground. A vast stretch of dead forest had been flattened. The blood fog had also spread, pervading everything, replacing the white fog.

She could no longer fly. Her chest had been torn open by Azuresong into a massive gash. The black heart, overgrown with purplish-red vines, was exposed to the air. Her giant wings were like insects with their bodies crushed — spread out, stiff and dead.

Sormarel's giant mouth vomited a mass of internal organs. Her lips trembled slightly.

"Kill me, Jace Sesso — or you'll all become monsters like me."

Her voice suddenly became incredibly real, as if they were back in that night sky — that quiet green dragon.

There was no choice. Wrensa and Grede had both been submerged beneath the blood fog, leaving only gradually weakening shouts.

Jace… had to end this Nightmare.

Jace raised the burning Azuresong and walked step by step toward the exposed chest. At that moment, he noticed his arm had already become no different from the limbs of those hobbling creatures. The mad whispers grew louder — like countless people surrounding him, shouting, persuading, seducing…

You want to kill your companions, Jace Sesso?

She never had any malice or wicked intent from start to finish. She was just an innocent child…

Your hands are stained with blood. Do you want to use your dark heart to judge a guardian of the forest and the Dream? She is far nobler than you… do you have the qualification?

"Do it." The dragon tilted her head, speaking softly. "Let me stop living in fear."

Jace felt his heart seem to skip half a beat. He nodded and raised his sword, plunging it into the dragon's heart. The life-flame instantly consumed that purple demon-heart. The entire world burned with it.

Jace weakly backed several steps, watching the fog in the sky gradually part, revealing azure blue…

A chill seeped in. Jace snapped his eyes open. Before him was a pitch-black hall, empty of everything. Outside drifted snow. Moonlight and snowflakes floated in through the cracks in the ruined building — quiet as an illusion.

A snowflake landed on his neck, cold enough to make him shiver. That was probably what had woken him.

He sat for quite a while before recognizing this was the Kaithrill temple. But there were no spirits here at all. The magical emerald-glowing object on the central altar was also gone.

"You're awake, master." Molophil appeared from a distance.

"What happened? Where are the spirits?" Jace asked.

"I killed them all," Molophil said, tossing down a bag of soul shards. "I know you don't want trouble — but they insisted on fighting."

"To be fair, Sazeno and I killed them," the imp crawled out from behind the altar. "My contribution was practically—"

"Nonexistent," Molophil said coldly.

"Pfft! What nonsense are you spouting… I'm much more powerful than before, I…"

Jace took a deep breath, ignoring the bickering of the two demons. Though he'd just woken from a deep Nightmare, he still felt utterly drained, almost wanting to fall back asleep.

He suddenly remembered something and touched his chest — then felt a hideous scar. So the satyr's stab had still left its mark.

But those blood-red nightmare corruptions had all vanished. His skin on his arms looked fine. "Jace… Jace… hurry and save Jace, Grede, he's in—"

Wrensa's voice came from beside him.

The two of them were both collapsed at his side, still covered by cloaks. Their skin looked no different either — they just seemed a bit cold.

Wrensa lay there with a frown, her lips still trembling, as if the corrosion had never existed. Grede lay beside her. Strangely enough, the Chain of Will was really around his neck.

"How'd it end up on him?" Jace asked.

"You put it on him, master," Sazeno said. "You forgot all about it? Ah, that's only normal — you were asleep… that was basically sleepwalking!"

Jace stared blankly, then went to take the cold iron chain off — but Grede suddenly grabbed his wrist, mumbled something with his eyes closed, and shook his head.

Jace yanked it back and put it around his own neck, thinking the dwarf had gotten addicted to it. Just then, his peripheral vision caught Sola, sitting at the hall's entrance.

Her lips were pale. Eyes closed, she looked as if asleep — no longer the Sormarel who had been covered in terror, struggling to escape.

Jace drew Azuresong. Arcane blue light instantly illuminated everything around.

"I always thought I could tell the difference between dream and reality." A familiar voice came from the other side.

"What?"

Jace looked at Sola — and to his surprise, she had opened her eyes.

Warm moonlight seeped between her eyelashes. The green dragon lowered her head. "So — what is this? Another trap?"

"You're the one who brought us here, Sola. I don't think you're in much of a position to call this our trap."

Even as he said this, Jace suddenly felt as if a weight had lifted. He sat to the side and let out a long breath. Sola stared at Jace for a long time, then raised her head and looked toward the top of the hall.

"I… no, I thought…"

She touched her chest, then asked: "Is the Nightmare over?"

"You'd know better than me," Jace said.

"Sola?" At that moment, Wrensa tried to stand, propping herself against the wall, her arms still a little weak. She looked over in surprise. "You're all right?"

"At least say 'thank you' — that she-dragon nearly killed us all," Grede muttered, one eye half-closed, head tilted.

"Thank you." Sola closed her eyes. Icy-silver tears streamed down her pale face.

"Thank you."
