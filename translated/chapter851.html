Chapter 851: Sleep and Entering the Dream

The snow over Kesselir was falling harder, and the group sat in silence. After the chaos of the nightmare, it was hard to say whether their exhaustion was physical or mental — but regardless, even though they had just woken from the dream, they were still so badly in need of sleep they could have dropped off on the spot.

The reason none of them wanted to close their eyes, Jace suspected, was the same for all of them: they were afraid of falling back into the dream.

Grede looked this way and that, got up to touch the walls and floor, still looking like he had not quite figured out what had happened.

Not long before, Vereesa had even tried conjuring a Light cantrip on her fingertip, effortless as anything. Jace knew she had been testing whether she could still cast arcane magic at all after waking.

And they were not the only ones with questions — he had plenty of his own.

It was just that Sola had not been in great shape for a while after they woke. She had looked dazed and pale the whole time, so Jace had not wanted to press her.

Now that she had eaten something and seemed a little better, he tried asking, "How are you doing, Sola?"

"Better, thank you for asking, Jace Sesso," Sola nodded.

When Vereesa saw Sola speak up, she could not help asking too: "Maybe to you all of this makes perfect sense, Sola, but to us it is genuinely hard to understand. I know we seem to have won, but what exactly were you—"

Sola nodded. "I thought I had escaped the Nightmare. But really, I was only pretending I had left."

She looked at Vereesa. "I have to apologize to you. I deceived you, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. Back at the owlbeast cave, I told you Queen Ysera's pendant did not work — but the truth was I was too afraid to set foot in the Dream's plane again. I was scared—"

"Of turning into that monster?" Grede asked.

"At that time..." Sola lowered her head and said softly, "I did not know exactly what would happen. Now I know. You killed that Nightmare-corrupted version of me inside the dream, and I feel like a weight has been lifted from my shoulders. Because I never could have broken free on my own — that thing was practically the deepest layer of my soul."

Jace said, "There is one thing I do not quite get. I remember you saying mortals cannot enter the Emerald Dream."

He said it while nibbling on some sweet dried cheese from his pack. The moment Grede heard him, he cut in: "What do you mean? Did not we just go in?"

"There are two ways to enter the Dream: through sleep, and by truly walking within it," Sola tried to explain. "The way you just touched the Nightmare was no different from any other mortal — you were dreaming."

At that, she thought a moment more before continuing: "Actually, it is not as though mortals have no chance of truly stepping into the Emerald Dream at all. But it takes a powerful Dreamwalker to open the way for you — Cenarius and his sons, for instance, or Queen Ysera and her closest guardians, or the most ancient and gifted of the druids."

"They have to expend an enormous amount of natural energy to open the Dream Gate. Only through that kind of entrance can other races truly visit the Emerald Dream in the flesh. I do not know how these satyrs managed it... but I am hardly surprised. They have been trying to damage the Dream for a long time now."

Vereesa picked up the stone the druid had lifted earlier. Compared to when the satyr had been holding it, the stone had lost every trace of magical luster.

"So we were put to sleep by this thing and dreamed a dream?" she asked. Sola glanced at the stone, her eyes still seeming to flinch slightly.

"Yes. That is really just a dream jade capable of mass hypnosis. Its power is nowhere near enough to open a Dream Gate, but..."

Here, she hesitated.

"But this stone let you see what you looked like inside the Dream," Jace said.

"Yes," Sola said. "It dragged that thing back into my consciousness. In the end, I never truly escaped."

Grede asked curiously, "If we had been killed while dreaming, what would have happened? Would we have woken up like you, Sola?" Sola fell silent for a while. Jace guessed a green dragon who could travel freely through the Dream's plane could not possibly be like mortals in that regard.

The four corrupted green dragons had not truly perished when they were killed before the Dream Gate — and Ysondre had even woken up and thrown off the corruption for a time. Of course, since the green dragons had never managed to root out the Nightmare's taint, Ysondre had eventually fallen again.

After a moment, Sola said, "All I can say is that the Nightmare would have turned your minds and spirits into servants of the Nightmare, like those blood-red monsters."

"What about our bodies here, then?" Vereesa asked.

"I do not know," Sola shook her head.

Everyone fell silent for a while. Whatever the answer, it probably was not good.

"I have another question," Jace said. "When we entered Kesselir, I felt my connection with Molofier being interfered with by something. Was that the Dream's power at work?"

Sola looked at Molofier and tried to explain: "Demons are the same as you — they could never enter the Emerald Dream on their own. Compared to mortals, they are not even... they were never part of Azeroth to begin with. They do not dream. The satyr demons were all Kaldorei once, transformed. They only managed to enter because they mastered a special method — especially after so many druids betrayed us and threw in their lot with them."

Jace said, "My point is not whether we can enter the Dream, Sola. It is that the link between me and Molofier was being interfered with by something. I think it was the Dream's influence, because there is no other explanation. After all, I did not see any demon-interfering magic circles or artifacts in Kesselir."

"That blocked feeling I had with my demon minions inside the dream — it was like an intensified version of the interference I felt the moment we entered Kesselir. But the key thing is, when we first entered Kesselir, we had not been hypnotized by that dream jade yet! Which means the Dream power in Kesselir did not come from the stone Vereesa is holding."

After hearing Jace, Sola thought it over, and the other two unconsciously stopped what they were doing.

"So beyond the stone hypnotizing us, there is something else here connected to generating dreams, is there not? Whatever it is, it strengthened our connection to the Dream." Jace touched his chest. "Like the satyr's stab wound, and the thing that moved from me to Grede..."

Here, Jace hesitated over whether he should tell Sola about the Chain of Will. In the end, he swallowed the second half of the sentence. "If it were just a normal dream, there would not be these physical effects, right?"

As he spoke, his tone grew impatient despite himself. Because he kept feeling that there was something else, something deeply wrong, that had happened just before he was dragged into the dream — but the endless cycle of sleeping, dreaming, waking, and remembering had left him unable to recall it for the moment.

It was right there...

He tapped his temple lightly as he thought. Vereesa caught his arm and rubbed it gently with her fingers. "I know what happened in the dream was terrifying, Jace, but Sola just went through something horrible too. Give her a little space."

Jace looked at her and smiled. "Do not worry too much about me. I am fine now. I just feel like... we are missing something, Vere."

"Actually, there are things I do not understand either," Vereesa nodded, looking at Sola. "For instance — can ghosts even dream? I mean, those horrible flesh monsters in the Nightmare... they all seemed to be the spirits of Kesselir transformed. Even now I can still hear their cries in my head, and even though they looked like that... they all still seemed loyal to Azshara, loyal to the ancient Kaldorei Empire. They still thought of themselves as Kaldorei nobles."

Yes, yes — Jace felt like Vereesa's words had jogged something loose. Not far off, Sola's lips moved, but she found she could not explain every detail of what had just happened either.

Grede smacked his forehead. "Oh, right — how did those spirits get into the Dream in the first place? They cannot dream, and there is no way some green dragon or... deer-man, druid, whatever, would have let them in."

Yes. Spirits. Spirits entering the Dream.

Jace fixed his eyes on the dim altar in the center. Yes — this was the very thing that had been radiating that emerald glow charged with Dream power. It all clicked into place for him at once.

"This — this is it. I remember those spirits surrounding it!" He walked up to the altar as he spoke.

The others gathered around too. Sola closed her eyes and sensed the altar's power. After a moment, Grede coughed uncomfortably and whispered, "She is not... going to turn into that again, is she?"

"I doubt it..." Jace whispered back.

"Druid." Sola opened her eyes. "Remember before we got here, I said I sensed a druid? They are connected to this altar!"

"You mean the ones the satyrs were disguised as?" Grede asked.

Sola turned around, excited. "So the druids I sensed back then were not satyrs at all — and besides, those satyrs were already killed inside the Dream by you, Grede Thunderfist! That means there are still other druids nearby. Gods, I never imagined this altar was being powered by real druids..."

"Mossa?!" the dwarf nearly jumped out of his skin, then froze. "Wait — where are these druids?"

The empty hall was bare in every direction. Were they supposed to search all of Kesselir?

Even exhausted as he was, Jace could feel the spirits of Kesselir still wandering outside — the unnatural cold that exceeded even the rest of Winterspring said as much.

The green dragon placed her hands on the altar again. Jace tried to sense the altar's residual power too, but Sola kept her eyes closed and said, "Stay back... Jace, your energy will interfere with my senses."

Jace nodded and stepped aside. After a moment, Sola opened her eyes again. "Below us."
