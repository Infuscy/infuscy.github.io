Chapter 852: The Calamity of Winterspring

"Below?" Several pairs of eyes turned to the floor. Jace asked, "Some kind of basement?"

It would not be at all surprising for an ancient elven city to have an underground palace. Grede immediately ran off to feel around the walls, as if hunting for a hidden passage.

"Over here!" Vereesa called from the far end of the hall — no one had noticed her get there. "I can feel magical energy this way!"

The group followed the ranger to the other side of the chamber, but it was nothing more than an ordinary wall, indistinguishable from every other surface in the hall.

"You sure?" Grede asked.

"Remember Eldre'Thalas?" Vereesa said. "I can sense the signature of the sealing magic these ancient Kaldorei cast. I am certain — there should be an illusion here..."

As she spoke, she uttered an arcane dispel incantation at the wall. The rough, solid stone before Jace's eyes immediately began to shimmer and fade, revealing the entrance to an ancient corridor.

Molofier went in first. She looked around and said, "The demon we ran into in the owlbeast cave came through here too, Master."

Hearing that, the expressions on several faces soured. "You are sure he is not still in there?" Jace asked.

"He should not be," Molofier said. "Did you run into that demon while you were inside the Azeroth's Dream?"

"I could not make out any demon features inside that dream," Jace shook his head. "I do not know which of those satyrs had been to the owlbeast cave. Maybe none of them — maybe it was someone else."

That was when Jace recalled the name the demon prince had shouted with his dying breath, back in the Nightmare. Xavius.

A chill ran down his spine.

Had Xavius summoned that felguard? And why had he fled?

"Whoever it is, they are not going to stop me from finding Mossa Mothmane."

Grede marched in with his shield raised. Vereesa caught Jace's eye and followed him into the corridor. Sola licked her lips, drew a breath, and went in after them.

Sure enough, it was an ancient elven underground palace. The extinguished braziers lining the corridor gave off a foul reek of shadow and necrotic death that reminded Jace of the demon prison they had entered in Eldre'Thalas.

Of course, this place was not on that scale. Following the long spiraling staircase down, tracking Sola's sense of the druid's presence, they eventually reached a lightless cavern directly beneath the altar.

Vereesa already had an arrow nocked. Melofier had melted into the shadows. Jace tried to Sense Demons toward the cavern's interior — and saw nothing but chaos and void.

But given that the Dream power here had fouled his demon detection before, he could not be sure there were no enemies inside either. "Give us some light, Jace," Grede said, raising his shield to his chest.

Jace nodded and drew Skysong. Blue radiance flooded the cavern in an instant.

Lying among the stones were several sleeping druids — and that tauren among them... was Mossa Mothmane!

"Mossa!"

Grede lunged forward and threw his arms around the tauren, shaking her vigorously. "You are actually here!"

Then he started muttering a stream of what sounded almost indistinguishable from Dwarvish but was presumably Tauren. Sola said, "That will not wake her, Grede. Stop shaking her. Let me."

Grede nodded and moved aside. Sola leaned close to the tauren's ear and said something softly. The tauren immediately broke into a fit of violent coughing.

She was enormous — each cough made the whole cavern rumble — before she finally spoke: "Solamiel..."

The tauren kept murmuring the name. Sola immediately took her arm and said in Thalassian, "I am here, Mossa. I am sorry... I..."

"I heard your voice, Lady. You are safe, you are safe."

Mossa turned her head and caught sight of the dwarf. Her small eyes went wide.

She babbled something dazed in Tauren, then switched to Common: "The Earth Mother has opened her eyes... what I mean is, what are you doing here, Grede? Or am I still... not awake?"

Her Common was still halting and broken, but she could mostly form sentences now.

"This is not a dream, Mossa! It is really me."

Mossa looked as if she could not believe Grede was the one she was seeing upon opening her eyes — but when she raised her head and spotted Vereesa and Jace behind him, her shock deepened.

"If I told you we came all the way here specifically to rescue you, would you believe me, Mossa?" Jace asked with a smile.

Mossa coughed hard a few more times and hauled herself off the stone slab. She was so massively built that she bowled Grede aside the moment she sat up, then lovingly helped the dwarf back to his feet.

"Of course I believe you! I have missed you so much, Jace, Vereesa..." She looked at the two of them, then pulled the dwarf into her arms again. "Especially you, my Thunderfist. I missed you... so much it hurt. I thought I would never see you again."

"I missed you too, Mossa. When we heard something might have happened to you... the rest of us could barely eat."

As Vereesa spoke, her eyes reddened. Jace let out a slow breath — after everything this journey had thrown at them, at least this one thing had turned out right.

Sazeno was dabbing his eyes and sobbing. Jace shoved him straight back into the pack. The imp struggled and wailed, "Let me be part of this beautiful moment, Master! Ow..." then went silent.

Molofier tilted her head and said softly, "If I said I was moved too, Master, would you believe me?"

"No," Jace said.

Molofier flashed a quick smile, then fixed her gaze on the tauren ahead again. She glanced at the other sleeping Kaldorei and cocked her head, as if braced to counter them leaping up and attacking at any moment.

The two unfamiliar druids were sleeping deeply. Grede noticed them too and could not help asking, "Are these two satyrs as well?"

"No," Mossa explained. "They are Kaldorei, like me. The satyrs... fabricated the lie of the Scythe of Elune. They deceived ambitious druids into coming to Winterspring searching for clues, and then..."

Mossa seemed to be running out of Common words and had to continue in Thalassian.

"They tricked druids into coming here, then used their dreams — channeled through the Kesselir arcanists' teleportation array — to tear open a small, unstable Dream Gate. They used it to feed the dark spirits here into the Nightmare, giving it sustenance and accelerating the Nightmare's corruption of the entire Emerald Dream."

Vereesa translated for Grede as Mossa spoke, and Jace more or less pieced together what had really been going on this whole time.

The satyrs used rumors to lure druids in, then trapped them with snares and brought them underground beneath the Kesselir ruins, harnessing their slumber as an energy source for a Dream rift.

The rift was not powerful enough to let ordinary mortals enter the Dream in the flesh, but the Dream energy it radiated was more than enough to affect the surrounding world — much like the Dream Gates in Ashenvale, the Hinterlands, and Duskwood. Of course, these effects were far weaker than a true Dream Gate.

Sola had believed the rumors the satyrs had spread and passed the clue to Mossa, wanting to find the Scythe of Elune together — and walked straight into the satyrs' trap.

At Kesselir, the satyrs had meant to awaken the side of Sola she had been fleeing from all along — the Nightmare — and let her devour the minds of these unexpected intruders within the Dream, corrupting their souls in the process. What they had not expected was that Jace and the others, already strengthened far beyond ordinary sellswords by the rift's influence, had enough dream-spirit power to slay a corrupted dragon in a Nightmare whose foundations were still shallow.

And most importantly, the satyrs had never imagined that Alexstrasza's blood flowed through Jace's veins.

After all, who would have thought that operating in secret in Winterspring would bring down on them the very people who had defeated Deathwing in Grim Batol on another continent?

After Vereesa finished explaining the whole affair to Mossa, Sola pressed her forehead and said quietly, "From beginning to end, those demons made a complete fool of me."

"It is not your fault, Sola." Mossa patted her gently. "The satyr who planted this seed of corruption is Savarris, son of Xavius — the leader of the Emerald Flame, a base and cunning creature. There are also two other Emerald Flame satyr lords, Tristan Evilhoof and Shadivels — they were the self-styled prince's right and left hands."

"They have already been killed by Jace, Grede, and Vereesa, Mossa," Sola said. "You should have introduced me to them earlier..."

Mossa glanced at the succubus, then shifted her gaze back to Jace and Vereesa. "I knew they could..."

Jace and Molofier exchanged a look. He had guessed right — it was precisely because of the demon that Mossa had been unwilling to tell the green dragon the truth.

"Still, killing them can save Winterspring, but it will not fully turn the crisis around," Mossa tried to say in Common. "Tell Cenarius — his children — and the Dragonqueen — her children. It is not only the satyrs here. Many powerful satyrs, especially the Emerald Flame satyrs, are scouring Kalimdor for places where they can wound the Dream, manufacturing... sources of Nightmare..."

Here, Mossa seemed to hit a wall of exhaustion and had to pant heavily several times. She must have been in an extremely weakened state. Jace looked at the other captured Kaldorei druids — their faces were ghastly too.

Grede supported her massive arm and asked, "Are you all right, Mossa?"

"My mana is completely drained, my love. From sustaining the dream rift."

Mossa did her best to say it in Common, then leaned back against the stone slab and sat down.

"I know... so we need to get these druids out of this demon's lair as soon as possible, Jace. Including Mossa — let them rest," Grede said, looking over.

"Can you carry us back, Sola?" Vereesa asked.

"Of course," Sola said.

"We will ride the lady dragon back to Everlook first, Mossa... very soon. You can rest then," Grede said — then suddenly realized he had said something wrong and looked at Sola. "Sorry. I forgot again."

"It is nothing, Grede. Honestly, carrying you all in flight is not so bad," Sola said with a smile.

"You seem to get along with them... both of those demons," Mossa said, snickering.

Sola said, "They pulled me out of a depths I could never have escaped on my own. It nearly cost them their will. And they forgave the foolishness I committed... for me, no repayment could ever be enough."

"Then start by taking us back to Everlook," Jace said.

"Mm." Sola nodded slightly.
