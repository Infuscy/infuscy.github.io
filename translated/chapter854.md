## Chapter 854: A Meal for One Gold

The room was cold. Mosa fell silent for a moment before speaking: "I thought that with the staff, I could help my people fight the centaur. Now it turns out to not only be a hoax, but I fear I have angered Remulos and offended Cenarius — neither of them wants that thing to appear in the world again."

"Do you really think getting your hands on the Scythe of Elune could have saved your people, Mosa?" Jace asked.

"I am sorry, Jace," Mosa said in Elvish. "I am grateful to you — you guided me to the forest's guardians. They have been kind to me and taught me so much. Without you... I might never have known what to do after leaving Feralas. But you do not understand everything about this staff."

"I do not like this feeling," Grede said. "There is at least one person at this table who does not speak Elvish, folks."

"I will translate," Vereesa said.

"Fine, damn it." Grede scratched his head. "My language skills have never been much good. Otherwise I would have picked up Valendris."

"Who is Valendris?" Mosa asked suddenly.

"Vae— er — ah." The dwarf coughed, gave an awkward laugh, and said, "Another elf. That is all over between us, Mosa. I forgot her long ago!"

"I can vouch for that," Jace raised his hand. "That elf could not have cared less about him."

"Go screw yourself," Grede bared his teeth — then seemed to remember something. "Ah, right, Mosa, she really could not have cared less about me. And — what matters most — I sure as hell did not give a damn about her either! Really!"

"You are a charming dwarf, Grede. She lost you, and that is her loss," Mosa said with effort.

"Exactly," Grede nodded quickly.

Vereesa looked at Jace and raised an eyebrow. Jace said at once, "That is all old history, Mosa. It is just — I may not be as ignorant as you think."

"I am not calling you ignorant, Jace, my good friend," Mosa lowered her head. "I am sorry I said that. But we certainly know more about the Moon's treasures."

"Dalaran's research on worgen began a long time ago." Jace drew two circles on the table with his finger and linked them together. "Many of the Kirin Tor's planar researchers have known for ages that worgen are beings from another world — and they even figured out that worgen come from the Dream. The Kirin Tor's foremost worgen scholar, Ur, built on the work of those shadow-magic researchers and devoted himself to studying the connection between worgen and the Dream. In his writings, he noted that most worgen slumber long in a 'nightmare-like dark homeland' — and disturbingly, some of them have already awakened, and they are hostile toward everything. He warned Dalaran repeatedly against trying to bring worgen into the real world. In fact, he believed that because of Dalaran's research, individuals may have already seeped through into the real world — and that is the source of the worgen legends that have persisted for thousands of years across the human kingdoms."

"You are quite interested in worgen," Mosa said.

Jace glanced at Vereesa. "I once thought a death knight prowling near my homeland was a giant worgen, so I did a great deal of research into them. If you use the Moon's staff to wake dream-worgen into the real world on your own, without that tree's hypnotic hold over them, those worgen could become a new calamity — they could turn the land you fought to reclaim into something truly horrible."

"But..." Sola said, "Daral'nir can soothe their savagery."

"True," Jace said. "Ur's writings say the same. But he also found worgen who, after waking, still kept their savage nature, Sola. Trust me — if summoning worgen were not dangerous, those Dalaran mages would have summoned an entire worgen nation by now. The fact that they dare not do it means the danger of summoning worgen is far beyond what we mortals can imagine. If you are truly unwilling to set this matter aside for now... why not try asking Queen Ysera herself?"

Vereesa said, "Now that you have recovered, you should be able to reach her, right?" Sola shook her head. "I still do not dare."

Just then the goblin waiter brought out a plate of roasted red fruit, followed by plate after plate of roasted white meat in fragrant sauce.

"Let us eat first, everyone," Grede picked up his knife. "That staff is gone no one knows where — even if we wanted to look for it, there is nowhere to look, right?"

Mosa nodded and said in Elvish, "I admit to you, Jace — I was ignoring the danger that staff might bring. If it truly were no trouble at all, the druids and green dragons would not be so wary of it."

"It is only that Sun Rock Retreat has been burned, and the tauren's last refuge in the Stonetalon Mountains is gone..." Her heavy voice even took on a note of tears.

"Cenarius's son, Remulos, says my heart cannot be still, and so I struggle to connect with the Dream. But how can I keep still — my people are homeless, hunted across the plains — how can I dream in peace in Moonglade!"

At this point Vereesa could barely bring herself to keep translating.

When she finally managed to get Mosa's meaning across to Grede, Grede's face had gone ugly as well.

"When we are done here, Jace, I want to stay," he said firmly. "Carry my decision back to Aerie Peak, to... my father's grave."

"Do not talk like you are going to die here, Grede," Jace said. "I do not think those centaur can take you down. But I will go."

"You think too much. I am only afraid if I stay too long here, they will worry!" Grede said.

Jace made a noncommittal sound, then looked at the tauren: "Forgive me for speaking bluntly, Mosa, but I do not think you alone — or even you plus Grede — is going to make a dent in the bigger picture. You should keep studying the druidic path with those demigods, and even spend more time with the Kaldorei druids. When you have truly learned something and brought it back to your people, you can raise more druids and fight the centaur with powerful natural magic. Actually, that was my original idea..."

Mosa fell silent for a while. "I am sorry, Jace. Hatred made me lose my head."

Jace shook his head. "It is only because I am on the outside that I can stay a little calmer, Mosa. It is a good thing nothing has reached the point of no return yet."

"Actually, this was all my fault to begin with," Sola said. "It was my desire to use the legendary worgen to fight the satyrs' crimes that dragged Mosa into this... By the way, you mentioned earlier that you still had business to attend to. Why did you originally come to Winterspring?"

"Er."

Grede, Jace, and Vereesa exchanged glances, hesitating over whether to bring up Ulthalesh.

"Mosa is still weak — she cannot fight — but I can help." Sola looked at Jace. "Please believe me. I may not be much of a fighter, but at least in my true form I could take on several Shardtooth bears. And I can heal, and I can hypnotize a good many people, if that would be of use..."

"I could too, Sola," Mosa said at once. "I am willing to help."

"Rest up first, Mosa — that is, if you are willing to help," Jace said.

Mosa immediately grabbed two fruits and stuffed them into her mouth, swallowed, and reached for a chunk of roasted meat. Watching her, Jace realized he had almost completely forgotten the smell of the food on the table.

Everything in the Nightmare had left him with no appetite even now.

"That is right — eat your fill first!" Grede slapped the table. "Everything else can wait until tomorrow!"

Vereesa nodded with a smile and drained her bowl of meat broth in one gulp. Sola stared at a huge bone-in cut of meat in the center of the table and swallowed.

Grede saw the green dragon's expression and gripped his knife, muttering, "I might need a saw..."

Jace simply drew his magic sword, and with a light, barely perceptible chiming of music, casually sliced a few times and cleanly cut the meat into six portions.

Grede had gone rigid. Sola and Vereesa stared at his Azuresong in shock. Mosa had bitten halfway into a fruit and stopped, mouth frozen.

Jace looked at them and cleared his throat. "Do not tell Malygos."
