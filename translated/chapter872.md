## Chapter 872: A Demon's Belongings Go to a Demon

After burning the black knight corpses with hellfire, they emerged from the vault. Sunlight streamed down through the cracks, warm on their faces.

Now that Ursolus had been retrieved from Scavel's vault, seeing this ray of sunlight again, Jace couldn't help feeling as though he'd purged some terrible evil and swept away the dark clouds shrouding this region — as if everything had returned to brightness and purity...

However, his sensitivity to aberrant magic told him that deathly plague still saturated the entire region, no different from when they'd first arrived. It was just that the source of the plague was no longer Ursolus in Scavel's vault, but rather the animals, plants, and corpses that had been influenced by the scythe.

Of course, the shadowy terror that had been trying to worm its way into Jace's mind and clutch at his heart had vanished without a trace.

Ursolus had left the Dream. The fragment it had influenced, now severed from the source of its dark power, was no longer able to seep into the real world.

Jace could sense that whether it was Grede, Vereesa, or Sola, the tension in their expressions had eased considerably. They no longer looked as oppressed as they had when they'd entered.

But Grede had been wearing an ugly expression this whole time.

Coming to the gentle slope outside the old wall, Grede caught his breath and said, "I don't want to carry this broken sword anymore."

"What's wrong?" Jace asked.

"I don't know. Maybe it's just my imagination. I really don't want to have anything to do with this sword — corpses, ghosts, and it's clearly a necromantic focus. The more I think about it, the weirder it feels." Grede pulled out the broken blade of Apocalypse, wrapped in a black knight's cloak. "If I carry this thing around for too long, am I going to turn into a dead man?"

Vereesa said, "Sylvanas told me that its previous master — a mage of the Order of Tirisfal — became a neither-dead-nor-living monster from holding this sword for too long. He was originally going to use this sword to go after Aegwynn, but before he even saw Aegwynn, Apocalypse had twisted him into something even more evil than Aegwynn."

"She even knew about that," Jace murmured.

"She's the Ranger-General of Silvermoon, Jace." Vereesa looked at the thing in Grede's hands and lowered her voice. "And I think she told me that with an ulterior motive."

"Huh?" Jace looked at her. "What?"

"She said it after learning about you," Vereesa said quietly.

Jace slowly nodded. Over there, Grede held the sword out toward him, grumbling, "Why didn't you say so earlier? I don't want to turn into a dead man. At least not for no damn reason. Whoever wants it can have it."

"You fat dwarf who doesn't recognize a treasure when he sees one." The imp grabbed the edge of the demon satchel. "I wanted that thing and master wouldn't give it to me. Now it's being handed to you and you don't even want it! Besides, the sword is already broken — what are you even scared of?!"

"Hah." Grede shook the broken sword and gave a cold laugh. "Say whatever you want."

Jace took the bundle from the dwarf. Although the necromantic energy on it was now barely perceptible, that was only in comparison to the power Eredin had unleashed while wielding the sword in the vault during that frenzied assault. Sensing carefully, one could still detect trace amounts of dark energy. Grede's worry wasn't entirely unfounded.

But he was already carrying Ursolus. This staff had cleaved through Apocalypse in a single stroke and was still perfectly intact. Even though he couldn't sense anything wrong with it at the moment, Jace didn't dare let his guard down.

"So, who wants to carry it?" Jace asked, holding the weapon out.

He looked at Vereesa — the normally bold and impulsive ranger was hesitating now. He looked at Sola, who directly shook her head slightly.

"Give it to me, then." Morophil licked her lips. "I'm a demon already — there's nothing left to 'die'..."

"You're not a demon at all!" the imp immediately shouted.

"Shut up, snot," the succubus said coldly.

The imp immediately yelled, "Ah! Don't you dare call me..."

Jace shoved it into the satchel and yanked the drawstring tight, then handed the broken sword to Morophil. "You carry it, then. If you sense anything off about this sword, tell me immediately."

"No problem, master. Can't you sense it through the soul-link anyway?" the succubus said.

"So you're not worried it'll twist your soul, Morophil?" Vereesa asked, looking over.

"I'm a succubus." Morophil swung the bundle in her hand. "Who's twisting who is still an open question, given the shape it's already in."

"I love this demon's confidence," Grede nodded. "Can we keep moving? I don't want to stay in this place a second longer."

Morophil raised an eyebrow and smiled. "Ah, who would believe the Burning Legion's demon blade Apocalypse is mine now. When I next get the chance to see Detheroc, I'll be sure to give him the good news. Actually, let me tell Brutallus first — he'll be thrilled for me. Ha, I can't wait to see his ugly face when he sees this sword. Although it probably won't change much."

"It belongs to all of us," Grede said, pointing at her. "You're just holding onto it for now."

"Of course I know it belongs to everyone. But you're the last person who should be reminding me of that, dwarf." Morophil smiled. "After all, you're the one who voluntarily gave up custodial rights."

"Can someone else carry it?" Grede asked, staring at Jace.

The dwarf looked over. Sola and Vereesa both averted their gazes and started climbing upward. The furbolg had already climbed up long ago.

That left only the two of them making eye contact. Grede glanced at the tightly wrapped staff on Jace's back and could only sigh.

"How are we getting to the southern coast from here?" the dwarf asked.

Jace put on the Will-Chain. "At least get Kenluk back to his people first, then head to Everlook to find Mosa. Once we've rested up, we'll all go search for Eldaras together. With Sola here, that's leagues better than any zeppelin."

"Ah, ha, I almost forgot!" Grede said. "Then let's hurry."

They traveled through the forest for a day or two. As they drew near the Frosthammer settlement once more, several furbolgs lurking in the nearby treeline looked over in surprise.

They came to Kenluk's side, examined his wounds, and questioned him in the furbolg tongue. Then they looked back toward Jace's group.

Jace couldn't read the expressions on these furbolgs' faces, but he could more or less feel that they were brimming with shock. "What are they saying?" Vereesa asked Sola.

"They don't believe it," Sola said helplessly. "They don't believe we resolved the source of the plague here in less than a week."

Jace said, "That's not surprising. After all, they've been tormented by this plague for decades and never figured out what was causing it. They had no idea Ursolus existed."

Although Jace took it in stride, Kenluk was clearly furious, explaining to the furbolg guards around him nonstop. But between his grumbling and the wild waving of his two thick arms, he didn't seem to be getting through to them.

Arriving at the cave entrance, the old furbolg was still waiting there. Seeing Kenluk and Jace's group, he leaned forward slightly. His already-small eyes opened a little wider.

The old furbolg leaned on his staff and stood, saying in Thalassian, "I heard the children encountered black knights in the snow valley. They followed you over, and I thought you were unlikely to return alive. I didn't expect you to actually come back. Did you see that evil temple?"

Jace explained as best he could in Thalassian: "That temple was actually a vault built by a human mage to hide a dark artifact. Many years ago, some demons tried to steal that artifact from within and forcibly broke the vault's seal, which is what caused the dark energy to leak out. You don't need to worry about that vault anymore — the threat it posed has been resolved. But the deathly plague is still roaming the forest. I imagine it'll take some time for it to die out completely."

A furbolg nearby leaned close to the old furbolg and said something in the furbolg tongue — his tone urgent and even tinged with anger.

After listening, Kenluk threw his short spear aside, strode forward, and with one hand seized the young furbolg by the pelt and shoved him against the cave wall. The young furbolg had no ability to resist at all.

But this violent move immediately drew uneasy growls from the surrounding furbolgs, and without hesitation Kenluk turned and let out an even more ferocious roar at all the other furbolgs, his momentum nearly overwhelming them entirely.

Although the atmosphere had grown this tense, Jace's group still had no idea what they'd been saying.

"Let him go, noble Kenluk. There's no need to antagonize your kin on our account," Sola said in a calm tone.

The tall furbolg hunter exhaled, shoved the young furbolg aside, and stepped back a few paces to Sola's side.

The green dragon continued in Thalassian, speaking to the furbolg Kenluk had pinned down: "Anyone who ventures deep into that temple will inevitably be stained by the necromantic energy within. You cannot use that to conclude we share the same ill intent as the artifact's creator, young Frosthammer."

"I harbor no suspicion of you, beautiful forest guardian." The young furbolg stood up, lowered his head slightly, then stared at Jace. "I'm just worried that all of you have been bewitched by this shadow-walking warlock. I can sense it — he uses demonic magic, and his heart is utterly dark. Beware of his schemes."

Jace wasn't particularly bothered by the furbolg's suspicion. What interested him more was why so many furbolgs could tell at a glance that he was a warlock, while the mages in Stormwind's Mage Quarter had such a hard time figuring it out.

"He at least brought the Timbermaw's medicine and saved many of your kin's lives, so watch your tongue." Vereesa lifted her chin and said coldly, "What kind of person he is doesn't fall within your judgment."

She spoke this with full fury, and entirely in Thalassian grammar and accent, which left the other party a bit taken aback. But from his expression, he'd definitely understood her meaning.

Grede seemed to sense that the furbolgs' and Vereesa's tones and attitudes were a bit unfriendly, that both sides were at daggers drawn, and instinctively reached for his waist — only to realize his hammer had already shattered. In the end, he grasped at nothing.

"Frankly, I don't give a damn what you think of me," Jace said in Thalassian. "I only came here to tell you roughly what happened inside, and we've brought Kenluk back. Although we couldn't protect his arm."

"No, I'm fine." Kenluk shook his head vigorously, then slammed his severed limb against the cave wall. The earthen wall caved in from the blow.

"Please forgive young Gegel — he knows nothing." The old furbolg coughed twice. "Although I cannot become the Frosthammer chieftain, the fact that I've managed to hold this crumbling ancient clan together at least proves I can still see things clearly, human."

He leaned on his long staff, caught his breath, and said, "I believe you, and I believe what you encountered in that cavern. But the young ones of the clan — they've seen little and are unwilling to believe things they haven't seen. But at least they still believe in something. Come with me. I'll make them understand."

With that, the old furbolg shuffled out of the cave mouth step by step, and Kenluk followed him.

The young furbolg who'd been shoved down propped himself up, still looking at Jace as if worried he wouldn't dare move. Only after Jace's group followed him out together did he trail along behind.
