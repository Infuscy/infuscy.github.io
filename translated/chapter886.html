Chapter 886: A Village in the Snow

The awkward silence lasted a moment before Grede asked, "What were we talking about again?"

"The aqir," Jace said. "And the kind that can die and resurrect over and over again — if those Tuskarr weren't making things up, anyway."

"Necromancy." Grede pointed at the black cloth bundle tucked under Morophil's arm. "Remember that mage's lair? That weapon could resurrect the black-clad warriors I'd just barely managed to put down. What a damned evil thing. So you think those aqir might have something like that too? Whatever Nathrezim forged that sword might've gifted one to the spiders' king as well! Those wicked things never stop trying to throw all of Azeroth into chaos."

Jace stared at the dwarf in silence. He hadn't expected Grede's rambling to get this close to the truth, even if it was slightly off the mark.

"Very likely," he nodded.

"You're actually not mocking me for once. That's weird," Grede muttered.

"That just goes to show my usual rebuttals aren't a habit — they're based on objective analysis," Jace said.

"Hmph." Grede snorted dismissively.

Vereesa said, "What surprises me more is that the northern continent has already descended into this much chaos, and neither Dalaran nor Quel'Thalas has reacted at all."

Jace said, "Based on the Dragon tongue those Tuskarr used, this has only happened in the last year or two. They haven't even decided to migrate elsewhere yet. And Dalaran and Quel'Thalas have each had their own troubles these past two years — the theft of the Eye of the Arcane, the Amani trolls, the Dark Portal... It's no surprise they've overlooked the recent changes in the north."

"So what should we do?" Vereesa asked.

"You're the Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas," Grede said. "Go back and tell your sister the general about this. Get Silvermoon to pay attention to what's happening in Northrend."

"Of course," Vereesa said. "I don't know if Sylvanas will care about the Northrend crisis — she's still dealing with the Amani trolls. Prince Kael'thas would probably take this more seriously. I should go to the Sunfury Tower and report it to him, so he can bring it up at the Kirin Tor council and get the whole Alliance's attention."

Jace nodded. "Then when we reach Dalaran, I'll find Krasus and talk to him about this. He owes me a massive favor — he'll definitely help. And if Kael'thas is interested, we're even more likely to get the Kirin Tor's attention."

"I hope the people of Northrend can hold out a little longer until aid arrives from the south," Vereesa said with a nod.

"You're Wrynn's advisor. Don't forget to remind that young king," Grede said.

"Stormwind is simply too far from the north. Even if Varian is willing to pay attention, I doubt there's much he can do." Grede nodded at that. "Should we bring back some evidence? That'd be more convincing."

"You mean we grab some proof related to the undead aqir?" Jace asked.

"If we get the chance, definitely. But right now we know nothing about the insect-folk," Vereesa said. "We don't know how to fight them, what they're capable of, where they're hiding... In the end we might end up surrounded by dead aqir and living aqir at the same time."

Jace knew where the entrance to the aqir kingdom of Azjol-Nerub was in the game — it sat right between Borean Tundra and Dragonblight, quite conspicuous.

But what about here?

He gazed out at the endless tundra stretching along the shore, the distant snow cliffs, the vast stretches of yellow grass vanishing into the horizon.

Here, he didn't even know where on the Borean Tundra they were, and he didn't have a single reliable map to reference — forget about finding the gates of Azjol-Nerub.

Besides, the landscape twenty years from now might look quite different from the present, given all the battles and conflicts that would happen in between. Azjol-Nerub had gone from an aqir kingdom to a place ruled by the undead. For these subterranean dwellers, frequently changing their surface entrances was probably routine.

"Morophil, are you good at tracking undead creatures?" Jace asked.

"Why would you think I'm good at tracking the undead?" Morophil turned her head and asked.

"You've dealt with Nathrezim, right? Nathrezim are all demons who wield necromantic power," Jace said.

Morophil replied, "To be honest, if there really are undead nearby, I can sense them. But my sensitivity to demons is far stronger than to anything else... Tracking Nathrezim by their necromantic aura would be a complete detour for me, Master."

"I can try, everyone," Somaelir said. "I can distinguish the scent of many creatures, including the silithid hiding in every corner of Kalimdor."

"Then I'll leave it to you, Sola," Jace said.

Somaelir said, "I'll do my best, Jace. But I have to be honest — I've never tried tracking undead before. I might overlook some potential danger."

"That's all right. As long as we search as close to the southern coast as possible. We're not too far from the blue dragon palace, the Nexus, and the Tuskarr described the source of the shadow as being in the far north. I think even if there are undead lurking around at this distance, they won't pose much of a threat to us."

Somaelir acknowledged him. Grede took a deep breath and said, "It's still hard to believe we've got a real dragon in our party. Ten years ago, I thought green dragons were just something starving lost travelers in the forest made up. Now here I am riding on the back of a green dragon. Even if I went back to Aerie Peak right now and told them, they wouldn't believe it..."

"That's because we make you think that way," Somaelir said. "Ysondre told me to remember to use hypnotic magic to put mortals who see us to sleep, so they'd dismiss the experience as a dream."

"Ah... so the green dragon sightings in the Hinterlands were all real?!" Grede exclaimed.

Vereesa, fiddling with her letter opener, said, "There are also cases of dwarves failing to catch prey and making excuses for themselves — at least the green dragon sighting reports I've heard from Quel'Danil have all been well-documented."

"What, your hunters come back with a full haul every time they leave the house?" Grede shot back. "Your hunters never make excuses for themselves?"

"At least not like you do..."

The elf and the dwarf started bickering again. Jace glanced at them and thought that at least they'd stopped dwelling on the troll-turning-into-an-elf thing.

They continued along the coast, and soon Somaelir picked up something off.

They flew over a highland and down to a forest at the mountain's base. Jace spotted traces of a road among the trees — built in a style reminiscent of Lordaeron. Could there be humans living here?

"I can sense the scent of insect-folk at the end of this road, Jace, if you're willing to trust me," Somaelir said.

Vereesa asked, "Should we land here and take a look up ahead?"

Everyone agreed. The green dragon landed on a clearing by a broken cliff, then transformed into the form of an elf and followed the others on foot.

By now it had just reached midday. Though snow had been falling steadily and the overcast sky was hazy, they could still make out the shadows of houses behind the trees at the road's end.

Jace had guessed right — humans did live here, and they might even have some connection to Lordaeron.

The largest human settlement in Northrend right now should be a place called Wintergarde. Jace had heard a goblin from Booty Bay mention it once — the goblins had trade relations with the town.

In the game, Wintergarde had already been destroyed by the Scourge. Its inhabitants had been converted into ghouls and skeleton soldiers, with only a handful of survivors living to see the Alliance army arrive and recount the town's fate.

The Alliance had built a stronghold — Wintergarde Keep — against the neighboring cliff, using part of the town as its foundation. But that place should still be far from here. Jace had the feeling they hadn't left the Borean Tundra yet, which meant this was likely a human village that had broken off from Wintergarde and migrated west before settling here.

As they drew closer to the houses, even someone without any magical sensitivity could tell something was off.

The village was tiny — just a single road with two rows of houses flanking it. It reminded Jace of the abandoned village he and Grede had come across while hunting ghosts in Deadwind Pass.

The resemblance wasn't just a matter of scale. It was the atmosphere. The place was deserted — no living, no dead. The streets were cold and still.

A gust of wind blew through, and only rags hanging from the windows for some reason swayed in the breeze. Grede was just about to call out — probably to ask if anyone was there — when Jace immediately stopped him.

Because he'd caught a scent. A faint smell of decay riding the wind, blowing straight into his face. "It seems an unknown disaster has already swept through this village," Sola said.

"Let's hope they left for Wintergarde on their own, like the Tuskarr did," Jace said.

"Though there don't seem to be any signs of insects here." Grede looked around. "I'm not questioning your abilities, Sola, but it looks like..."

"Maybe I'm just not used to tracking in the real world yet. I'm sorry," Sola said. "Don't sell yourself short, Sola," Jace said.

"Either way, we need to check every one of these houses. See if there are any survivors." Vereesa gazed toward a two-story house with its closed door in the distance. "Maybe they can even tell us where to find those resurrected insect-folk."

"Damn it." Grede bit his lip, muttering to himself. "More dead to deal with. The sight of corpses makes me sick."

"Every living person gets a chance to become a dead one after meeting your hammer, dwarf," Morophil said. "I fail to understand why a warrior who considers himself excellent would fear that."

"The ones who aren't fully dead, all right? And I'll correct you — that's disgust, not fear!" Grede grumbled as he looked around at the others. "So, anyone got a good weapon for me? My Headsman got thrown at that blue dragon and teleported clean away!"

"I've only got one sword left myself," Vereesa said, patting her waist.

"This can be yours." Morophil lifted the broken Apocalypse she'd been carrying under her arm. "Sod off, demon," Grede said.

"Then have this." Morophil tossed the Nathrezim black blade over. Grede caught it mid-air, hefted it, and asked, "This isn't that death knight's black sword you got off Hogger, is it, Jace?"

"It is," Jace confirmed.

Grede swallowed. "Fine, fine, this one'll do. Let's move."

After searching a few unlocked, empty houses along both sides of the street without finding anything of value, they converged on the two-story house.

It was the largest building in the village. Even if it wasn't where the village head lived, it had to be an inn or a wayhouse for travelers.

Jace examined the walls and roof. The place looked no different from one still occupied. Even if it had been abandoned, it couldn't have been for more than a year.

But what was unusual was that every window of the house — large or small, first floor or second — had been completely boarded up with wooden planks, not a single gap left.

Approaching the door, Jace immediately caught a fishy smell.

This fishiness was different from the faint stench of decay that had lingered in the village streets, and it was also distinct from the peculiar odor of Shadow magic.

Grede whispered toward the door, "Hello?" Unsurprisingly, there was no response.

The group exchanged glances, and Jace said, "Break it open."

Vereesa nodded and kicked the wooden door to pieces. The fragments flew into the house with a bang, and a thick, fishy stench rolled out.

Jace drew Skysong and channeled arcane light into the room — but his view was completely blocked by something grayish-white. "Your senses were spot on, Sola," he said, turning back. "It's full of spider webs in there."
