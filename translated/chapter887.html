Chapter 887: "The King of the Dead"

Seeing the room full of spider webs, Jace immediately sheathed Skysong. The flames on Morophil's body dimmed at once as she edged into the shadows, doing everything she could to keep her magical fire from touching the webs — because if they caught, the whole house could go up in an instant.

Grede and Vereesa took the lead, hacking through the webs with a sword and a long dagger as they pushed inside. They reached a narrow corridor, and the deeper they went, the denser the webs became. The floor began to show patches of dried slime left by something unknown.

The group searched the rooms near the corridor for a while, confirmed there were no living people trapped in the webs, and regrouped at the cellar door.

Basically, this was the last place in the whole village left to search.

The cellar door couldn't be pried open or broken down by force. Vereesa backed up a few steps, took a running start at the wooden door, intending to give it everything she had — but right before she made contact, she heard something muttering on the other side.

She faltered, leaning against the door without landing the kick, but raised her short blade toward the entrance. "Did you hear that?" the ranger asked.

Grede nodded, pressed his ear to the door and listened for a while. Jace and Sola pricked up their ears too. Before long, another sound came from inside.

"You're not dead..."

The speaker's accent was very strange and awkward, but Jace could still easily make out that it was Common, and he could understand it. "There really is a villager alive in there." Grede was about to charge the door when Jace stopped him. "Don't."

"What's wrong?" Grede asked.

"Alive... maybe," Jace said quietly. "Villager... not necessarily. There's something off about his voice."

"Voice?" Grede frowned. He'd clearly sensed it too, he just couldn't put his finger on what.

Sola stepped closer to the door and called out, "Who are you?"

"I am a resident of this village. I hid here to escape the plague brought by the King of the Dead," the voice from inside said pitifully. "I'm starving. I don't even have the strength to stand, let alone open that door. Please help me, whoever you are..."

The group outside exchanged glances, their hesitation deepening.

The King of the Dead?

For a moment Jace thought the "king" it mentioned was Anub'arak, the nerubian king from the game — the Crypt Lord.

But Anub'arak had been killed by Arthas and raised into Scourge servitude. Right now Arthas was still comfortably playing prince, so Anub'arak should still be alive.

So the "King of the Dead" they spoke of — could it be the Lich King?

Jace realized the other party's Common was quite fluent, with many traces of Lordaeron speech patterns and habits, but he gradually picked out what was awkward — the speaker's vocal organs weren't right.

So there were many tiny, fractured undertones in his speech. Altogether, it didn't sound like the voice of a soft tongue, throat, and lips, but rather a collection of strange, hard objects and magical force interweaving, desperately trying to imitate a human voice.

And most importantly, it seemed to be lying.

Jace exchanged a glance with Morophil. Man and demon were in agreement on that point.

But if the other party was an enemy, that didn't quite add up either, because Jace clearly sensed fear and despair seeping through the door, even tinged with a trace of powerless anger.

Even though these emotions carried Shadow power far beyond what any mortal's feelings could reach, if the thing on the other side was an undead monster trying to disguise itself as a human to trick the people outside into a surprise attack, logically it shouldn't have exposed so many negative, cowering emotions.

"I'll give you one chance. Tell us honestly what you are," Jace said. "Watch your next words carefully. If you lie, we'll come in and kill you. But if you're honest, no matter what you are, we'll consider treating you as a friend. Trust me — telling your lies apart won't be difficult."

After Jace spoke, the others nodded. A long while later, a crackling, grinding sound came from inside — the kind that made the hair stand up all over your body.

"I am indeed not human. I... regret my attempt to deceive you," the voice said, suddenly hoarse and blurred. "Don't kill me. I've already paid too much just to survive. I don't want all that effort to be for nothing."

"I am a resident of the Kingdom of Azjol, Azjol-Nerub. I am a scout. In a battle to cleanse the necrotic plague, I was wounded and driven to the surface. I had no choice but to hide in a human basement to survive. Later these humans discovered me, sealed up this room, and moved away. I've been trapped here ever since."

"I know my appearance is peculiar to you surface-dwellers, and that it frightens you, but I don't want any conflict with surface people right now. Because for both the underground dwellers and the surface folk, the appearance of the King of the Dead is a disaster..."

"What does 'Azjol-Nerub' even mean?" Grede asked. "So what you're saying is, the thing in there is an insect?!"

"Can we trust it?" Vereesa asked.

"At least one thing it said was right — when it comes to facing the dead, we should be natural allies."

Jace remembered that in the game, the nerubians of Azjol-Nerub still preserved a small faction willing to cooperate with the Alliance and the Horde. In other words, they weren't naturally hostile to surface-dwellers.

Although these insect-folk's ancestors had been born from the bodies of the Old Gods, after tens of thousands of years of separation from the sealed Old Gods, they had gradually been assimilated by Azeroth and evolved into an intelligent subterranean race.

Of course, no matter what, they were still Shadow-touched creatures of flesh and blood — vigilance couldn't be dropped entirely. After all, there was the precedent of Ahn'Qiraj; a sealed Old God hadn't completely lost its influence.

"Then let's open the door," Vereesa said.

Grede nodded and threw his shoulder hard against the door, cracking the wooden boards — but the tough spider silk held the damaged planks together, refusing to tear.

The group hacked away with their swords for a long while before they finally broke open a hole big enough to pass through. A thick, fishy stench rolled out from within. The floor was covered in dark yellow slime and broken shells. This human-built cellar, wrapped in layer upon layer of spider webs and unidentifiable fleshy tissue, was no different from the spider crypts Jace and Grede had seen at the bottom of the troll ziggurats in the Hinterlands.

In the deep corner of the cellar, a trembling shadow was watching them. Vereesa raised a finger and cast Light to illuminate its appearance. Everyone present gasped in shock. Grede scowled and took several heavy breaths — he looked severely nauseated.

Overall, the creature's lower half resembled a spider — a giant abdomen covered in gray and brown patterns, bulging high, possibly swollen from its wounds, terrifying as though it might burst open at any moment. In stark contrast, its upper half lay slumped on the ground like a shriveled little man.

It fixed a skeletal, grayish-white head on them. The combination of that horrifying appearance and its powerless, weakened state made anyone who looked at it simply feel uneasy.

Everyone raised their weapons, ready to finish the creature off at any moment. Vereesa held up the Light and stepped a few paces closer, then saw the nerubian's hind legs hanging feebly from the webs on the wall. When the light shone over, a swarm of unnamed tiny beetles scattered with a rush.

"Damn it, bastard..." Grede cursed incessantly, dodging the scurrying bugs, interspersed with a few choice insults in Dwarvish.

"It's alive," Morophil said.

Sola nodded and quietly murmured some incantation. More and more tiny bugs poured from the crevices of the nerubian's body, scattering like flowing sand.

Grede watched the bugs run off and scratched his neck hard. "How can there be so many tiny bugs alive in a place this cold?"

"Because these bugs were never alive to begin with," the nerubian said, its hollow, lifeless eyes fixing on the dwarf as its bizarre mouthparts clicked and writhed. "I'd almost forgotten... what it felt like without them. Thank you, wielder of Nature magic. I feel much better."

"Undead maggots?" Grede muttered. "I should've expected this kind of cursed thing in a place like this. I'm starting to regret coming in here."

Sola said, "I'll try to cleanse the disease these maggots have caused, then help him mend his wounds."

As she spoke, she guided Nature magic, and before their eyes the cracks in the nerubian's body were slowly mended by the magic. Its fluids dried quickly, filling the wounds.

Seeing the nerubian recover somewhat, Vereesa said, "I have a few questions."

"Ask them, Quel'dorei," the nerubian said. In just a short while, its voice was already much clearer and stronger than before.

Vereesa was clearly a little surprised that the nerubian recognized her race. She paused, then said, "You mentioned the King of the Dead. Earlier we heard the Tuskarr speak of shadows hidden in the far northern mountains. Are these the same thing? Or do they share the same source?"

"I don't know," the nerubian shook its head. "What I know is that the King of the Dead is corrupting our kingdom with an unspeakably evil plague, raising the dead who had long rested in their tombs. Azjol-Nerub is no longer at peace. This plague grows worse by the day. Many elders have been forced to call it what it is — a war. A war between the living and the dead within the kingdom itself. I imagine the surface-dwellers have been affected as well, but this was never our intention."

Based on the nerubian's account, Jace knew this "King of the Dead" very likely referred to the Lich King himself. It was entirely possible that the later title "Lich King" for Ner'zhul had spread from the mouths of surviving nerubians.

"How long ago did this happen?" Jace asked.

"A year, two years. It all came so fast, giving no time to react," the nerubian said.

"Those Tuskarr also mentioned that shadow descended quickly. Even if it's not exactly the same thing, it's definitely connected," Jace said.

"Then we need to get back and report this as soon as possible," Vereesa said. "If a year or two is enough to devastate this land like this, if we let it go on for a few more years, Lordaeron and Quel'Thalas could both be affected."

"We could grab a few of the insects here as evidence," Jace said. Vereesa looked around and nodded. "Let's do that."

"Then, can you leave here on your own?" Jace asked the nerubian ahead.

"Of course. Thank you for your help. You are kind surface-dwellers," the nerubian murmured.

"I hope you make it back to Azjol-Nerub alive and help your people keep resisting the resurrected dead," Jace said.

"Azjol-Nerub will triumph," the nerubian said. "My kingdom spans the entire continent, and it is unfathomable in its depth..." Wait — unfathomable?

Jace had been about to leave, but hearing the nerubian mention Azjol-Nerub's unfathomable territory, he suddenly remembered something.

During the war against the Scourge, the nerubian grew increasingly unable to resist the ever-growing numbers of dead, so they dug as deep as they could, building more underground fortresses to hold back the dead.

But the deeper they dug, the closer they got to the tendrils of Yogg-Saron buried beneath the continent of Northrend, eventually releasing the Old God's minions imprisoned below and re-enslaving these nerubians who had already broken free of the Old God's control.

Caught between the Old God's minions below and the Scourge above, Azjol-Nerub collapsed rapidly, no longer able to resist. The Lich King thus enslaved the entire nerubian empire.

Jace warned, "Beneath Northrend lies a darkness you cannot resist, earth-dweller. Don't think you can keep digging downward in peace... You should already know this clearly, shouldn't you?"

Seeing the nerubian hesitate, Sola said, "He's right. The Kaldorei tried to plant Andassil in Northrend, but the roots of this World Tree grew too deep and touched a darkness it could not resist. In the end the entire tree was corrupted. The Kaldorei druids had to cut it down to prevent its corruption from endangering all of Azeroth..."

"If you intend to keep digging, the final outcome is hard to say," Jace said.

The nerubian said, "If the situation truly develops to that point, then we will have no choice."

Jace didn't know what to say to that. When the Lich King used his undead legions against the nerubian kingdom, the nerubians were virtually isolated and without aid.

And the scout before him didn't look like anyone of particularly high standing in Azjol-Nerub. Even warning him alone would probably have no decisive effect on the entire spider kingdom.

All they could do at this point was head south as quickly as possible and alert the entire Alliance.
