Chapter 888: The Fjord

After leaving this nameless human settlement and continuing eastward, Somaelir once again carried the group on her back as they set off.

This time, the faint melancholy of leaving Kalimdor behind on the journey home, and the relief of finally being on the return trip, had been largely washed away by Northrend of the necrotic plague.

Everyone spoke much less. All of them stared at the far shore, as if this dark disaster could ride the wind into the sea and come straight after Sola.

Vereesa carried the cloth bundle in her hand — inside was a box bound with branches Sola had enchanted with Life magic, holding samples of the undead maggots retrieved from the cellar. Though none of them harbored any illusions that this would lead the mages of Quel'Thalas to devise a counter to the plague sweeping down from the north, having some evidence was still far better than making claims out of thin air.

Flying close to the coast all the way, the climate grew colder and colder. Somaelir had to stop and rest every evening.

Fatigue from flying was one factor; the second was that she also needed to maintain the power of the Dream to ensure that the passengers on her back — herself included — wouldn't fall ill from hypothermia at high altitude. That was what really drained her strength.

Roughly three days later, in the evening, they rested on a snow-piled cliff.

Jace looked back along the coast they'd come from. They'd flown a long way northeast along the shoreline, which was why the climate was much colder than it had been on the tundra before.

Vast sheets of snow and floating ice covered the sea. From time to time came the soul-shaking sound of ice colliding, echoing hollowly between the lifeless cliffs.

Walking up to higher ground and gazing north toward the inland interior, there was an endless snowfield — nothing but white snow and wind as far as the eye could see. This should be Dragonblight from the game — a great snowfield in the middle of Northrend's southern shore.

Countless ancient dragons had chosen this place as their final resting place. Over hundreds of thousands of years, no one knew how many dragon bones had accumulated here, and so the entire region had taken its name from that.

After checking the luggage, Jace walked a little ways downslope through the snow, unthinkingly recalling the dream he'd had several times before.

The difference was that in the dream he could see the Wyrmrest Temple — that great bronze tower sitting in the middle of Dragonblight — but here he couldn't catch even a glimpse of its shadow.

Just then, Jace noticed Sola also gazing north from a height not far away, in the same direction. He looked at her and asked, "Do you know where we are?"

"The burial grounds of my kind," Sola said. "Perhaps one day I will return here as well, to the Emerald Sanctuary and accept my final destruction."

Like with Grede, Jace often forgot that Sola was a true dragon.

Hearing her describe her bond with this snowfield in the first person, he felt a profound sense of disorientation. After a pause, Jace asked, "I heard there's a tall tower here where dragons live. Do you know where it is?"

Sola shook her head. "No. I've never been here. But I've heard my brother Itharius mention legends of the Wyrmrest Temple. I imagine a sacred place wouldn't be easily found by lowly young dragons like us, or ordinary mortals — otherwise it would lose its purpose as a sanctuary."

Jace nodded thoughtfully, unable to hide his disappointment. He'd been hoping to lay his own eyes on what the dragons' holy temple looked like, but he hadn't expected that even Sola couldn't find its exact location here.

But then again, Alexstrasza was in Grim Batol at this point, Malygos dwelt in the Nexus, Nozdormu wouldn't be at the Wyrmrest Temple wherever he was, Neltharion's corpse was with Alexstrasza, and as for Ysera, she should still be in the Emerald Dream. In other words, none of the Dragon Aspects were at the Wyrmrest Temple. The motivation to visit had weakened considerably.

Thinking it over, it wasn't so much of a pity after all.

Seeming to sense Jace's disappointment, Sola added, "You helped the red dragons defeat Deathwing, Jace Sesso, and received the blessing of Queen Alexstrasza. By any measure, you don't fall into either of those two categories I mentioned. I think if you, Vereesa, and Grede wished to go to the Wyrmrest Temple, any dragon who knows the way would be willing to take you. I'm sorry I don't have the ability to do so."

Jace said, "It's all right. I was just curious about what I've heard of the Wyrmrest Temple. For the vast majority of humans, including me, that place might as well be a myth. When you get to Stormwind, you'll have to tell my teacher, Master Marin, all about the legends of the Wyrmrest Temple you heard from Itharius."

As the coastline gradually turned south, after several days the air grew noticeably warmer. The shores shifted from endless white to gradually revealing grasslands and forests.

Jace guessed they'd arrived in the region the game called Howling Fjord.

But unlike the game, where viking-giant villages were everywhere, they saw no signs of habitation among the tranquil pine forests and rushing rivers here.

Still, that wasn't necessarily a bad thing. The vrykul were extremely skilled at hunting dragons, and Somaelir running into vrykul here could be very dangerous.

Reaching the Howling Fjord area meant they'd soon have to cross the sea southward again. Considering they might be flying over open water for several days straight, the green dragon carried the group and chose a spot beside a river in a quiet, peaceful pine forest to rest up and prepare for the journey ahead.

The previous few days, because the harsh climate had made finding prey difficult, Grede and Vereesa had been the ones going out to hunt.

This time, where they rested was basically the warmest place in all of Northrend. Much of the scenery wasn't all that different from the Tirisfal Glades, so Jace volunteered to go catch fish in the river.

Right now he was standing in the river with his trousers rolled up, holding a sharpened wooden spear and watching the fish in the water.

Considering the goal was to make dinner, he couldn't use Shadow magic. Otherwise, he doubted a single fish in this stretch of water would escape — they'd all be tormented to death by his corruption magic.

With that thought, he could only sigh, raise his arm, brace the spear, and search for his next target.

The sky gradually darkened. Seeing he still hadn't had any luck, a shadow slipped through the water toward him. Jace held his breath, locked on, and readied to stab again...

Suddenly, a whoosh — an arrow flew in from the distance and pierced the fish clean through. The current washed it to his feet. Jace picked up the arrow — it was a deep-green, sturdy barbed pike.

Even pierced by a Kaldorei silver arrow, it still thrashed wildly, trying to rear up and bite his face. "We're starving here, Jace," Vereesa shouted from the riverbank.

Jace stretched, carried the fish to the shore, and said, "If you make a warlock double as a fisherman, you'd better be ready to go hungry."

"Then is there anything else to eat?" Vereesa looked up with a smile, set her cloak aside, and asked as she loosened her straps.

Jace caught glimpses of her midriff and the patterned undersharp beneath, and drew a sharp breath.

Vereesa walked over. The pale skin visible between her chemise, flushed white from the cold, made his heart race even though she was stepping on the icy riverbank stones.

"Aren't you cold, Wen?" he asked.

"Cold? In front of you?" Vereesa pushed Jace back against a broken slab of stone jutting from the riverbank. "Besides... I finally got a chance to be alone with you, Jace. I don't want to let it slip by."

"Honestly, it's not completely alone," Jace said, glancing toward a distant branch. "Morophil is right there."

"What a pity. Then let her watch." Vereesa grabbed Jace's wrist and slipped it inside her chemise, slowly closing her eyes with a soft gasp.
