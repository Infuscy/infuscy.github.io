Chapter 889: Crossing the Northern Sea

Compared to the ocean between Kalimdor and Northrend, the strait between the continent of Lordaeron and Northrend was much narrower.

Flying south from the northern tip of the Fjord for a little over a day, they saw the islands off the northern coast of Quel'Thalas. At noon that day, facing the sun after the skies cleared, Vereesa stood steady on the dragon's back and drew a long breath toward the southeast.

Jace could feel that warmth — the magical breath of the Sunwell. Before long, two golden shadows appeared on the skyline, slowly approaching.

"Stop, green dragon... You may not come any closer. We do not wish to harm you, but ahead is the border of Quel'Thalas." The voices of the high elf dragonhawk riders rang out from afar.

"Soldiers!" Vereesa called out. "I am Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner. With me are Jace Sesso and Grede Thunderfist!"

"Lady Vereesa..."

"Ranger-Lord, forgive us for not recognizing you. We will guide your mount to Quel'Dalar Outpost."

Before the dragonrider finished speaking, Vereesa cut him off. "Friend, her name is Somaelir. She is not our mount."

"Yes, my lady. Welcome to Quel'Thalas as well, Lady Somaelir," the dragonhawk rider called.

"Pleased to meet you," Somaelir said.

The dragonhawk riders seemed a little surprised that this green dragon could speak. They said something to each other and adjusted their mounts' direction, falling into formation to fly alongside the green dragon. Still, their gazes flicked over from time to time, as if they'd never seen a green dragon before.

Quel'Dalar Outpost — Jace had never heard of this place, in the game or here.

But in Quel'Thalas, outposts and villages like Quel'Lithien and Quel'Danil were extremely numerous. Some were for trade and contact with humans, others were watchposts for monitoring troll movements.

Jace guessed that most of these outposts had probably been destroyed by the undead legions during the later Scourge invasion, which was why they were unknown.

The green dragon landed in an elven village amid a pine forest, escorted by two dragonhawk riders, where a few white towers and cottages stood.

Seeing several Farstriders approaching, servants and handmaidens in the watchtowers and cottages curiously peered over from the windows, staring wide-eyed and whispering. Even a number of arcanists and apprentices in robes couldn't help but come out to steal a look.

They probably had never seen a real green dragon. Although green dragons were more numerous than blue dragons, they were the hardest dragon kind to encounter in the real world besides bronze dragons.

If not for Somaelir being expelled from the Emerald Dream, it would have been hard to see her landing so openly before mortals. "This is the place, Jace," Vereesa said.

Jace jumped down from the dragon's back with her, and the two embraced. Vereesa pressed her cheek against Jace's and whispered, "Don't forget what you promised. If you intend to take any further action with that staff, remember to write to me. Don't keep me in the dark about everything."

"Of course I remember," Jace said.

"And of course, even if nothing important comes up, don't forget to write," Vereesa added. "This time I can try writing in Thalassian." Jace nodded.

"That would be wonderful." Vereesa smiled, raised her head, and tasted Jace's lips. "When I'm done handing off my work here, I'll find a chance to request leave from Sylvanas and come see you."

"Then I'll wait for your good news," Jace said. "But remember to send money first."

Vereesa pinched Jace's nose. "Of course... Oh, right. That mage tower — if that demon really can open that door, write to me about that too. Don't reveal the details of that matter explicitly. We can't let outsiders know we're trying to contact... the people on the other side."

"I'll remember," Jace said. "No need to worry about outsiders finding out. By the way, Sola — can I tell her?"

Vereesa glanced toward the green dragon sitting over there. Somaelir was bending her neck, using her teeth to nibble at an uncomfortable spot on her back.

"She doesn't count as an outsider. But it might be best to test her feelings about that teleportation portal first. I remember she disliked Aegwynn and Medivh, that mother-and-son pair, quite intensely. If we also attempt to open a portal to Draenor, she might react strongly."

"I more or less understand," Jace said.

"There's one more thing. Ever since we left Frosthammer, it's been on my mind." Vereesa looked up, glancing at the staff wrapped in the black knight's cloak on Jace's back. "In Master Skarvell's vault, Morophil still stepped in without hesitation to help me hold off those black knights. If she hadn't helped, I might have had a very hard time dealing with three black knights attacking at once... let alone those frenzied ghosts."

"And you remember what she said — she told you to go save Grede, then blocked those ghosts' harassment for you. So tell me: when you were in Booty Bay, did you ever actually give up the order for her to protect me? I mean... I'm grateful that demon was willing to help me, but..."

She lowered her head, seeming not sure how to continue. Jace said, "I'm certain I already told her in Booty Bay that there was no need to save you. But she's a demon, not a machine. If she hadn't helped you and you'd been defeated by the black knights, then Grede might have had to fight four or five on his own, or I would have suddenly faced the black knight leader Eredin and two or three of his subordinates all at once. In that situation, no one... could have held out, Wen. Morophil definitely understood that too, so in helping her she was also helping me, wasn't she?"

Vereesa thought it over and finally nodded. "Maybe I'm paying too much attention to that demon."

"You didn't seem to care much about her that night by the river in the fjord," Jace said.

Vereesa blushed and said softly, "That time wasn't satisfying enough. I always felt like I had to go back and bring fish to everyone. Next time I come to Stormwind, be sure to get a proper night's sleep, Jace Sesso — otherwise I'll claim half your life..."

"I'll be waiting," Jace said with a laugh.

Returning to Somaelir's side, Grede was holding a piece of elven shortbread and looked at Jace. "After we leave Dalaran, are you still going to the Lordaeron capital?"

"If possible, I do want to see my parents. But I need to find Krasus first," Jace said. Grede licked his fingers and asked, "Are you really not going to warn that old king?"

"Me? An evil warlock... I don't think he'd listen to a word I say. I won't waste my time. If you'd come with me and Vereesa to the palace last time, you'd know." Jace shook his head with a wry smile. "He'd trust Antonidas's advice far more than mine, and Antonidas would listen to the opinions of the Kirin Tor Council members."
