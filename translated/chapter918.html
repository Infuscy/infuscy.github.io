Chapter 918: The Only Reliance

That evening, Jace was able to rest in a crude shack in the surface-level farm. It looked as shabby as a stable, but at least it wasn't that sweltering, nightmarish cellar.

Warlocks and disguised succubi guarded the isolated farm outside, but Morophil still managed to slip to the door with her powerful stealth, undetected by any warlock or demon.

Jace sensed Morophil's shadow arrive at the door and opened it. A succubus disguised as a woman at the end of the corridor glanced over. "Do you need anything?"

"Hungry," Jace said. "Got anything to eat?"

"There won't be anything tonight," the succubus said. "If there's anything else you need, you can come to me, Mister Jurik."

Jace nodded and closed the door. Morophil sat behind him, her eyes murmuring with resentment. "You can disregard me, master — but if you disregard Lady Windrunner's feelings, that's an entirely different matter."

"Don't threaten me," Jace whispered. "That was a matter of necessity. You're not a felhound — you understand that, don't you?"

"But it was a second succubus, wasn't it? I failed the Windrunner's charge — I couldn't stop my master from being bewitched by another seductive—"

"All right, all right." Jace rubbed his temples. "Summoning that succubus today took real effort. I need a good night's sleep tonight. Stop pestering me — unless you can replenish my energy like Sulenna's Kakless."

"Impossible," Morophil said at once. "Master, you should know how much energy and time it costs me just to siphon a little mana from you... I'm starting to feel like I'm putting in more than I get out."

"Then there's nothing more to say." Jace said, "Here's the problem — the people at the lighthouse don't know you exist. Once they summon me over, what are you going to do?"

"Should I follow you to the lighthouse, master?" Morophil asked.

"Of course. You expect me to jump into a demon's den alone? Not to mention we still don't know what sort of creature this Guide is." Jace thought for a moment, then said, "How about this — you sneak onto that dock man's boat and hitch a ride to the lighthouse with him. Tonight."

"Has master seen a new succubus and taken a sudden dislike to me? You don't even want to spend one night together?" Morophil said, frowning.

Jace sighed and lowered his voice. "You need to get there ahead of time, because I don't know when I'll be teleported away, or when that so-called lighthouse keeper will come ashore and when he'll go back. If I get summoned to the lighthouse tomorrow morning and you go to that fishing village instead, you might not see the fake keeper for days. By the time you reach the lighthouse, those warlocks might have already sold my kidneys."

Morophil said, "Understood, master. I will never abandon you. Still less would I go looking for a second master, the way you summoned a second succubus..."

"All right, stop nagging." Jace waved his hand.

The demon grinned wickedly, kissed Jace on the forehead, then turned and vanished through the shadows, pushing the door open behind her.

Deep into the night, Jace still hadn't managed much sleep when he heard another knock at the door.

He'd assumed it was the light-brown-haired warlock coming to ask about succubus-summoning knowledge. But when he opened the door, it was Demiseat Kraus.

"Today's summoning was truly eye-opening, Ji — Mister Jurik." Demiseet held her hands awkwardly in front of her waist. After a moment of mental preparation, she said, "Actually, I wanted to ask — now that you've gotten me inside this circle, can you teach me the incantation for that magic?"

Jace rubbed his eyes. "You really are in a hurry. Here's the problem — if I teach you this incantation, what if you teach it to someone else?"

"Absolutely not," Demiseet said at once. "Such a powerful magic... if I learned it, I would never casually leak it. It would bring enormous dark magic risks."

"And am I just casually leaking it, then, Demiseet?" Jace asked.

"That's completely different — it's not 'casual' at all." Demiseet came inside and closed the door, speaking softly. "We summoned the doomguard Lord Kaltz together, and now I've brought you close to the lighthouse. Isn't that enough to earn your trust? If those people found out I helped you infiltrate the lighthouse, they'd definitely kill me."

"Then why did all the warlocks captured by the Wizard Sanctum say your magic was taught by me?" Jace asked, sitting on the mat beside him. "The materials I wrote weren't shared with many people. You lot who went to the Altar of Storms with Mo'sul are the prime suspects."

Demiseet lowered her head guiltily. "I'm truly sorry. At the time, we just... wanted to use our demon knowledge and our unique materials to trade for the qualification to acquire new knowledge. So we gave those things to the other warlocks in Moonbrook. We never meant to cause you trouble. And most importantly, it wasn't my idea."

"Mo'sul's idea?" Jace asked.

Demiseet said, "No. He never had any intention of following the Guide. He went silent after the Guide arrived in Moonbrook. I heard from 'Short Staff' that he'd met with the Guide, but it hadn't gone well."

"Have you met the Guide?" Jace asked.

"I have. He's a remarkably young-looking man. Judging by appearance alone, it's hard to believe he's a seasoned warlock. But just like you, Sesso... you can't judge by looks alone." Demiseet said, "He once invited me to meet him alone, to receive his teachings, to gain deeper dark power and summon more powerful demons. But I never made up my mind, because I hadn't figured out his background. So I've been doing peripheral work to help out around the lighthouse."

"Does this man look handsome?" Jace asked.

"Why do you ask that?" Demiseet hesitated, but seeing Jace's serious expression, she finally nodded. "I have to admit, he is indeed charismatic. That might be one of the reasons so many people follow him. But as for 'handsome' — I suppose everyone has their own standards of beauty..."

So the Guide really was a dragonkin, then?

The so-called dark power was actually drinking dragon blood to undergo an ascension ritual. But as Sola had said, this ascension ritual was only a crude version conducted by dragonkin — it only temporarily strengthened Shadow power. It wouldn't actually turn someone into a dragonkin.

This made sense too. The black dragons definitely wouldn't want the Kingdom of Stormwind to know black dragons were pulling the strings. If they used the complete ascension ritual to create a whole batch of dragonkin, wouldn't the whole scheme be exposed? By then, SI:7 wouldn't be able to cut ties with the warlocks and save themselves — they'd have to strengthen cooperation with the warlocks to keep hunting dragonkin, while preparing the kingdom for a joint invasion by Blackrock Spire and the black dragons.

Mo'sul must have seen through these black dragon schemes, which was why he refused to drink the dragon blood. Was the vial of blood behind his house smashed by him? If it had been smashed accidentally during a struggle, Mo'sul's situation might be dire.

If the Guide was a dragonkin, then there had to be more than one dragonkin here. There might even be dragonkin in this very stronghold. Which reminded him of the two cloaked, weapon-bearing figures in the cellar — even they were suspects.

Once he reached the lighthouse, Jace couldn't very well summon a felhound in plain sight to sniff out dragonkin. For now, it seemed the only thing he could rely on was the mirror-stone staff he'd brought with him.
