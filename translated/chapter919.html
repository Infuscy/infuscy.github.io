Chapter 919: No Room for Impatience

Since he'd promised Demiseat Kraus he'd teach her the Hand of Gul'dan, Jace couldn't very well keep dragging his feet without giving her anything. After all, she'd taken a great risk getting him to the lighthouse, and from the Altar of Storms to now, he'd only given her a single demonstration. Beyond that, he'd revealed almost nothing — not the incantation, not the other conditions.

So even though it was the middle of the night and dead tired, he still followed her outside.

There was no hint of dawn yet. He followed the warlock woman to a patch of woods not far from the abandoned farm. She glanced toward the farm to make sure no one had followed them to eavesdrop, then said, "Now can you tell me the incantation, Mister Jace Sesso — the magic that can slay the pit lord Azgoth with a single strike..."

"I learned this incantation and practiced it for a long time before I could complete it. I don't think I can teach you in a matter of days, Demiseet," Jace said.

Demiseet said, "Even a straightforward incantation like Fear can't be mastered in a single night. After witnessing you cast that Shadow meteor at the Altar of Storms, I could never forget it. No matter how grueling the learning process, I've steeled myself for it."

Jace glanced at her. "Then remember this — Savia, Avrus, Antea, Minuelle, Wilma." As Demiseet listened, she nodded slowly.

"Savia..."

She softly repeated the incantation. After finishing, she even closed her eyes, as though savoring some exquisite delicacy.

Then Demiseet pulled a small book from her waist pouch, glanced at what was written inside, carefully closed it again, and stood off to an empty spot.

She raised her hand, and Jace immediately recognized the casting gesture — it was the very motion he'd worked out himself for channeling Shadow when casting the Hand of Gul'dan. This warlock woman had managed to copy his casting gesture just from observing him at the Altar of Storms.

But after standing there casting for a while, with the incantation only half complete, Demiseet's expression grew increasingly strange, and she stopped.

Because no Shadow power had gathered at all — Jace sensed it too.

To him, this came as no surprise, because when he'd first figured out this incantation and attempted it for the first time, he'd also wasted his effort. Demiseet had the casting gesture he'd worked out for channeling Shadow magic, but without the matching mental guidance, it was useless.

Marin had once mentioned that casting often required finding the right resonance point with the magic. After casting so much Shadow magic, Jace felt that compared to arcane power, Shadow was far more a matter of "going by feel" — especially when emotions were involved, the "going by feel" part became even more pronounced.

That's right — he still hadn't told Demiseat Kraus the key to casting the Hand of Gul'dan: emotion. Specifically, two emotions crucial to this incantation — loneliness and hatred.

Without finding these two emotions, without brewing them to the right intensity and pouring them into the spirit that gathered Shadow power, one could not successfully cast the Hand of Gul'dan.

Jace remembered spending day and night in Duskwood, groping his way through it for several days, before finally figuring out the importance of emotion with Morophil's and Sazeno's help. He'd managed to cast what could only be called a barely successful version with extreme difficulty.

Of course, compared to what he'd cast beneath the walls of Nethergarde Keep, that version had been leagues worse.

Actually, if you asked the present-day Jace to reproduce that magic, he couldn't do it either. The thick, potent dark power radiating from the Dark Portal, the intense hatred and unwillingness carried by the countless slain soldiers' souls, and even the helplessness he'd felt facing the nearly unstoppable pit lord Azgoth...

And if not for the fact that both Marin and his daughter had been on the wizard tower, he would never have brought himself to use Life Tap several times in a row to strengthen his Shadow energy, just to condense a single dark meteor powerful enough to slay a demon lord.

Recalling that scene, Jace felt as though it had happened in another life.

Demiseet, somewhat dejected, kept murmuring the incantation. She came back to confirm it with Jace once more. After confirming there was no mistake, she tried again, following the gesture and the incantation — but in the end, still nothing gathered.

"Could you demonstrate it once more, Mister Sesso?" the warlock woman said, embarrassed.

"Didn't we make it clear from the start — don't expect to learn it in a few days?" Jace put on a look of near-exasperation.

The warlock woman stammered for a while. Jace knew what Demiseet was worried about — she suspected there was something wrong with the incantation he'd given her.

"Ah, honestly." Jace stood up, leaning against a nearby tree. "Then I'll demonstrate for you. But I won't demonstrate the complete spell — I don't want to waste a soul shard, and I don't want to use this magic to attract every mage in half of Westfall here."

"Rest assured, Mister Sesso. The Wizard Sanctum's people don't have the energy to search such a remote place — this is practically the coast," Demiseet said happily. "But I understand. As long as I can learn it stage by stage, that's enough."

Jace nodded and chanted the incantation once more.

He barely even used his hands to guide the Shadow's gathering — just habitually raised his hand. The night sky already seemed to grow darker.

The wind picked up bit by bit. Jace watched as swaths of dark power spun through the air like gauzy violet silk. Feeling that his mana had already begun to drain, he relaxed his focus, stopped chanting, and took a deep breath to steady himself.

Demiseet, watching from the side, already wore an expression of utter existential bewilderment. But when it came time for her to try, she still seemed to gather nothing at all.

She stared at the sky, pondering for a while, then said, "Complex Shadow magic may be influenced by the caster's emotions, Master Sesso."

"There is such a theory," Jace said. "But I didn't learn this magic from some dark magic school — I learned it from the orcs. The orcs never precisely told me what emotions to seek out to aid in gathering Shadow power. I just... go by feel. If you can figure it out more clearly than I have, perhaps the dark meteor you eventually unleash will be even more powerful than mine. Practice more, find the feel, Demiseat Kraus."

Hearing Jace say this, the warlock woman seemed to regain her confidence all at once. She said at once, "Since you unleashed the most terrifying Shadow meteor during the Dark Portal campaign, then imagine — what emotions would the mighty Jace Sesso have when facing the demon army at Nethergarde Keep?"

"Fear, dread, or... a desire for destruction? I know you've slaughtered countless Burning Legion demons, Mister Sesso."

"Whatever it is, it's not impatience," Jace said evenly.

Demiseet sighed, somewhat disappointed. "I understand."
