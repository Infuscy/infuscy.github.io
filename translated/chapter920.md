## Chapter 920: The Island

That evening, Jace was in the mill's cellar discussing succubus summoning with the warlocks.

For one thing, Jace was reluctant to help these strange warlocks of uncertain background — who were, what's more, helping the black dragons cause trouble in Westfall — improve their summoning magic. For another, the cellar's environment was so appallingly bad that Jace could barely stand to remain there a moment longer.

Even the two other warlocks' succubi, sitting naked beside them and listening to their discussion, couldn't stir Jace's interest.

After all, compared to Morophil, these two succubi fell short in every way — looks and figure alike.

The advantage of being a warlock among warlocks was that even if you were temperamental, or zoned out halfway through a discussion without saying a word, the other warlocks wouldn't pay your state any special mind.

It wasn't like attending class or a meeting at the Wizard Sanctum, where someone was always watching whether you were paying attention at any given moment. After sitting in silence for a while, the other warlocks simply continued discussing at their own pace.

Jace knew that many warlocks' mental states were less than normal under Shadow's influence. In an environment like this, suddenly losing it happened from time to time. Being able to sit quietly on the side without speaking was already the most acceptable state to everyone.

Just as Jace was hoping the warlocks would finish their discussion quickly so they could go eat dinner and he could seize the chance to return to his stable-like quarters and rest, a remark from Kloven beside him suddenly caught his attention.

"In a sense, the succubi in the Twisting Nether are the ones choosing us summoners," he said.

"I think so too," an elderly female warlock beside him said. "I used to think only powerful demon lords chose whether to answer a summoning. Now it seems lesser demons do as well."

"Perhaps truly lesser demons, like imps, are incapable of refusing a summoning," the bald warlock said.

"At least succubi can," Kloven said. "So when we use a token related to love and desire to attract succubi, what we're really doing is piquing their curiosity. I imagine if we used enough soul shards as summoning material, we should be able to summon succubi too — attracting them with power. What do you think? Come to think of it, it makes sense. If a demon lord like Gul'dan wanted to summon a succubus, he certainly wouldn't need any love token as casting material."

At that, the warlocks all laughed.

Kloven's male succubus, sitting beside him, said, "A shivarra attracted by power would likely only be interested in power. And such shivarra mostly serve the Legion, because the Legion can provide souls, arcane energy, and Shadow... far beyond what a mortal summoner can offer, my master."

"So you actually do develop feelings for your master?" the old woman beside him sneered.

"I don't deny it," Kloven's male succubus said with a smile. "My willingness to answer my master's summoning already more than proves the point, doesn't it? I value my master's power, and I also appreciate the way he summons me."

As he listened, Jace couldn't help but start thinking about why Morophil had answered his summoning.

The day before, when he'd attempted to summon that succubus, he'd felt as though quite a few succubi in the Twisting Nether had wanted to reach out to him, to connect with him, to answer the summoning.

This was probably what Kloven was trying to express — that when succubi in the Twisting Nether sensed a summoning ritual, they were also choosing their summoner at the same time.

But when Jace had summoned a succubus for the first time, even with that staff as a spell amplifier, the responses in the Twisting Nether had been far fewer than the day before. That was probably because the Shadow power he could command back then was nowhere near what it was now.

But Morophil was clearly a special succubus — a demon killer who had never answered a mortal summoning, a fighter who had clearly endured countless brutal battles, and who had lost her wings.

Why had she been willing to come to Azeroth at a time when not many ordinary succubi were willing to answer his call?

Morophil had said it was because she sensed Jace had an extraordinary aura, and because he carried an Eredar staff, which made her very curious.

But if she could be curious, couldn't other succubi have the same curiosity?

And Jace clearly remembered that when Morophil had first arrived in his room, she had been somewhat disappointed at first, and had urged him to quickly chant the banishment incantation to send her back to the Twisting Nether.

The more Jace thought about it, the more interested he became in what Morophil's past service to the "wingless lord" Brutallus had really been about.

If not for the fact that he was currently in a nest of dangerous warlocks and facing the threat of black dragons, he would have been willing to sit down properly with Morophil and talk about the past, to get everything clear.

Just then, a clatter of claws scraping against stone came from outside. A felhound came panting into the room and spat a paper scroll covered in demon saliva onto the ground.

Kloven walked over, picked up the scroll, glanced at it, and looked back. "Tomorrow morning, our Mister Jurik will be heading to the lighthouse."

Jace sat up straight. "At last... I was worried he'd refuse me."

"A summoner like you is exactly what the Guide needs most. But earning his trust and receiving his knowledge and blessings is no easy matter," Kloven said. "I hope you earn his trust smoothly and get to meet him in person soon."

Jace nodded. He had no intention of earning any trust or receiving any blessings, of course — all he wanted was one dragonkin head. Or a pile of dragonkin heads.

The next morning, Jace waited early in the cellar hall.

Kloven kept glancing at his pocket watch. Demiseat Kraus seemed somewhat nervous. Jace wasn't sure whether she was worried he'd sell her out at the lighthouse, or that if he got caught at the lighthouse, she'd never get the chance to learn more of the "Hand of Gul'dan."

"Jurik..."

A voice drifted into Jace's mind from another plane.

"Accept the summoning, Jurik. Your application to go to the lighthouse has been accepted. The lighthouse is summoning you. Accept the lighthouse's summoning..."

"I accept," Jace said softly.

Suddenly, it felt as though Shadow had stretched out from the cracks of space and seized his soul and flesh, pulling him into a narrow fissure. The dark cellar suddenly shrank into something like a tiny ant hole, receding from his sight and consciousness.

Jace felt as though he had been hurled into a boundless abyss, until he was flung out the other side — into sunlight, a cool breeze, fresh air...

Breaking free of that cellar felt like being liberated. His mood was indescribably buoyant.

He steadied himself with ease and looked around. He was on the island now. The fishing village on the opposite shore had shrunk to an uneven smudge on the ground. Beside him, the towering lighthouse was far grander than he'd imagined. Below the lighthouse stood two or three buildings — probably the keeper's quarters and his family's.

A place like this was practically tailor-made for a circle of warlocks to conduct their research. He suspected it would be a while before the Wizard Sanctum's mages could make gryphon mounts common enough to deal with a place like this.

Standing on the open ground and rocks around them were a number of strangers, watching Jace with expressionless faces. A young black-haired man stepped forward. "This isn't your first time answering a summoning, Mister Jurik."

"It isn't," Jace said candidly.

"The lighthouse welcomes an experienced warlock like yourself. I trust you'll soon meet our Guide and achieve the goal that brought you here," the man said with a smile.

"Let's hope so."

As Jace said it, he glanced at the mirror-stone staff in his hand. The blurry reflection of the man within — and in his eyes, the murky yellow light unique to a black dragonkin.
