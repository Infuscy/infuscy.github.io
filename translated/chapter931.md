## Chapter 931: Gradually Blending In

The next day, Jace had arranged to meet Grede and Sulenna at the Pig and Whistle Inn. He'd returned from Moonbrook, met the king, met the mages — and though all of them claimed to be his friends, Jace felt none of the ease he should have felt after seeing friends.

Erland, as one of the victims, had spent two months locked up in a prison cell on the false charge of summoning demons to plague Moonbrook; it was hard to say which of them had suffered more.

Probably only heading to a tavern with Grede Thunderfist and the others for a drink could properly vent the unpleasantness that had built up over this period.

In the evening Jace arrived at the Pig and Whistle. The inn was packed at this hour; he scanned back and forth several times before noticing the blond woman waving at him from the corner. Sulenna, normally so conspicuous, was now swallowed up by the surging crowd.

"Since you have the ability to detect demons," Grede said, watching Jace weave through the crowd and settle into the seat in the corner, "next time try learning to detect dragons — that way you could spot Sulenna at a glance in a crowd."

"If there were really such a magic, it wouldn't be this much trouble," Jace said, raising an arm to point north and west. "I'd just run to the kings of every major kingdom, scan them with one glance to see which one the black dragon has become a son-in-law to, then collect a few gold coins as an appraisal fee. Wouldn't that be a lot easier than running myself half to death like now?"

Grede burst out laughing. He looked over at Sulenna, who was also smiling, and said, "See that? This is the ambition of a human-kingdom court mage — even if he drags a black dragon into the open, he'd only dare ask for a few gold coins as a reward."

"Still better than a gryphon rider — no, a 'former' gryphon rider — who nearly got slaughtered by an orc shaman for a dozen silvers," Jace said, propping his cheek on his hand.

Grede coughed and looked at him. "It's as if you didn't come with me. You're buying this meal — I'm not paying a copper, damn it."

"You're only allowed to talk about me, but I'm not allowed to talk about you?" Jace said, reaching over to touch the cloth bag that clearly held a small box. "I even prepared a gift for you. But looking at you now, no point taking it out — I'll take it to Booty Bay tomorrow to sell for cash to cover this meal."

Hearing that, Grede couldn't help but lick his lips and grin. "What is it? I apologize, Master Sesso — it was my fault I dragged you into trouble with that orc. My bad, all right? Just let me take a look..."

Jace shot him an unwilling glance, took the box out of the bag and set it on the table, glanced around to make sure everyone else was busy boasting, drinking, and carrying on — that none of the three people in the corner were being watched — then slowly opened it a crack and immediately shut it again.

But that single crack was enough to drain what little color remained from Sulenna's already pale face. "A black dragon's artifact," she whispered. "Is this related to the lighthouse incident, Jace?"

"You know about it?" Jace asked the green dragon.

"What incident?" Grede looked at Sulenna, then at Jace, baffled. "Have you two met before? How come I'm the only one who doesn't know anything — what happened?"

"Master Marin told me," Sulenna said. "He said you killed quite a few black dragon cultists at the lighthouse, along with some warlocks, and most importantly, a dragonkin blessed by a black dragon. Based on his description, I'm guessing that was a drake, Jace?"

"A drake — is it that huge monster we saw in Grim Batol?" Grede asked. "Yes," Jace said. "A black dragon's kind, though."

"You single-handedly killed one of those... things?" Grede asked, shocked.

"Along with three dragonkin, though Morophil helped too, so it doesn't count as doing it alone," Jace said.

"Good thing the demon lady was with you," Sulenna said. "Drakes are generally very powerful. They're not as clever as dragonkin, but they're far more loyal and reliable, which is precisely why we favor them more. We're also more willing to grant them as much power as possible..."

"Shh..." Jace quickly scanned the vicinity and lowered his voice. "First it's 'demon lady,' then it's 'we'... Watch yourself — this isn't Ashenvale."

"Ah, sorry." Sulenna hunched her shoulders and whispered, "Being around you two, I forget all the rules of living in Stormwind."

"Stop dwelling on these trivialities, Jace," Grede shook his head. "If you run into something like this next time, remember to call for us — at least call Sulenna. She knows dragons well, doesn't she? Don't take such risks alone. I get nervous just listening."

"Only warlocks could approach that lighthouse. They were very wary of outsiders. I'm a warlock myself, aren't I? That's how I got close to that drake," Jace said. "If I'd called you two over, you wouldn't have been able to mix in with them alongside me."

"What's so hard about disguising yourself as a warlock?" Grede mimicked the hand gesture a mage uses to cast Arcane Missiles, twirled it, and pointed at Jace with crisp enunciation: "Frakkt!"

Jace stared at the dwarf's performance with the look of someone watching an idiot. A long time ago he'd worried that Grede would be tempted by the power of shadow magic and go down that path, but looking at it now, there was no need to worry at all — shadow held not even a scrap of interest for a Wildhammer dwarf like him.

After observing Jace's reaction, Grede leaned back in his chair helplessly and said, "Fine, it is difficult. But we could wait on the shore — the moment something feels off about that lighthouse, Sulenna can turn into a dragon and fly me up to the lighthouse to help you. Think about it — a heroic Wildhammer green dragon knight descending upon a lighthouse seized by evil forces to rescue a trapped warrior. By the..."

By this point Sulenna's face had already darkened. Grede awkwardly pulled back his gesture and nodded. "A beautiful lady of the forest and her clumsy, flightless dwarf attendant."

Only then did Sulenna nod in satisfaction.

"All right, all right, I get it, both of you. This time it was my fault — it was truly dangerous, and Morophil was badly injured too," Jace said. "Next time I definitely won't go off on my own."

"You've said that before too," the dwarf sighed, then suddenly remembered something. "Right, what exactly was that thing — the black dragon's artifact?"

"That's a life gem, Grede," Sulenna explained in a low voice. "Only the most powerful dragon nest guardians can possess something like that. It's a gem that can strengthen flesh and restore power. I'm guessing it was a treasure that drake was wearing?"

"Right," Jace said. "The mages of the Wizard Sanctum found it on his corpse, and they've already done some basic tests that proved it works on humans too. I figure if it works on humans, it should work on dwarves as well."

"The Black Father's necklace worked on me, Jace," Grede said, staring at the box. "Then a black dragon's gem shouldn't be a problem either?"

Heh heh. He chuckled twice, wiped his hands, and pulled the box over to hug it to his chest. "But it would be too embarrassing to accept something this valuable from you, Jace — after all, I didn't help you out at all..."

Jace said, "In a couple days I'm planning to go find someone from the Stonemasons guild about starting renovation work on Zul'Mashar Tower — still short several dozen gold..."

"Cough, cough!"

At that, Grede couldn't help but break into a violent coughing fit, bracing himself on the table as if he were about to cough his lungs out. Jace frowned and said, "Take it, take it — I won't take your money, damn it."

"No, no, no!" Grede quickly looked up. "I probably just choked on my own saliva. You ask the Stonemasons first — if the money really isn't enough, I'll chip in too, Jace, no problem."

Jace tapped his fingers on the table and eyed him. If he believed that, then he'd believe anything.

The food was more or less all served. The three of them chatted a bit about how things were going at Moonbrook. When Jace got to how he'd used Utharius directly to cut the tall dragon lord Raschrel clean in half, both Sulenna and Grede's eyes went wide.

"Next time you spar with me, you stay far away from that thing," Grede said, eyeing the bulky cloth bundle resting on the adjacent bench.

"I'm not insane," Jace said.

"Best stay far away from Skysong too," the dwarf added. "Sure, sure..." Jace said.

As for what happened next — how the cultists had entered the room and how Jace dealt with them in detail — he didn't bring it up, and neither did the other two.

But Jace could tell they weren't incurious. They just tacitly didn't mention it — at least Grede could surely tell that Jace didn't really want to talk about those things.

Jace was grateful for their understanding too. He didn't want to talk about what had happened later that night.

After that, Jace chatted with Sulenna about the dragon matters she'd discussed with Marin. Later, when they got to upcoming plans, Jace said to her, "I should have nothing much to keep me busy here in Stormwind after this, Sulenna. Once we've saved up enough to start renovating Zul'Mashar, I can take you over to have a look around the swamp then."

"You mean Atal'Hakkar?" Sulenna asked.

"Yes," Jace said. "Didn't you always want to know how things were over there?"

Sulenna hesitated, mulling it over for a moment, then said softly, "All right. But when the time actually comes to go see, I feel like I'm not quite ready to face Lord Eranikus. After all, if he knew I'd defied Queen Ysera's orders and interfered in Emerald Dream affairs, he'd probably be angry with me too."

Jace thought to himself that Eranikus definitely wasn't what she imagined him to be — though of course he couldn't just come out and tell her everything directly.

"Let me know when you've thought it through, Sulenna."

"I'll go too," Grede raised his hand.

"Have you found a weapon to replace your warhammer?" Jace asked. "Not yet — I'm looking," Grede said.

"You see?" Jace looked at Sulenna. "You're not the only one who isn't prepared." The three of them talked late into the night, not parting until there were hardly any people left at the Pig and Whistle.

Grede had drunk a lot and was stumbling drunk, his walk unsteady. Jace and Sulenna escorted him home together and came back downstairs only after making sure he was asleep.

Sulenna had also had quite a bit to drink, but being a dragon, she showed almost no reaction at all, like a normal person.

Jace hadn't drunk that much either, though considering what he'd just been through, Grede had noticed and didn't force him to keep pace.

Gazing into the dark depths of the Dwarf District's alleys, the distant spire of the Cathedral of Light still lit up in the distance, Sulenna said, "Now I'm starting to understand why so many dragons enjoy disguising themselves as humans and living in human cities, Jace. I think I'm slowly growing fond of these walls too."

Jace nodded — and wasn't it the same for him?
