Chapter 932: The Night Watch

A day later, Jace linked up with Erland in Goldshire and set out together in his carriage for Duskwood.

Erland had spent the last couple days tidying himself up, shaved his beard, and finally looked something like his old self — though he'd genuinely lost a lot of weight compared to before. The hulking Stonemasons-enforcer look he'd had when he first showed up at the Slaughtered Lamb, the one that made people nervous, had faded quite a bit.

It was the middle of the afternoon, and the streets seemed even more crowded than usual. As they passed the tavern entrance, Jace noticed a decently dressed man — looked like a noble or a merchant — standing on a cargo crate nearby, shouting something at the top of his lungs.

"In the past, because of the orc invasion, because of the Dark Portal, Grandshire was the front line defending Stormwind. Whether it was King Llane or his son, our beloved King Wrynn, they had to find a way to protect us. Not because our safety was threatened — but because those nobles, and they themselves, were threatened. Orcs aren't one-armed skeletons — they could kill their way from here to the palace in less than a week! But now, they clearly feel... those ghosts that stalk the night, those ghouls crawling out of their graves — they probably can't cause much trouble. And the rest of us? Our lives or deaths are the last thing they care about!"

The man vented his grievances at full volume, and from the side came a different voice.

"Haven't you heard about the demons at Moonbrook? Once the warlocks there are cleaned out and there really are no more demons... the soldiers will come back."

"Cleaned out," Erland muttered under his breath. "Listen to that, Jace — listen to what they're calling us. Talking about it like sweeping cockroaches out of a kitchen."

Jace smiled. "He's not talking about warlocks like us."

"You think the people here can tell the difference between warlocks like us and warlocks like 'them'?" Erland said, glancing sideways at Jace. "Do you think there might come a day when succubi and imps can walk down the streets of Stormwind, and the passersby would treat them the way they treat half-elves right now — just disdain or disgust — instead of panicking and running off to the Stormwind stockade or the church to summon guards and priests?"

Jace said nothing. He honestly didn't know how to answer that.

Then, not far off, a middle-aged man suddenly roared at the crowd ahead: "You want those soldiers back? Who do you think they are — the Silver Hand? Haven't the soldiers already done enough damage to our town? I've had it up to here!"

"As long as they can guarantee our safety, the rest isn't that important. But the problem right now is they won't even come. Tell the mayor, tell the Eberlocs — if they're not willing to send soldiers to protect us, then we'll form our own."

"You're insane! Keep spouting this madness and you won't have long before they arrest you. One of these nights you might find the king's spies sleeping under your bed!"

"Then what are we supposed to do? Face the undead that could show up in town at any moment, face the cultists learning dark magic — just sit here and wait to die? If it takes a few soldiers coming to town in person to arrest me, then I'll count that as my contribution to defending Grandshire!"

"Why would they arrest him? What are they afraid of? We respect the Eberlocs, we're loyal to the Wrynn, loyal to the kingdom — but only if we're alive to talk about it, don't you think? When those fanged ghouls show up at my door, nobody but the Holy Light is going to help me!"

"Chances are even the Holy Light won't help you by then, Josef — after all, you haven't donated to the church in a long time."

A few scattered laughs rippled through the crowd, but far more was silence — and in that silence hung a current of unease and tension that Jace could feel.

The man up high raised his hand. "If the people elsewhere in the kingdom are afraid of the eternal night here, then we'll guard this night ourselves, everyone. After all, I believe everyone still willing to stay in this forest has their own reason for not being able to leave!"

The ones keeping order here weren't Stormwind soldiers — they weren't even Duskwood town-hall guards. By their dress, they were just rangers from around town.

Considering that the dangers in Duskwood were only marginally better than the situation at Moonbrook before, it was no surprise the townspeople were unhappy.

In the game, the ones guarding Duskwood weren't Stormwind Kingdom soldiers either — it was an organization called the Night Watch, formed spontaneously by the townspeople after Stormwind's army pulled out.

Judging by this, the seeds of it were already being sown here and now.

The carriage arrived at the Dagger Society's shelter. The two men climbed down, and the house seemed to have noticed the visitors outside — the door opened right up.

"You actually got out!" The one who came out was Sulenna Kayldon. She saw Erland and covered her mouth in surprise, then caught sight of Jace and her eyes went wide.

"Jace..." she murmured the name. "You must be the one who figured out how to fish this idiot out of prison, right?"

"By rights I never should have been locked up in the first place!" Erland said. "The Holy Light can witness my loyalty to King Wrynn and my respect for the Wizard Sanctum — and, fine, Jace helped out a little."

"Pfft." Sulenna spat on the ground, laughed, and threw her arms tightly around Erland. "Come on, get inside."

Once they were in, Morophil materialized off to the side. Gakres, who'd been leaning against the wall, straightened up, bowed deeply, and said "Welcome" in Eredun.

Sulenna said, "It's been a long time, Miss Morophil. A powerful succubus is always a sight for sore eyes. I've seen far too many weak succubi pulled God-knows-where lately — makes you wonder whether they've even managed to figure out the incantations Jace worked so hard to sort out..."

"Your mouth is really sweet, Sulenna," Morophil said, grinning happily.

Jace cleared his throat and looked at Sulenna. "Best not flatter her too much. Right — has Kayleyn finished my robes?"

Morophil pursed her lips and shot her master a sidelong look. Sulenna said, "Of course. She left your robes with Ursula — you can go take a look. She's been experimenting with that strange furbolg-tribe formula you brought back from Kalimdor these past few days, Jace — filled the whole basement with a weird medicinal stink."

"Is she sleeping right now?" Jace asked.

"Probably," Sulenna said. "I heard from Ursula that you were planning to go check out Moonbrook. Did you go? How did it turn out?"

Erland laughed. "Once you bring in Jace Sesso, what chance do the demons there have? I'd say the Moonbrook business is pretty much wrapped up. The mages who were guarding me are already planning reunions with the classmates and colleagues who came back from Moonbrook."

"Duskwood hasn't heard a whisper of it yet..." Sulenna glanced out the window. "Looks like those people were right — the king really does seem to have written this place off."

"If that's how it is, it seems we could turn this into a permanent hideout, couldn't we? I certainly don't want to see any more mages." Erland looked between Sulenna and Jace. "What does Galders think?"

"Galders won't agree," Sulenna said. "We can't be too high-profile in Duskwood. There's another group of warlocks here too — did you forget? Maybe they're keeping a low profile because of the Moonbrook business, maybe they haven't noticed us yet... peaceful coexistence now doesn't mean we'll stay on good terms forever."

"Maybe they all died in the lighthouse," Erland said, glancing at Jace. "What lighthouse?" Sulenna asked.

Erland smacked Sulenna on the rear. "If you don't know, then I've got a story for you — I'll tell you slowly tonight."
