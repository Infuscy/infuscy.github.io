## Chapter 933: Better Robes, Stronger Potions

Heading toward the alchemy lab, the closer they got, the more a bitter smell hit them.

Pushing open the door, Ursula was asleep in her chair leaning against the wall, a bubbling cauldron still gurgling away beside her. Ursula's imp stood to the side, watching Jace and whispering, "Shh — don't wake my mistress."

"Who is it?" Ursula opened her eyes and looked over. The imp immediately said, "Sorry, I woke my mistress up."

"Ah, Jace, you're back." The alchemist sat up. "Did you come back from Moonbrook?"

Jace said, "The Moonbrook business is more or less handled. I think after some time, once things calm down, we can move back to Stormwind. SI:7 might reach out to us again — at least that's what the king's side told me."

"Finally, some good news," Ursula said. "Getting all kinds of herbs here, and other ingredients, is genuinely too difficult — and there aren't many people we can trust either... Right, was the Moonbrook business really connected to the black dragon?"

"Not just connected — the black dragon was running the show," Jace said. "It makes me suspect there might be black dragons inside Stormwind too. I've already told Erland that when we work with SI:7 again, we need to pay attention to this. You'll need to be careful too."

Ursula said, "When the time comes I'll summon another felhound to help us guard the basement. Just don't know if we'll get one as clever as Mister Basadim. He couldn't talk, but he could accurately understand every instruction I gave him, no matter how complex... as long as it was in Eredun."

At that, Jace thought back to the guy at the lighthouse who'd summoned his felguard on the spot inside the cave — that had been pretty intimidating for a moment.

"Do you know a warlock named Zubel?" Jace asked.

Ursula thought for a moment. "Zubel... I think I've heard the name. It's quite distinctive. He's a human too, isn't he?"

"Where did you hear it?" Jace asked.

"Probably Galders mentioned it. This warlock seems to have some reputation among the warlock circles in Ironforge. Did you run into him at Moonbrook?" Ursula said.

Jace said, "Strictly speaking, not Moonbrook — the lighthouse. After the Moonbrook mines were sealed off, those people fled to the lighthouse to hide, using the sea to block anyone searching from the shore."

"The lighthouse?" Ursula frowned. "Yesterday when I went out, I heard at the tavern that something major happened at the western-coast lighthouse — a mage killed a lot of people there, including some warlocks. That lighthouse?"

Before the others could even answer, her eyes suddenly went wide. "That mage wouldn't happen to be you, Jace — seeing as you're sort of with the Wizard Sanctum..."

"It was me," Jace said. "I didn't expect that many people to die either. I underestimated the black dragon's mental magic."

Ursula looked at Jace's eyes and said, "All I can tell you, Jace, is that everyone I know who learned about this is grateful for what that 'mage' did. Whether it's the Duskwood townspeople, the herbalists, or the warlocks who felt something off about that 'guide.'"

"I think this is exactly what Galders mentioned before — using shadow magic to fight evil, not some dark magic running out of control. Whatever the kings and nobles worry about, it shouldn't be this."

"Let's hope so," Jace said with a small smile.

"It is so," Ursula raised an eyebrow. "What about that warlock?"

Jace frowned. "At the time he demonstrated a summoning incantation — a spell to summon a specific demon. He could summon a single felguard he'd contracted with straight from the Twisting Nether, or wherever, right in front of him. Even the warlocks there at the lighthouse were pretty shocked — and mind you, the warlocks that guide had screened to be allowed near him were already the more outstanding ones in the warlock community."

Jace recalled the cultists who'd appeared from the half-collapsed doorway of the stone hall after he'd defeated Raschrel, along with the warlocks enslaved by black dragon blood.

He didn't remember a Zubel among them.

Jace thought it over. "I should ask around the Wizard Sanctum when I get the chance — he was probably captured by the mages. If I could learn this summoning ritual from him..."

"Might I be able to summon Basadim back again?" Ursula asked. "Very likely," Jace said.

Honestly, Jace cared more about his own demons. Sazeno was the weakest, but Jace wasn't too worried about that guy dying — he didn't draw much attention and had all kinds of ways to run. The one he cared about most was Morophil.

That day at the lighthouse fighting Raschrel had been dangerous enough. That drake champion was far more powerful than an ordinary wyrmling, and backed by the life gem to the last, he'd nearly killed Morophil.

By now Jace felt he couldn't start over building trust with a new succubus — and once Morophil was killed, even if she was resummoned from the Twisting Nether, there was no telling how long it would take or how much power she'd lose.

Judging by the pained, lost look on Zubel's face when his felguard was killed, a demon dying in Azeroth and returning to the Twisting Nether was definitely not the cost-free, casually dismissible affair it was in the game.

But regardless, mastering a fixed summoning technique was far better than knowing nothing.

Ursula smacked her forehead. "Ah, almost forgot — your robes are still with me. Back at the lighthouse, facing those black dragons and warlocks, without these robes protecting you... you didn't get hurt, did you? If something went wrong, I've got plenty of restorative and healing potions of all kinds here — though since I know about it, it's probably been many days already, so you must have found your own way by now?"

"Actually, I found a way right then and there," Jace took a deep breath and said. "And it wasn't a real black dragon — just dragonkin. Not that hard to deal with. Those ones probably wouldn't dirty their hands showing up personally for work like this."

"Who knows what your 'not that hard to deal with' even means, Jace Sesso," Ursula said, handing Jace the box that had been storing the void robes as she spoke.

"Isn't Kayleyn here?" Jace asked.

Ursula said, "She went back to Alterac to visit her family — left the day before yesterday — but I suspect she really wants to swing by Dalaran while she's at it. She told me she's planning to research a new set of robes. In her own words, while basing the design on orcish patterns, she also wants to account for resistance against multiple types of magical attacks. To dig into that properly, she has to go to Dalaran to see."

"New robes?" Jace said. "If there's anything I can help with, tell her to come find me." Ursula nodded. "Of course we'll need your help — and these new robes will probably need you to test-wear them."

"Sounds weird," Jace said. "The way you say it, it doesn't feel like I'm test-wearing clothes — it sounds like I'm a test subject for potions."

"After all, Kayleyn said only by having you wear them can the robes' true quality be tested..." Ursula said with a smile.

"Speaking of potions — what about that furbolg formula, Ursula? Sulenna said you've been experimenting with that potion these past few days," Jace said.

"Ah, yes — this is the most important part." Ursula pulled out her record book and flipped through several pages. "I ran tests using a small portion of Black Lotus I'd set aside, recreating it almost exactly by the formula's instructions. For that so-called demon-blood-fed herb, I used a kind of fel-blood leaf herb the goblins provided — they told me Kalimdor furbolgs gather that herb, so I figured it couldn't be wrong."

"Fel-blood leaf?" Jace asked curiously. "Does it grow in Kalimdor?"

Ursula said, "It's in the Eastern Kingdoms too — and according to some demon hunters, that blood weed already exists around Nethergarde Keep. I'm guessing the rumor about the herb being fed on demon blood isn't pure fabrication. To recreate that potion, I even tracked down some of the supplementary ingredients I didn't think were necessary through the goblins... I won't even get into the difficulties I went through these past few days."

"But something in the process isn't right — the potion we made had no effect on us at all, and was even somewhat toxic. It irritates the stomach severely, causes terrible diarrhea and vomiting, plus heart flushes, flushed face... though this isn't exactly unpredictable, given it's got things raised on demon blood in it. It's just that I don't know any furbolgs — couldn't find a furbolg to test it on to see if I've perfectly recreated it... if only you'd brought me a sample bottle back from Kalimdor."

Jace said, "At the time, that furbolg herbalist didn't seem to have any ready-made either. I'm guessing Black Lotus isn't exactly cheap for them either."

He thought back to the Flask of the Titans recipe from the game — he had a feeling that besides Black Lotus, Gromsblood, and the crystal vial, there was one more required ingredient. But if someone mentioned it he might still be able to remember; trying to accurately recall the details of a potion formula from a game he'd played over twenty years ago was really asking too much of him.

Ursula thought for a moment. "Or maybe the issue is with those supplementary ingredients — among the things the furbolgs add for taste and flavor, there might be some our bodies can't digest. Or it could be the brewing environment. This alchemy lab is just too rudimentary. When I get back to the Slaughtered Lamb, I'm going to renovate it to the standard of the kings' or Dalaran's facilities... no idea how much that'll cost. But how much better could a furbolg's brewing environment be than mine? You've been to that furbolg herbalist's place — could you tell if there was anything special about it?"

Jace shook his head. "Even if there's some special natural magic blessing where the furbolg tribe lives, I wouldn't be able to sense it."

Ursula frowned and stared at the formula, then after a while asked, "Could there have been a translation error?"

"I promise you, you don't need to doubt that step, Ursula," Jace tried asking, "How many kinds of potions that boost defense and stamina do you know?"

"Too many," Ursula counted on her fingers. "Elixir of Defense, Elixir of Fortitude, Flask of Stone, Stoneshield Potion..."

As she went on, the expression on her face shifted bit by bit, then she frowned. "Yes, I know what you're getting at, Jace. As a potion that strengthens stamina and boosts power, it seems to be missing a stabiliser — and that's without mentioning that things raised on demon blood need even more stabilising and neutralising. That substance is Stonescale Eel oil! I should have thought of it earlier — damn it, this isn't some hard-to-spot problem! Maybe I was being too respectful of the formula as it was and didn't account for the differences between furbolgs and us — their stomachs are far tougher than ours. I'll start revising this formula right away and begin a new attempt tomorrow."

Stonescale fish oil — Jace remembered it too.

That was right. In the game, many high-level protective potions that called for Black Lotus also called for this fish oil. Of course, the game never explained what exactly it did in the potion — it was just one line in the materials list.

Ursula picked up a quill and scribbled a long list on the paper beside her, then looked up and asked, "Will you be staying in Duskwood these days, Jace?"

Jace glanced at what she'd written. "No — next I'm heading to Zul'Mashar Tower. Erland set up a meeting with the head of the Stonemasons guild to discuss renovating that tower."

"Then if I have results, can I come find you at Zul'Mashar Tower?" Ursula asked.

"Or send a letter. There are gnolls on the road halfway here — still pretty dangerous," Jace said.

"Understood." Ursula looked at the long string of notes she'd just written. "Wait for my good news."
