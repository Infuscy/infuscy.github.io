## Chapter 946: The Opposite Direction

The next afternoon, Jace called Grede and Sola to the Leskowa family house he'd bought near the Church District, intending to discuss Onyxia with them.

Since the renovations had been completed, he hadn't really had a chance to come inside and look around carefully. After returning from Kalimdor, he'd been immediately tangled up in all sorts of dragon- and demon-related business, and during the time he'd rested at Ilgalar Tower, he couldn't be bothered to fly back and forth between Stormwind and Redridge.

For one thing, Katrana's name had already spread across all of Stormwind, and discussing related topics in any public place drew too much attention. For another, Jace also wanted to take the opportunity to see what the house looked like after the renovations.

Of course, the renovation work on this house wasn't a remodel or interior decoration, but rather maintenance focused on the building's security, so from the inside it didn't look much different.

But quite a few places, especially the roof, did look noticeably new, and those dwarves seemed to have gone out of their way to clean — the whole house looked much brighter than the last time Jace had left it. These few gold pieces were well spent.

While he was on the staircase inspecting the sturdiness of the railing and the stairs themselves, Sola arrived first.

The moment she stepped in, she curiously surveyed the interior of the house. Jace couldn't help but ask, "Can this place be blessed?"

Sola gave him an exasperated look. "At this rate, in a few decades every house in Stormwind will have been blessed by me, Jace."

Jace laughed. "Promise this is the last one."

"If we're in the city, it might draw unwanted attention from the Wizard Sanctum's mages," Sola said.

Three or four violent knocks interrupted their casual chat. Jace opened the door and looked at Grede. "Can you knock any gentler? What if you break the door?"

"All right, all right, but for such an expensive house, you couldn't even spare some decent wood?" Grede grumbled as he came in, glancing at the two of them. "Have you met that Alterac princess?"

Sola nodded. Jace said, "That's exactly what I wanted to talk to you two about."

Seeing Jace's expression, Grede pulled over a chair and sat down. "What's wrong with her?" Jace looked at Sola. "Did you notice anything strange about her?"

Sola thought for a moment. "She seems more mature than she appears?"

"Much more mature," Jace said. "Let me be direct — I suspect she might be a black dragon in disguise."

Sola's eyes widened slightly. Grede asked, "Why are you so paranoid? You think because her father's a black dragon, she has to be one too?"

"I'm not making baseless accusations, Grede," Jace explained. "Let me put it this way — everyone, including you, including Sola, channels shadow power when their emotions fluctuate. That's why many warlocks can sense other people's emotions, even detect their lies. But that Lady Katrana Prestor shows absolutely none of it."

"Maybe she just has a good temper," Grede said.

"I thought so too at first, but yesterday I deliberately provoked her, and she did get angry — yet her inner self was calm, not like a real person at all," Jace said, staring at the dwarf seriously. "You call me paranoid, I admit it, but you have to acknowledge my sensitivity to shadow, Grede."

"So she got angry without any shadow. What does that mean?" Grede asked.

Sola said, "It means she's completely shielded her shadow power through some means — perhaps an object, perhaps... some more powerful magic."

Grede was stunned. He looked at Jace, then at Sola. "Then she's fooled the entire city."

"At least not the three of us," Jace said.

And Mathias Shur, he thought to himself.

"Then how did you determine she's a black dragon in disguise, and not some other ill-intentioned witch?" Grede asked.

Jace said, "And she wants me to find her father, dead or alive, and she may be deliberately steering me toward Blackrock Spire... You and I both know what's atop Blackrock Spire right now."

"Blackrock Spire? Isn't that a suicide mission?" Grede said, gripping his chair. "Did you agree to her?"

"I agreed to scout first and make a plan," Jace said. "My idea is to play along for now. I reckon she sees me as quite a thorn in her side and wants to find a proper excuse to make me disappear without the king or anyone else looking into why I vanished."

"If you really plan to go to Blackrock Spire, don't leave me behind, Jace," Grede said. "If she wants you to disappear, it probably won't be that simple."

"Don't worry, I never intended to go up Blackrock Spire," Jace said. "But rather... below it. Shadowforge City."

"What, you're going to find the Dark Iron dwarves?" Grede asked. "What do they have to do with it?"

Jace explained, "The Dark Iron dwarves, in order to deal with the black dragons occupying their Blackrock Spire... have been searching for ways to disrupt black dragon magic. I heard they may have obtained a spell that can pierce dragon disguise magic."

"There's such a thing?" Sola asked, surprised. "Do all the Dark Iron dwarves know it?"

Jace said, "Of course not, otherwise it wouldn't be a secret weapon. Even the member of the Thorinsilver Brotherhood who told me this wasn't entirely clear on what it actually was. I want to go take a look. That way, I'll at least have one more card in hand."

"You're going alone?" the dwarf asked.

"I can only go alone," Jace said. "I'm a warlock. Dark Iron warlocks would be quite happy to exchange knowledge with an outside warlock. Besides, I have a cloak that can turn me into an orc — enough to get me through the orc blockade outside Blackrock Mountain."

"Could ask the red dragons to get me one too... oh right, that orc-transforming orb, Jace," Grede said.

Jace asked, "Then what about inside Shadowforge City? You're a Wildhammer dwarf, Grede. You appearing in Shadowforge City would be more dangerous than an elf showing up in the Dwarf District."

"I..." Grede rubbed his beard, suddenly at a loss for words. After a long while, he finally said, "Then wouldn't Windrunner be of no help either? Not only is she not a Dark Iron dwarf, she's an elf. She can't even turn into an orc."

"So it's not that I don't want your help this time — it's truly a job for me alone," Jace said. "But if I go to Shadowforge City, someone has to keep watch over Stormwind. Grede, you and Sola observe that princess's movements here. It'd be even better if Windrunner came — I've already written to her. Then I'll go find Mathias Shur and see if he and my warlock friends can help in any other way, and look for other possible methods to expose her..."

"I can turn into an orc, and into a Dark Iron dwarf too, Jace," Sola suddenly said.

"What?" Jace was startled by her words.

"I know there are orc and Dark Iron dwarf prisoners in Stormwind's jails, both male and female. I can reference their appearances to shift my form. That's no problem for me," Sola said.

"I'm grateful you want to help, but you don't need to take this risk with me, Sola," Jace said.

Sola said, "I know you don't trust the stability of my transformations, but since Azuregos pointed out my problems, I've been practicing my shapeshifting magic this whole time. In the past, I only transformed in the Emerald Dream for fun, never seriously considering I might be exposed, which is why it was so sloppy."

"But you don't speak Dwarvish," Jace said.

"Time in the Emerald Dream may not be entirely stable," Sola said in Dwarvish, "but it's certainly long enough."

Jace choked awkwardly, and Grede was dumbfounded.

Both of them looked at this green dragon simultaneously, feeling that her Dwarvish was even more standard than their own.

"Then how about this," Jace said, picking up the Mirrorstone Staff from the wall. "First let me check whether you're a human inside this thing."

"All right," Sola said confidently.

Jace leaned close to the stone and glanced inside, seeing the blonde woman not far away. The only thing off about her was a faint moonlight-like silver glow in her eyes. It really wasn't that weird, upright lizard from before.

"How is it?" she asked.

"Looks good!" Grede said immediately. "What else do you have to say now? Let Sola help you, Jace. After all, even Molphris had problems at that lighthouse; when the moment of truth comes, are you really going to rely on that little imp to save your life?"

Jace nodded and said, "I'll go talk to the people managing the prison, see if I can meet with those prisoners."
