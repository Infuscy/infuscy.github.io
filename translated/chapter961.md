## Chapter 961: The Black Iron Bar

Following the gnome through a side passage into a doorway, Jace climbed a dark staircase all the way up. In the narrow space, the dwarves passing by stared unblinkingly at him and the succubus beside him.

The stairwell was quiet — not much chatter or clamor from the dwarves — a stark contrast to the rumbling magma outside. Combined with those wary gazes, the atmosphere had grown decidedly tense.

The dwarven citizens here were dressed far more shabbily than the dwarves Jace had seen on the streets earlier. Many were caked in thick dust, their beards smeared with grime, and the whole space reeked of a dry stench mixing burnt smells with sweat.

"Does the Black Iron Bar have rooms?" Jace asked quietly.

The gnome explained, "Of course. The Black Iron Bar is the largest bar in the Three Hammers, and of course it can also—"

"Speak Dwarvish, you white-skinned runts with your damn bird-talk!" a red-bearded dwarf suddenly bellowed from the side, startling the gnome.

"What's that gnome saying? Plotting to blow up our city?" a female dwarf called from below. "And that human too!"

"He's a warlock…"

"Look at that demon… her tattoos are fucking gorgeous."

"I didn't even notice — where are her wings?"

By now, whispers were spreading through the crowd.

"Everyone," Sola said, trying to sound as serious as she could, "I'll remind my friend — he just isn't very fluent in Dwarvish yet."

Hearing Sola's words, the irritable dwarves seemed to deflate all at once. After a moment's hesitation, they stopped pestering and went on their way.

Jace noticed something strange. Recalling how the Dark Iron dwarves he'd encountered earlier had been quite respectful toward Sola — calling her "madam" and speaking courteously, even the less-educated soldiers occasionally using honorifics —

Actually, he'd had a nagging worry: that the female prisoner Sola had met in the prison might not be ordinary. But that shouldn't be possible — Sola had said she'd made some modifications to avoid being recognized…

After pondering this for a while, they exited the stairwell and emerged onto a lively, market-like terrace.

Quite a few Dark Iron dwarves had gathered here, chatting and selling small wares. The dwellings and windows carved into the higher cliff walls on both sides were as numerous and bright as stars in a night sky.

"Right here," the gnome said, pointing to a stone doorway surrounded by dwarves on the upper steps. "That's the Black Iron Bar. Doesn't look like much, does it? But that's really it."

"Thank you very much, sir." Jace pulled out five silver coins and handed them over. "I'm still curious about what we discussed earlier. If you hear anything about a way to counter black dragon magic, come find me at the Black Iron Bar. There'll be additional thanks."

"Ah…" The gnome pocketed the coins. "Thank you very much. I'll keep an eye out for you. Though I don't know your name yet…"

The name Jurk was on the tip of Jace's tongue, but he suddenly remembered that was the Dwarvish name Grede had given him — probably not convenient in a dwarven stronghold like Shadowforge City. So he said, "Slovik."

"Slovik — sounds like a Gnomish name, sir." The gnome smiled. "I'll ask around for you. Hope to see you again!"

Arriving at the tavern entrance, Jace still had some doubts and turned to Sola beside him. "Did you notice how the dwarves here treat you?"

"I noticed," Sola frowned. "What's wrong?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "Let's keep a low profile and not do anything unnecessary for now."

"All right," Sola nodded.

Under the prying eyes of the dwarves, they entered through the stone doorway. A rich aroma of ale hit them first, followed by roaring laughter mixed with coughing.

"The fuck… my wife used an axe that long, just like that! You know, just like that… my ass has been in two pieces ever since!"

"Hahaha! Gade, I tell you—"

"Yeah, thanks to your wife for teaching you how to shit, that's what!"

"Celebrate shitting!"

"Celebrate shitting!"

Listening to the clamor and cursing, Jace stared inside in a daze, feeling as though he'd stepped into another world entirely.

Of course, the whole tavern was black and gray — after all, it was still part of Shadowforge City, built from obsidian and Blackrock Mountain stone like everywhere else. But the atmosphere was noticeably more relaxed than outside. Even from the coal-black faces and blood-red eyes of the Dark Iron dwarves, one could sense a rare happiness — at least on the surface.

After all, if there was any place in Shadowforge City where one could forget the predicament of the orc blockade, aside from the arena, it was probably the Black Iron Bar.

"Hey, tall boy, you're quite cute. Come here, let me get a look at you."

A shirtless, red-bearded Dark Iron dwarf beside him raised a silver tankard of ale. "I don't know how long it's been since a good-looking young fellow like you graced the Black Iron Bar. Drop your trousers and let Uncle see how you've developed. Hah, don't worry — if you make me feel good, I'll make you feel good too. Let me tell you a trick: just grab hold of—"

Jace ignored him and kept walking. The Dark Iron dwarf kept rambling at the spot where Jace had been standing, until a moment later he froze and asked, "Where'd he go… where'd he go?"

"Your succubus is beautiful, human. Miss Namara's going to be jealous," a nearby Dark Iron dwarf muttered, eyeing Morophile.

"Where are your wings, beautiful lady? I've never seen a succubus without wings!"

"She might be an Eredar demon. A female Eredar is just a succubus without wings."

"You've ever seen an Eredar?"

"That's a stupid question — I've seen everything! I've seen a doomguard's rear end too. Remember the day Maigeloda got his eyes blackened? Hah!"

The dwarves burst into laughter again, some even tumbling under the tables. One Dark Iron dwarf slipped on spilled ale and toppled into a couple who were going at it under a nearby table, knocking them to the ground. The female dwarf kicked him hard, and with an "Ow!" he was sent sprawling, whimpering.

Jace glanced at Morophile — even she was at a complete loss for words.

Sola breathed uncomfortably and murmured, "Well, I wasn't quite prepared for this…"

"You think I was?" Jace said under his breath.

"Tidy up the tables and chairs, you brainless things!"

An angry woman's shout came from not far away. Immediately someone in the dwarf crowd yelled back, "My brain got sucked out by you last night, Miss Namara! Can you spit it back out so I can have a taste?"

"Miss Namara's spit belongs to Sergeant Kadru — don't even think about it…"

"That was last week. Maybe next month it'll belong to me!"

"Who's Sergeant Kadru again…"

"Isn't that Kadiru? The slaver?"

"That was last year's. Miss Namara lost interest in him ages ago!"

Amid the clamor, a graceful, pale purple succubus walked slowly through the crowd on cloven hooves — like a black cat emerging from a filthy garbage heap, yet immaculate and utterly at ease.

As she drew closer, she noticed Jace, and also caught sight of Morophile, but quickly looked back toward those babbling Dark Iron dwarves as if unconcerned.

But with that single glance, most of her ease had already dissipated.

Clearly she wasn't unconcerned. Even in a tavern muddled with shadow magic, Jace could clearly sense the panic and trepidation that surged within her the moment she saw Morophile.

She was merely holding her composure, hoping that this succubus before her was just an illusion conjured by some psychic magic.

"Miss Namara, right?" Jace stepped forward to greet her.

"Ah, I didn't notice such a handsome young man had come to the Black Iron Bar, and with such a…"

"As you see, my companion," Jace said.

"Hello, Miss Namara." Morophile smiled.

Namara pursed her lips uncomfortably. "Hello. Welcome to Shadowforge City, my lady. It's an honor to meet you."

"Though I trust you've already felt more than enough hospitality." She tried to brush off the unnatural emotion, smiling as she fanned her mane-covered pair of wings — but then seemed to realize something and nervously folded them behind her back.

"Well then, what can I get you, warlock?"

"I'm looking for a place to stay. Arrange two rooms for me and my friend."

"She'll be staying alone, I take it?" Namara looked at Sola and nodded. "Where are you from, Ironforge Peak or Ironforge?"

"None of your business, succubus," Sola said.

Namara raised her eyebrows in surprise and said quickly, "Ah, of course, no offense, honored lady."

"Have you two met before?" Jace asked, already calculating whether Sola should find time to change her disguise.

"No." Namara smiled. "A human might not be able to tell, but any Dark Iron dwarf knows — this lady's accent is unmistakable. The Thaurissan family and all the nobles who've served them since before the War of the Three Hammers have maintained the accent of the Anvilmar era, never changing with the passage of time."

"Am I right, my lady?" she asked, looking rather pleased with herself.

Sola didn't dare respond for a moment. The succubus smiled. "Please, follow me, honored guests."

Watching Namara's back, Jace moved close to Sola and asked quietly in Drakonic, "Where did you learn Dwarvish?"

"From the Emerald Dream, of course. That was a long time ago," Sola said.

"All right." Jace nodded.

Considering Sola's age and the malleable flow of time in the Emerald Dream, for all Jace knew this green dragon had learned Dwarvish before the War of the Three Hammers even happened.

"Oh, and remember — don't speak Drakonic around other dragons," Sola reminded him with a smile. Jace glanced at her. "I know, I know…"
